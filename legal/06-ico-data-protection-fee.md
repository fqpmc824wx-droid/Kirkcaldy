## Do we have to pay?

**Yes, almost certainly.** Any organisation that keeps personal data on computers (our Google Sheet, emails and texts) must pay a yearly fee to the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO), unless an exemption applies. None of the exemptions fit a dental practice that keeps a waiting list and will keep patient records.

The fee is paid by the **company that runs the practice** (the "controller"). Associates and staff do not pay their own.

**Important check first:** if the same company already runs another practice (for example Glenrothes) and is already registered with the ICO, **you do not pay again**. You just update that company's ICO entry (add the new address and the waiting-list activity). Each separate company pays its own fee.

## How much?

The fee depends on the company's size. Figures below are the latest we found (set in February 2025). **Please confirm on ico.org.uk before paying.**

| Tier | Who | Fee per year |
|---|---|---|
| Tier 1 (micro) | Turnover up to £632,000 **or** 10 staff or fewer | £52 (£47 by Direct Debit) |
| Tier 2 (small/medium) | Turnover up to £36 million **or** 250 staff or fewer | £78 (£73 by Direct Debit) |
| Tier 3 (large) | Bigger than Tier 2 | about £3,763 |

A new practice will most likely be **Tier 1**.

## How to pay

1. Go to **ico.org.uk/fee** (type it yourself; do not click links in emails – there are scam letters asking for this fee).
2. Use the ICO's self-assessment to confirm the tier.
3. Fill in the registration form and pay by card or Direct Debit.
4. Save the **ICO registration certificate** (it has a registration number) in the legal folder.
5. Put the ICO registration number in the website's privacy page.
6. Set a reminder to renew each year.

## What the owner needs to give (only for the form)

Only needed if the company is **not** already registered:
- the company's **full legal name**;
- the **company number** (from Companies House);
- the **registered office address**.

These same details must also appear on the website's privacy page, so people know who is responsible for their data.
