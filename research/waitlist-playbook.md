# Waiting list playbook: Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic

### From sign-up to first appointment attended

*8 November 2026. Written for the owner, the practice manager and the callers. This is operational guidance, not legal advice. Anything marked **Check** should be confirmed with NHS Fife or your compliance lead before you rely on it (see Section 9). Figures from spa, salon, gym and software vendors only show direction and are not proven. The NHS, legislation and peer-reviewed sources are stronger. Where a figure or timing is our own judgement, it says so.*

---

## The five principles behind this playbook

1. **Your problem is handling the volume, not finding demand.** A record US spa presale was "nearly 550 founding members" ([Hand & Stone](https://handandstone.com/franchise/blog/hand-stone-long-island-sunvet-opening/)). You expect 1,200–2,600+ sign-ups a month. A new NHS practice in Crewkerne got more than 4,500 expressions of interest in one day and had to pause its form ([NHS Somerset](https://nhssomerset.nhs.uk/news/opening-of-third-new-dental-practice-brings-nhs-care-to-thousands-more-people-in-somerset/)).
2. **Silence is what turns a list sour.** A wait feels longer when it is uncertain, unexplained or unfair, and people waiting feel "a fear of being forgotten" ([Maister](https://davidmaister.com/articles/1/52/index.html)). In healthcare, keeping people warm mostly means reliable admin: regular, honest updates and one clear contact route ([Healthwatch England](https://www.healthwatch.co.uk/news/2026-04-16/two-thirds-patients-and-carers-experience-nhs-admin-problems)).
3. **Speed matters twice: getting the first call made, and getting from sign-up to invitation.**
   - Across 200+ aesthetic practices, the median practice took 12h13m to send a first reply from a person. At that practice, 48.9% of leads got no message from a person within a week ([Aesthetix CRM](https://aesthetixcrm.com/speed-to-lead-aesthetic-practices/)).
   - For free software waitlists, an informal poll of founders found about 50% conversion when people were invited within a month, and under 20% after three months ([Lenny Rachitsky](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-waitlist-conversion)).
4. **Invite people privately, in fair stages. Never cause a public scramble.**
   - In King's Lynn, public daily quotas led to queues from about 4am ([LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/news/dentist-queues-kings-lynn-patient-appointments/)).
   - In Faversham, a "first 20" release brought in 27,000 calls ([KentOnline](https://www.kentonline.co.uk/faversham/news/huge-overnight-queues-and-27-000-calls-as-dentist-opens-up-n-291092/)).
   - In Bristol, police attended a reopening queue ([LBC](https://www.lbc.co.uk/news/nhs-dentist-stops-enrolling-patients-bristol-queue-disperses/)).
5. **Keep NHS registration and private treatment separate.** NHS registration must never cost anything, depend on private treatment, or be sped up by paying ([GDS Regs Sch 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1); [PCA(D)(2026)1](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2026-01.pdf)).

## 1. The journey

| # | Step | When | Who | Message | Sheet status afterwards |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Sign-up | Instant | Automation | SMS **S1** + email **E1** | NEW |
| 2 | First call | Same day, 8am–9pm. People who sign up after 9pm are called first thing next morning | Caller | Phone, then **S2** (after the call) or **S2b** (missed call) | WAITING – CONFIRMED or NOT REACHED |
| 3 | Kept warm | Service update every 3 weeks to everyone. Practice news at most every 7–10 days, only to people with marketing consent = yes | Practice manager writes; automation sends | **S3** / **S3b**; Stream B emails | No change |
| 4 | "Still want your place?" | After 6–8 weeks of waiting, then every 8–12 weeks until invited | Automation, then a caller | **S4** + 2 reminders 48h apart + a call | WAITING – CONFIRMED / NOT UNTIL [month] / DORMANT / LEFT |
| 5 | Invite | Weekly waves, sized to free diary slots 1–3 weeks ahead | Automation sends; callers chase | **S5** + **E5** (self-book link + phone number) | INVITED – W[n] |
| 6 | Booked | When they book | Caller, reception, or the patient self-booking | Read-back on the phone; **S6** confirmation with medical-history form and calendar link | BOOKED |
| 7 | Reminders | 3 days before (two-way) and 1 day before | Automation; callers phone anyone who hasn't confirmed | **S7**, **S8** | APPT CONFIRMED |
| 8 | First appointment | On the day | Reception (NHS forms signed in the practice); dentist (examination, treatment plan) | – | REGISTERED or DNA 1 |
| 9 | Missed it | Same day | Caller | **S10**, plus one more offer | REBOOKED or DNA 2 |

Three facts shape the whole journey:

- **A sign-up is not a registration.** Registration happens when the practice accepts the patient and a dentist examines them. The application can be made electronically ([Sch 1 para 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1/paragraph/5)). NHS inform says the practice "may put you on a waitlist to be registered until you've been seen for the enhanced exam" ([NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/care-support-and-rights/nhs-services/dental/receiving-nhs-dental-treatment-in-scotland/)).
- **Registering with you automatically ends the person's NHS registration elsewhere** ([Sch 1 para 9](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1/paragraph/9); [Citizens Advice Scotland](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/scotland/health/nhs-healthcare-s/nhs-dental-treatment-s/)). So complete registration at the first appointment, not before. That way nobody gives up their old practice and then waits unseen.
- **NHS forms are signed in the practice.** GP17 signatures are taken on paper or on a signature pad or tablet. NSS does not describe remote signing ([NSS](https://www.nss.nhs.scot/dental-services/claims/gp17-forms-and-patient-signatures/)). Collect everything else online before the visit, and leave a few minutes in each first-visit slot for signing.

## 2. The first call

### What the call is for
1. **Make human contact fast.** Lise Kuecker (Studio Grow): "When people go digital-only, none of them ever hit their goals." ([Athletech](https://athletechnews.com/the-90-day-presale-playbook/))
2. **Confirm** the household and everyone's details.
3. **Set honest expectations:** how invitations work, a realistic window, and the date of the next update.
4. **Keep people safe.** Send anyone in pain to the emergency route. Explain that registering with you ends their registration elsewhere. Say that you never ask for payment.
5. **Record preferences** that will later help fill the diary.

### Rules for callers
- **Attempt the first call on the same day**, between 8am and 9pm. New sign-ups come before retries of older ones. Glofox's target is under 5 minutes in business hours ([Glofox](https://www.glofox.com/blog/lead-response-time/)); treat that as an aim and same-day as the rule.
- **Make no more than 3 attempts per stage**, at different times of day, plus one SMS after the first missed call. Include one attempt between 3pm and 9pm: at one Edinburgh practice, 55% of link clicks came in that window ([dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/09/22/nhs-dental-participation-scotland/)). After that, mark the person NOT REACHED. They stay on the list and keep getting service messages.
- **Stop at once if someone asks not to be called**, and record it in the "Do not call" column. "Persistent and unwanted solicitations" are banned ([DMCC Act Sch 20 para 28](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20)). The CMA's example of this is a trader that does not record removal requests ([CMA207](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices)).
- **Plan for more calls than two people can make (our estimate).** 1,200–2,600 sign-ups a month is roughly 40–118 new people a day to call, before retries, re-checks, invitations and reminders. Two callers will fall behind in the peak months. Set up an overflow rota from the ~100 remote staff now, with the same script and access to the Sheet. If the backlog of people not yet called keeps growing, cut Meta spend. The usual limit is follow-up capacity, not lead volume ([FM Consulting](https://www.fmconsulting.net/member-retention/the-5-minute-lead-audit-your-gym-may-not-need-more-leads-it-needs-a-better-sales-process/)).

### Script outline (about 3 minutes)

> **1. Hello.** "Hi, is that [first name]? It's [name] from Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic. You joined our waiting list for NHS registration [today]. Have you got two minutes?"
>
> **2. Details.** Confirm full name, full postcode, mobile and email. "Who else in your household would like to register?" Take names and ages. For under-16s, note which parent or guardian is applying ([Sch 1 para 5(6)–(7)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1/paragraph/5)). Prompt parents to include babies ([NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/care-support-and-rights/nhs-services/dental/receiving-nhs-dental-treatment-in-scotland/)).
>
> **3. How it works.** "We invite people from the waiting list in stages as appointments become available, and we keep households together. Joining is free. When it's your turn, we'll text and email you a link to choose a time, or you can phone us. I can't give you an exact date yet. We expect the first invitations from [month], and we'll send you an update by [date] either way."
>
> **4. Current dentist.** "When did you last see a dentist? Have you had a letter saying your registration has ended?" Then: "Just so you know, when you register with us, your NHS registration at any other practice ends automatically. That only happens at your first appointment here, so you won't be left without a dentist while you wait." Record that they understood.
>
> **5. Pain check.** "Are you in pain, or do you have any swelling just now?" If yes: "We can't see people on the waiting list for urgent problems yet. Please phone NHS 24 on 111 [or the NHS Fife emergency dental service, **Check** number]." Flag it in the Sheet. Do not move them up the list.
>
> **6. Preferences.** Ask for their best days and times. "Could you come at short notice if a slot frees up?" (Y/N). Ask about anything that would help: access needs, nerves, an interpreter, or preferring a phone call to a link.
>
> **7. Safety.** "We'll never ask for payment, bank details, or a passport or ID to join our list. If anyone does, it isn't us." Fake NHS dental websites have collected payments and passport details ([dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/2024/11/01/warnings-after-fake-nhs-dental-practice-scammed-patients/)).
>
> **8. Close.** "You'll get a text in a moment confirming we've spoken. Is there anything you'd like to ask?"

**If they ask about implants or private care:** "Yes, we offer private treatment too. It's completely separate and never affects your NHS waiting list place. Would you like us to send you information?" Only tick marketing consent if they clearly say yes, and record the date and the wording used.

**If they're frustrated ("why not me yet?"):** explain the ordering rule (Section 4), give the date of the next update, and offer to check their details. Escalate abusive calls to the practice manager under a published zero-tolerance policy. In a UK survey of dental professionals, 57% had experienced or witnessed abuse in the past year, and 64% of those linked it to frustration over waiting times ([Scottish Dental](https://www.sdmag.co.uk/2023/08/29/access-frustration-fuelling-abuse/)).

**Never say:**
- "accepting/taking on NHS patients"
- "limited places"
- "first come first served"
- a firm date you can't guarantee
- that a treatment is "not available on the NHS", unless that is true ([Sch 1 para 17(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1))

### Status values (set up as a dropdown)

| Status | Meaning | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| NEW | Signed up, not yet called | Call the same day |
| CALLING | Call attempts made (count them in the attempts column) | Next attempt at a different time |
| NOT REACHED | 3 calls + SMS with no answer | Service messages only; try again at the re-check |
| WAITING – CONFIRMED | Spoken to, details checked | Update every 3 weeks |
| NOT UNTIL [month] | Wants a place, but not before a given month | Don't invite before that month |
| RECHECK SENT | "Still want your place?" message sent | 2 reminders, then a call |
| DORMANT | No reply to the re-check after reminders and a call | Keep on the list; reinstate at their original sign-up date if they reply |
| INVITED – W[n] | Invited in wave n | Invitation follow-up (Section 5) |
| INVITED – NOT BOOKED | No booking 7 days after the invite | One more offer in a later wave |
| BOOKED | First appointment booked | Reminders |
| APPT CONFIRMED | Replied YES, or confirmed by phone | – |
| REGISTERED | Attended and signed the forms | Normal patient journey |
| DNA 1 / DNA 2 | Missed the first appointment | One more offer, then review |
| LEFT – [reason] | Registered elsewhere, no longer needed, moved away, or asked to leave | No further contact |
| DO NOT CONTACT | Asked not to be contacted | None |

## 3. Keeping the list warm until opening

### Two message streams, chosen by the marketing-consent column

| | **Stream A: service messages** | **Stream B: marketing** |
|---|---|---|
| Who gets it | Everyone on the list | Only people with marketing consent = yes |
| What it covers | Confirmation, status updates, opening-date news, how invitations work, what registration includes and costs, what to bring, your dentists' names and GDC numbers, "still want your place?" checks, invitations, appointment confirmations and reminders | Meet-the-team features, fit-out reveals, open evenings, private treatment (implants and so on), any offer |
| Never include | Private treatment, offers or event promotion, not even in a P.S. | Anything that suggests buying something affects NHS access |
| Opt-out | "Reply LEAVE to come off the list" | One-click unsubscribe plus "Reply STOP", with "this doesn't affect your NHS waiting list place" |

Why so strict? In January 2026 the ICO fined Allay Claims £120,000. It rejected the firm's claim that its texts were service updates, finding them "clearly promotional". Even "subtle promotion" can turn a service message into marketing ([emailexpert](https://emailexpert.com/ico-fines-225000-nuisance-marketing/)). **Check** the current ICO wording directly. It could not be read for this research, and it is being reviewed after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025.

### Cadence
- **Instant:** S1 + E1.
- **After the first call:** S2.
- **Every 3 weeks (Stream A):** a short factual update ending "next update by [date]". Gym operators warn against going quiet after sign-up and say to "manage expectations around delays" ([ClubWise](https://www.clubwise.com/clubwise-blog/how-to-run-a-gym-pre-sale/); [Club OS](https://www.club-os.com/blog/pre-sale-playbook)). Never publish an opening date that isn't certain.
- **If there's a delay:** tell everyone that week (S3b).
- **"Still want your place?":** after 6–8 weeks of waiting, then every 8–12 weeks (our judgement). Google also advises senders to "periodically send messages to confirm that recipients want to stay subscribed" ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)).
  - Use NHS Highland's model: one-tap answers, a request to reply within 48 hours, and two reminders 48 hours apart ([NHS Highland Patient Hub](https://www.nhshighland.scot.nhs.uk/your-services/access-to-services/patient-hub/)).
  - Add a call from one of your callers before changing anyone's status. Never delete someone just because they didn't reply; mark them DORMANT.
- **Stream B:** no more than one email every 7–10 days ([Consentz](https://www.consentz.com/waiting-list-marketing-aesthetic-clinic/)).

**Email set-up:** set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC on the sending domain before ad spend scales. Keep the Gmail spam rate below 0.3% (Google recommends below 0.1%), and suppress addresses that bounce repeatedly ([Google](https://support.google.com/a/answer/81126)).

### Stream A templates (everyone)

**S1: instant SMS**
> Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic: thanks [first name], you're on our waiting list for NHS registration ([n] people). We'll call you from [number] [today / within 2 days], 8am–9pm. Joining is free. We never ask for payment, bank details or ID. Toothache now? Call NHS 24 on 111. Reply LEAVE to come off the list.

**E1: instant email.** Subject: *You're on our waiting list – here's what happens next*
- **What happens next.** We'll call you from [number] and send you an update by [date]. We invite people in stages as appointments become available, and households are invited together. "There's no need to queue or keep phoning. We'll contact you."
- **What NHS registration costs.**
  - Examinations are free ([PCA(D)(2023)5](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca2023-d-05.pdf); [SDR Amendment 170](https://www.scottishdental.nhs.scot/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/Amendment-No.-170-to-the-SDR-Pay-Award-12-October-2026.pdf)).
  - NHS treatment is free if you're under 26, pregnant or had a baby in the last 12 months, or on certain benefits.
  - Otherwise, the most you pay is £384 per course of treatment ([NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/care-support-and-rights/nhs-services/dental/receiving-nhs-dental-treatment-in-scotland/); [Citizens Advice Scotland](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/scotland/health/nhs-healthcare-s/nhs-dental-treatment-s/)). **Check** the current figures before you print them.
- **Already registered somewhere?** That registration ends automatically, but only at your first appointment here.
- **Urgent problems while you wait:** NHS 24 on 111, or [NHS Fife emergency dental service].
- **Who we are.** Real address, real photos of the building, your dentists' names and GDC numbers, and links to the group's other Fife practices. State that you are a mixed NHS and private practice ([GDC](https://gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising)).
- "We will never ask you for payment, bank details, passport or ID to join our waiting list."
- **Privacy notice link** and how to leave the list.

**S2: after a call**
> Thanks for speaking to us today, [first name]. You're on our waiting list for NHS registration, and we'll update you by [date]. Questions? Call [number], 8am–9pm. Reply LEAVE to come off the list.

**S2b: after a missed call**
> Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic tried to call you about our waiting list. There's no need to call back. We'll try again [tomorrow]. You're still on the list.

**S3: update every 3 weeks**
> Kirkcaldy Dental update: [our dentists Dr A and Dr B are confirmed / the fit-out is finished]. We expect to start inviting people from our waiting list to book from [month]. There's nothing you need to do now. We'll text you when it's your household's turn. Next update by [date]. Reply LEAVE to come off the list.

**S3b: delay**
> Kirkcaldy Dental: sorry, our opening has moved from [date] to [date] because [reason]. You're still on our waiting list, and nothing changes in how we invite people. Next update by [date].

**S4: "Still want your place?"**
> Kirkcaldy Dental: do you still want to register with us? Tap to let us know: [link]. Or reply YES, LATER (with the month) or NO. If you've registered elsewhere, that's fine, just tell us.

The form options are: *Yes, I still want to register* / *Yes, but not until [month]* / *No – I've registered elsewhere* / *No – no longer needed* / *Update my phone or email*. The Scottish vaccine programme found that out-of-date contact details led to missed appointments ([Scottish Government](https://gov.scot/publications/coronavirus-covid-19-vaccine-deployment-plan-update---july-2021/pages/invitations-appointments-and-digital-solutions)).

### Stream B templates (marketing consent = yes only)

- **B1, "Meet Dr [Name]":** who they are, their GDC number, what a first visit is like, and a photo in the real surgery. Footer: *You're getting this because you asked for news from us. Unsubscribe [link]. This never affects your NHS waiting list place.*
- **B2, private treatment information (e.g. implants):** education first, clear prices. It must say:
  - "Implants may not be suitable for everyone and are subject to a satisfactory assessment" ([GDC](https://gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising)).
  - "Private treatment is completely separate from NHS registration and never affects your NHS waiting list place." Waterloo Dental in Lanark uses similar wording ([Waterloo Dental](https://lanarkdentist.com/nhs-waitlist/)).

## 4. Invite waves and fairness

### What NHS Scotland rules allow
- **You set the order.** The GDS Regulations put no duty on a practice to accept every applicant, and they have no rule on the order of acceptance ([Sch 1 para 5](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1/paragraph/5)). NHS Highland says "a dentist is not obliged to accept all patients for NHS dental treatment" ([NHS Highland](https://www.nhshighland.scot.nhs.uk/your-services/in-person-services-and-locations/dental-services/registering-with-a-dental-practice/)). Write your rule down, apply it consistently, and log the reason for any exception.
- **You cannot sell priority or attach conditions.** The Regulations ban "any fee or other consideration", "any inducement" and any extra "condition" on NHS services ([Sch 1 para 20(1)(e),(g)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1)). There can be no fee or deposit to register or to book the examination. A child's registration should not depend on a parent going private ([PCA(D)(2026)1 paras 12–13](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2026-01.pdf)).
- **Equality Act.**
  - Under-18s are not protected from age discrimination in services, but adults are. Ordering by age that disadvantages adults needs to be "a proportionate means of achieving a legitimate aim" ([Equality Act s.28 and s.13](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2010/15/section/28)).
  - Treating disabled people more favourably is allowed (s.13(3)).
  - Never order by race, sex, religion, sexual orientation or anything that stands in for them. Postcode can act as a stand-in.
- **Pain does not move anyone up the list.** Send them to NHS 24 (111) or NHS Fife's emergency dental service. Urgent-care duties apply to registered patients, not to people on your list ([PCA(D)(2026)1 paras 25–27](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2026-01.pdf)).

### Ordering options

| Rule | Verdict | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Order of joining, households together | **Recommended default** | Easy to explain and seen as fair ([Maister](https://davidmaister.com/articles/1/52/index.html)). Wales's Powys pilot ordered people by the date they joined ([Senedd Research](https://research.senedd.wales/research-articles/developments-in-nhs-dentistry-in-wales/)) |
| Children / families with children first | **Possible – Check first** | There are Scottish precedents for age-limited NHS registration: children-only practices ([NHS Ayrshire & Arran](https://www.nhsaaa.net/?p=25316)), and age limits under 17, under 21 and 18-and-under ([NHS Highland](https://www.nhshighland.scot.nhs.uk/your-services/in-person-services-and-locations/dental-services/registering-with-a-dental-practice/)). Powys gives "first priority… to children" ([PTHB](https://pthb.nhs.wales/news/health-board-news/you-can-join-the-nhs-dental-waiting-list-using-the-dental-access-portal/)). Adults could challenge this, so write down a legitimate aim, such as prevention |
| Reasonable adjustments or priority for disabled people | **Allowed** | Equality Act s.13(3) |
| People with no dentist or who have been de-registered go first | **Check** | Not verified as lawful. Answers to "currently registered?" are unreliable anyway (see below) |
| Local postcodes first, or postcode limits | **Check; don't publish yet** | Postcode can stand in for protected characteristics, and no Scottish source shows postcode priority in use |
| SIMD 1–2 (most deprived areas) | **Give extra support, not queue priority** (unless checked) | Use a personal booking call and a choice of slots. Adult participation is 53.4% in the most deprived areas against 62.4% in the least deprived ([PHS](https://www.publichealthscotland.scot/publications/nhs-dental-data-monitoring-report/nhs-dental-data-monitoring-report-quarter-ending-march-2026/)) |
| Paid priority, a private plan, or a private hygienist visit first | **Never** | Sch 1 para 20; PCA(D)(2026)1 para 12 |
| "Refer a friend to move up" | **Never** | See Section 7 |
| Screening by "fit", or by protected characteristics | **Never** | Equality Act |

**Don't let "already registered = yes" push anyone down the list.** Scotland has lifetime registration: 95.1% of people are registered, but only 62.1% of them have been seen in two years ([PHS](https://www.publichealthscotland.scot/publications/nhs-dental-data-monitoring-report/nhs-dental-data-monitoring-report-quarter-ending-march-2026/)). More than 34,000 Fife residents were de-registered in 2024 ([Central Fife Times](https://www.centralfifetimes.com/news/25813106.fife-nhs-dentist-access-shows-slight-improvements/)). Breeze Kirkcaldy de-registered its patients in July 2023 ([Scottish Government FOI](https://www.gov.scot/binaries/content/documents/govscot/publications/foi-eir-release/2025/09/foi-202500476593/documents/foi-202500476593---information-released---documents/foi-202500476593---information-released---documents/govscot:document/FOI+202500476593+-+Information+released+-+Documents.pdf)).

**Publish this rule on the website and in the FAQ:**
> "We invite people from our waiting list in stages as appointments become available. We generally invite people in the order they joined, keeping households together, and we make reasonable adjustments for anyone who needs them. Joining and registering are free and never depend on private treatment."

If you adopt child-first after checking, add it *before* the first invitations go out. Internally, people who have confirmed they still want a place go ahead of DORMANT ones. A dormant person who replies goes back to their original sign-up date. This is our judgement, adapted from launch practice of inviting engaged people first ([LaunchList](https://getlaunchlist.com/blog/convert-waitlist-to-paying-customers)).

### Wave sequence
- **Wave 0, soft-opening week.** Invite staff, family, and staff from your other practices. Use it to test booking, NHS forms, SMS templates and the reception flow ([ScaleHaven](https://scalehaven.io/blog/new-med-spa-marketing-first-90-days/); [Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/back-to-basics-5-ways-restaurants-can-prepare-successful-grand-openi/720142/)). Don't use these guests for public reviews (see Section 7).
- **Wave 1: the first 50–100 sign-ups** (our judgement). This tests the invite → book → remind → attend loop with real patients.
- **Waves 2 onwards: weekly**, growing as you measure your real booking and attendance rates.
  - Only invite people you can offer an appointment within 1–3 weeks. Booking more than 4 weeks ahead was linked to about 5 times the odds of a no-show, compared with booking less than a day ahead ([Marshfield cohort](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149957/)).
- **Spread each wave's sends over 2–3 days and different times.** Inbound calls spike after every send ([Scottish Government](https://gov.scot/publications/coronavirus-covid-19-vaccine-deployment-plan-update---july-2021/pages/invitations-appointments-and-digital-solutions)). Re-introduce yourself to people who joined long ago: "You joined our waiting list in October…" Switching on automation for a whole old list at once "feels sudden and impersonal" ([Swasco](https://blog.swasco.co.uk/blog/gym-lead-follow-up)).
- **Never publish wave sizes, numbers of places or invitation dates.**

### Capacity planning
> **Households to invite this week** = free new-patient exam slots 1–3 weeks ahead ÷ (average people per household × booking rate × attendance rate)

**Worked example (illustration only; use your real diary).** Assume:
- 120 exam slots free in the next 1–3 weeks
- 2 people per household (in Faversham, 60 enquiries became 120+ patients: [KentOnline](https://www.kentonline.co.uk/faversham/news/huge-overnight-queues-and-27-000-calls-as-dentist-opens-up-n-291092/))
- 70% of invited households book
- 80% of booked patients attend

That gives 120 ÷ (2 × 0.7 × 0.8) ≈ **107 households (about 214 people)**. Replace the 70% and 80% with your own figures from week 2. Only overbook once you know your real no-show rate; the vaccine programme overbooked to match its actual no-shows ([Scottish Government](https://gov.scot/publications/coronavirus-covid-19-vaccine-deployment-plan-update---july-2021/pages/invitations-appointments-and-digital-solutions)).

**How long will the list get? (our arithmetic)**
- 2,000 sign-ups × about 2 people ≈ **4,000 people**. After that, 500–700 sign-ups a month adds about 1,000–1,400 people a month.
- Benchmarks vary widely. The Dunfermline SDAI practice was expected to register 1,500 per surgery in year one, which is about 87 a week across 3 surgeries ([Scottish Government FOI](https://www.gov.scot/binaries/content/documents/govscot/publications/foi-eir-release/2025/09/foi-202500476593/documents/foi-202500476593---information-released---documents/foi-202500476593---information-released---documents/govscot:document/FOI+202500476593+-+Information+released+-+Documents.pdf)). Your Glenrothes practice registered 2,598 in its peak month.
- If people join faster than you register them for more than a few weeks, waits stretch past three months. That is where interest falls sharply ([LaunchList](https://getlaunchlist.com/blog/convert-waitlist-to-paying-customers)). It is your signal to cut Meta spend or pause the form.

**Other limits on capacity:**
- **Each registration adds urgent-care duties**, both in hours and out of hours where there is no Board-wide service ([PCA(D)(2026)1](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2026-01.pdf)). Removing a patient needs 3 months' written notice ([Sch 1 para 10](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1/paragraph/10)). Pace invitations to clinical and emergency capacity, not to the length of the list.
- **Dentists are the bottleneck.** Time each wave to clinicians who are confirmed and on the dental list.
  - New recruits waiting for mandatory training can now be listed provisionally for 6 months ([PCA(D)(2026)2](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2026-2.pdf)).
  - Newly listed dentists fall into Tier 2 of the new prior-approval system, which has lower thresholds.
- **SDAI grant (if you apply).**
  - You must register 1,500 new NHS patients per grant-aided surgery within 12 months of the first payment.
  - Patients registered with any of the practice's contractors in the previous 36 months don't count. That may include transfers from Glenrothes.
  - At least 80% of gross income must come from NHS work (GDS) ([PCA(D)(2023)2](https://www.scottishdental.nhs.scot/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/PCAD20232-Revised-Scottish-Dental-Access-Initiative-25-April-2023.pdf)).
  - Kirkcaldy is listed as an eligible area ([PCA(D)(2025)1](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2025-01.pdf)). **Check.**
- **Pause switch.** Decide in advance: if the number of people not yet called is more than [2 days of caller capacity], turn the ads off and change the form to say "We've paused new sign-ups while we contact everyone already on our list." Practices in Crewkerne and Chard both paused this way ([NHS Somerset](https://nhssomerset.nhs.uk/news/opening-of-third-new-dental-practice-brings-nhs-care-to-thousands-more-people-in-somerset/)).
- **Keep Meta targeting tight.** In Crewkerne, about 25% of sign-ups came from outside the local area ([NHS Somerset](https://nhssomerset.nhs.uk/news/opening-of-third-new-dental-practice-brings-nhs-care-to-thousands-more-people-in-somerset/)).

## 5. Opening week

### Before the doors open
- **Wave 0 is done** and the problems it found are fixed.
- **Test the automations.** No invitation or reminder should ever go out for a slot that has passed or been filled. In an English survey, 23% of NHS users had an invitation arrive after the appointment date ([Healthwatch England](https://www.healthwatch.co.uk/news/2026-04-16/two-thirds-patients-and-carers-experience-nhs-admin-problems)).
- **Put up a door notice, a website banner and pinned social posts** saying: **"No walk-in registrations. Join our waiting list online or by phone, and we'll contact you."** In Forres, more than 500 people queued in pouring rain for a sign-up morning, and the practice then moved to online sign-up ([Forres Gazette](https://www.forres-gazette.co.uk/news/hundreds-queued-in-the-rain-for-nhs-places-at-dentist-331804/)).
- **Have a press and social holding statement and an FAQ ready before Wave 1.**
- **Set the inbound phone message:** "To join our waiting list, please visit [web address], or leave your name and number and we'll call you back. If you've been invited, press 1." Faversham logged 27,000 calls against 700 emails ([KentOnline](https://www.kentonline.co.uk/faversham/news/huge-overnight-queues-and-27-000-calls-as-dentist-opens-up-n-291092/)).
- **Staff up.** "It's always better to be overstaffed" ([Restaurant Dive](https://www.restaurantdive.com/news/back-to-basics-5-ways-restaurants-can-prepare-successful-grand-openi/720142/)). Keep the overflow callers on for invitation follow-up.
- **Tell the Fife Dental Advice Line**, which checks monthly which practices are providing NHS registration, and ask to be listed as "join our waiting list online" ([NHS Fife evidence](https://www.parliament.scot/-/media/files/committees/health-social-care-and-sport-committee/correspondence/2024/nhs-fife-response.pdf)).
- **Check all materials and the website.**
  - Materials: no NHS logo, and never "NHS" in the practice name ([NHS Scotland brand guidelines](https://policyonline.nhslothian.scot/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NHS-Scotland-Brand-Guidelines.pdf)).
  - The website must show the complaints routes (NHS Fife for NHS care, the Dental Complaints Service for private care), a link to the GDC, GDC numbers, and a "last updated" date ([GDC](https://gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising)).

### Booking: phone and online side by side
- **Put both routes in every invitation:** a self-booking link to a released block of first-exam slots (if your diary system allows it) *and* the phone number. Never make booking or cancelling link-only ([NHS England DNA guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/reducing-did-not-attends-dnas-in-outpatient-services/)).
- **Test online booking against phone booking.** The evidence on whether online booking reduces no-shows is mixed: fewer at a private eye clinic, more at a public hospital ([Frontiers](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12081397/)). A salon vendor claims 49% fewer, with no method given ([Phorest](https://www.phorest.com/blog/salon-no-shows/)). At one Edinburgh practice, about five in six patients used the booking page rather than calling ([dentistry.co.uk](https://dentistry.co.uk/2026/09/22/nhs-dental-participation-scotland/)).
- **Send invitations mid-afternoon to early evening**, then test what works for you.
- **Book the whole household in one go**, in back-to-back slots.
- **Follow up invited households over 7 days** (adapted from [Glofox](https://www.glofox.com/blog/sales-follow-up-process/)):
  - Day 0: S5 + E5.
  - Day 2: call if they haven't booked.
  - Day 4: SMS offering a specific slot: "Would [day] at [time] suit your family? Reply YES or call [number]."
  - Day 7: a final call offering a specific slot.
  - Then mark them INVITED – NOT BOOKED and give them one more offer in a later wave. Aim to book within 48 hours ([FM Consulting](https://www.fmconsulting.net/member-retention/the-5-minute-lead-audit-your-gym-may-not-need-more-leads-it-needs-a-better-sales-process/)).
- **On every booking call:** "Could you read the day and time back to me?" This was part of a package that cut missed appointments by 31.7% in two NHS GP practices ([JRSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3308641/)).

**S5: invitation SMS**
> Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic: it's your household's turn to book your first NHS examination ([n] people). Choose a time: [link], or call [number], 8am–9pm. Please book by [date]. If we haven't heard from you by then, we'll give you a call. Reply LEAVE to come off the list.

**E5: invitation email** should cover:
- the first visit is a free examination
- link to the medical-history form
- what to bring
- arrive 10 minutes early to sign the NHS forms (our judgement)
- parents or guardians register under-16s at the same time
- any registration elsewhere ends at the first appointment
- how to cancel or rearrange
- the missed-appointment policy (once confirmed)
- "we never ask for payment, bank details or ID"

### Reminders for every booked first appointment
What the evidence shows:
- In a trial with 54,066 patients, two automated reminders (3 days and 1 day before) gave 4.4% missed appointments, against 5.3–5.8% for either reminder alone ([AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/optimizing-number-and-timing-of-appointment-reminders-a-randomized-trial)).
- SMS and phone reminders work about equally well. Attendance was 67.8% with no reminder and 78.6% with SMS ([Cochrane CD007458](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:24310741%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json)).
- SMS alone is not a cure. In a 2026 meta-analysis, phone reminders significantly improved attendance, while SMS alone narrowly missed significance ([JHMHP](https://jhmhp.amegroups.org/article/view/10215/html)).
- Adding an automated call for high-risk patients cut no-shows from 11.3% to 9.6% ([Penn Medicine](https://pair.upenn.edu/publication/automated-calls-added-to-sms-reminders-reduce-missed-appointments-among-high-risk-patients/)).
- Locally, SMS reminders at a Kirkcaldy dental access centre cut missed appointments from 31% to 14%, in a small before-and-after study in 2011 ([Perry](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/search?query=EXT_ID:21968040%20AND%20SRC:MED&resultType=core&format=json)).

| When | Message | If there's no reply |
|---|---|---|
| At booking | **S6** + email (medical form, calendar link) | – |
| 7 days before (optional) | Email: what to expect, parking, what to bring | – |
| 3 days before | **S7** (two-way) | Call non-repliers and high-risk patients the next day |
| 2 days before | – | Medical form not done → call |
| 1 day before | **S8** | Unconfirmed and can't be reached → offer the slot to standby |
| Morning of (optional, family blocks) | Short nudge, within 8am–9pm | Untested; try it and measure |

**S6:** Booked: [names], [Day DD Mon] at [time], Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic, [address]. Please fill in your medical history form before you come: [link]. Add to calendar: [link]. To change, reply CHANGE or call [number].

**S7:** Reminder: [names] at Kirkcaldy Dental on [Day] at [time]. Reply YES to confirm or CHANGE to rearrange, or call [number] (8am–9pm).

**S8:** See you tomorrow at [time], [address]. Please arrive 10 minutes early to sign your NHS registration forms. Can't make it? Reply CHANGE or call [number] and we'll rebook you.

**High-risk flags** (our guesses, not a tested model):
- no reply to S7
- a previous missed appointment (the strongest single predictor, OR 4.42: [Marshfield](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11149957/))
- a large family block
- booked more than 3 weeks ahead
- medical form not completed

For households in SIMD 1–2, offer extra support rather than suspicion.

**Wording**
- **Do:** a plain reminder with a phone number and a way to reply.
- **Don't:** "Please be fair to others waiting…". That "empathy" message did worst in two NHS trials. The only wording that clearly cut missed appointments stated a specific cost to the NHS ([PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0137306)). No verified figure exists for NHS Scotland dentistry, so don't invent one.
- **Don't** put up posters or posts counting how many people missed appointments ([JRSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3308641/)).

### Standby list for cancellations
- Offer freed slots to people in the "short-notice" column, **one household at a time**, in sign-up order.
- Give each offer a short expiry (for example 30 minutes). Only send offers between 8am and 9pm, and no later than about 2 hours before the slot (our judgement; [Acuity](https://acuityscheduling.com/learn/fill-slots-with-waitlist-software) lets you set this cut-off).
- Don't text one slot to 10 people at once. That turns into a scramble and raises fairness concerns for people with less digital access ([JMIR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13395261/)).
- As a guide, expect about 1 in 4 freed slots to be refilled. That was the median across 90 US health systems, not a target.

**S9:** Kirkcaldy Dental: a first examination has come free on [day] at [time] for up to [n] people. Reply YES by [time] if you'd like it. If not, no problem, you keep your place on the list.

### Missed first appointments
- **Treat a first missed appointment as a reason to offer one more slot, not to remove the person.**
  > **S10:** We missed you today, [first name]. We'd still like to see you. Reply BOOK or call [number] and we'll find another time.
- **Children:** set a "Was Not Brought" follow-up rule ([BDA](https://bda.org/media/xc2px0rp/wnb-implementation-guide-aw.pdf)). **Check** Scottish child-protection guidance.
- **Missed-appointment charges: not yet.** The guidance conflicts:
  - PCA(D)(2026)1 para 15 says registered patients can be charged ([PCA(D)(2026)1](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2026-01.pdf)).
  - Citizens Advice Scotland says only if a sign is displayed, and not if the slot was refilled ([Citizens Advice Scotland](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/scotland/health/nhs-healthcare-s/nhs-dental-treatment-s/)).
  - Scottish Dental magazine quoted NHS guidance that "surgeries can't charge you for not turning up" ([sdmag](https://www.sdmag.co.uk/2023/10/03/no-shows-charged/)).

  Never charge for a missed registration examination, and never take a deposit for one. Your practice leaflet must explain the arrangements for cancelling appointments (GDS Sch 5 para 9).
- **Plan for summer.** Salon no-shows peak in June–August ([Scratch/Treatwell](https://www.scratchmagazine.co.uk/news/beauty-salon-no-shows-uk-locations-highest-rates/)). The evidence is weak, but plan extra reminder effort.
- **Screen before registering, not after.** Removing a registered patient who never attends needs 3 months' notice, or NHS Fife's approval for shorter notice ([Sch 1 para 10](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1/paragraph/10)).

## 6. Private treatment (implants etc.)

### The legal lines
- Private care is allowed "with the consent of the patient" ([Sch 1 para 17(1)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1)).
- You must not falsely say that needed care isn't available on the NHS, or mislead patients about the quality of NHS care, to win private work (para 17(2); repeated in [PCA(D)(2026)1 para 30](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2026-01.pdf)).
- NHS services cannot carry a fee, inducement or condition (para 20(1)(e),(g)). There is no deposit to register or to book the examination. A child's NHS place must not depend on a parent going private ([PCA(D)(2026)1 paras 12–13](https://www.publications.scot.nhs.uk/files/pca-d-2026-01.pdf)).
- A Bristol practice reportedly told patients to pay about £69 for a private hygienist visit when they registered. It apologised "for any confusion". The local health board (ICB) said patients "should not be obliged to pay for an appointment at the time of registering" ([Fishponds Voice](https://fishpondsvoice.co.uk/?p=1601)). The BBC report was seen only through syndicated copies. This was in England, but the lesson applies here.

### How to offer private treatment properly
1. **Keep it out of the NHS journey.** No private content in Stream A messages, invitation calls, invitations or first-visit check-in.
2. **Private options come from the dentist, after the examination, in the treatment plan.** The plan must list private alternatives and their costs ([Sch 1 para 5(2)(d)(iii)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1)).
3. **Market only to people with consent = yes**, and in separate messages (Stream B).
4. **Say it everywhere:** "Choosing private treatment never affects your NHS waiting list place or your NHS registration."
5. **Follow the advertising rules.**
   - Say you are a mixed NHS and private practice.
   - Implant promotion must say the treatment "may not be appropriate for every patient" and is "conditional on a satisfactory assessment" ([GDC](https://gdc-uk.org/standards-guidance/standards-and-guidance/gdc-guidance-for-dental-professionals/guidance-on-advertising)).
   - Don't make false time-limit claims ([DMCC Sch 20 para 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20)), and don't rerun a substantially similar offer soon after its stated end date ([CMA207](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices)).
   - Only call something "free" if nothing at all is payable (para 23), and don't use inflated "(£X value)" anchors. **Check** GDC and ASA rules on launch offers.
6. **Deposits are fine for private consultations**, such as implant consultations, with clear written terms. They are the salon sector's strongest no-show tool ([Phorest](https://www.phorest.com/blog/salon-no-shows/)). Never for NHS care.
7. **Keep complaints routes separate.** NHS care goes through the NHS complaints procedure; private care goes to the GDC Dental Complaints Service ([Citizens Advice Scotland](https://www.citizensadvice.org.uk/scotland/health/nhs-healthcare-s/nhs-dental-treatment-s/)). Keep NHS and private paperwork clearly apart.
8. **Optional: private emergency appointments for people on the list.** Waterloo Dental in Lanark says such patients "keep your NHS waitlist position" ([Waterloo Dental](https://lanarkdentist.com/nhs-waitlist/)). If you offer this, mention NHS 24 first and record that the person's place is unaffected.
9. **SDAI.** If you take the grant, at least 80% of gross income must come from NHS work, so model implant revenue against that ([PCA(D)(2023)2](https://www.scottishdental.nhs.scot/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/PCAD20232-Revised-Scottish-Dental-Access-Initiative-25-April-2023.pdf)).

## 7. What to borrow vs avoid from other industries

| Tactic (where it comes from) | For your NHS list | How, or why not |
|---|---|---|
| Instant confirmation (all sectors) | **Borrow** | Already in place. Add the next step, the next-update date and the "we never ask for payment" line |
| Same-day human follow-up (med-spas, gyms) | **Borrow** | The common gap is slow human contact ([Aesthetix CRM](https://aesthetixcrm.com/speed-to-lead-aesthetic-practices/)) |
| Phone calls, not automation alone (gyms) | **Borrow** | "You have to be willing to pick up the phone" ([Athletech](https://athletechnews.com/the-90-day-presale-playbook/)) |
| Releasing places in waves (gyms, product launches) | **Borrow** | Size waves to the diary, send them privately, never announce them ([Resamania](https://resamania.co.uk/blog/gym-pre-sales-campaign-ideas/)) |
| Soft opening before the full opening (restaurants, spas) | **Borrow** | A test week with staff and family ([Hand & Stone Nolensville](https://handandstone.com/franchise/blog/hand-stone-member-turned-owner-redefines-relaxation-in-nolensville-with-the-brands-first-franchise-offering-side-by-side-facials/)) |
| Two reminders, 3 days and 1 day before (healthcare) | **Borrow** | [AJMC](https://www.ajmc.com/view/optimizing-number-and-timing-of-appointment-reminders-a-randomized-trial) |
| Read-back of the appointment (NHS GP practices) | **Borrow** | [JRSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3308641/) |
| "Still want your place?" list clean-up (NHS Highland) | **Borrow** | Never silently delete people who don't reply |
| Pausing sign-ups when overwhelmed (Crewkerne, Chard) | **Borrow** | Use honest wording |
| Weekly nurture emails (med-spas) | **Adapt** | A service update to everyone every 3 weeks; feature content only to consent = yes ([Aesthetix Media](https://aesthetixmedia.com/blog/med-spa-grand-opening)) |
| Founder story, meet the team, design reveal | **Adapt** | Stream B only. Use real photos; the finished building must match what was promised ([Club Solutions](https://clubsolutionsmagazine.com/2020/08/the-point-of-pre-sales/)) |
| Open evening or preview event (gyms, med-spas) | **Adapt** | Invitation only, for list members with consent. No sign-ups or registrations taken on the day (lesson from Forres) |
| "Book tonight" desk at the event ([More Booked Consults](https://morebookedconsults.com/blog/medspa-grand-opening/)) | **Adapt** | Only for households already invited in the current wave |
| 48-hour list-first window ([Consentz](https://www.consentz.com/waiting-list-marketing-aesthetic-clinic/)) | **Adapt** | The list is the only way in. NHS appointments are never released publicly |
| Cancellation waitlist ([Acuity](https://acuityscheduling.com/learn/fill-slots-with-waitlist-software)) | **Adapt** | Offer slots one household at a time, not as a race ([JMIR](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13395261/)) |
| Founding memberships, paid presale (gyms, Hand & Stone) | **Avoid** | NHS services cannot carry a fee or inducement ([Sch 1 para 20](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ssi/2010/208/schedule/1)) |
| Deposits, prepayment, card on file (salons) | **Avoid for NHS**; fine for private consultations | "You do not need to pay a deposit for an appointment to register" ([NHS inform](https://www.nhsinform.scot/care-support-and-rights/nhs-services/dental/receiving-nhs-dental-treatment-in-scotland/)) |
| Paid priority or a "VIP" fast track | **Never** | Sch 1 para 20(1)(e),(g) |
| Early-bird or founder discounts | **Not applicable to NHS** | NHS charges are set nationally. Private offers need a real end date that you honour |
| "First 50 patients", countdowns, "specials end this week", "filling fast" | **Avoid** | False urgency is banned ([DMCC Sch 20 para 7](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/2024/13/schedule/20); [CMA207](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/unfair-commercial-practices-cma207/unfair-commercial-practices)), and it breaks the owner's rule |
| "Free consultation ($150 value)" | **Avoid** unless genuinely free, and never with inflated values | DMCC Sch 20 para 23 |
| Refer a friend to move up the list (Robinhood) | **Never** | Backfires when demand already exceeds capacity, and looks unfair ([Tuck](https://tuck.dartmouth.edu/news/articles/refer-a-friend-jump-the-line)) |
| "Enter your friends' numbers" forms | **Never** | ICO refer-a-friend consent risk ([Foot Anstey](https://www.footanstey.com/our-insights/articles-news/what-do-you-need-to-know-about-the-icos-latest-guidance-on-refer-a-friend-schemes/)) |
| Public daily quotas, walk-in registration days | **Never** | King's Lynn, Faversham, Bristol and Forres all ended in queues and headlines |
| Reviews from soft-open guests, staff, family or free-treatment guests | **Avoid** unless the connection is clearly disclosed | Fake or undisclosed incentivised reviews are banned (DMCC Sch 20 para 13) |
| A private hygienist visit as part of joining | **Never** | The Fishponds case |
| "X people missed appointments" posters, or "be fair to others waiting" texts | **Avoid** | [JRSM](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3308641/); [PLOS ONE](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371%2Fjournal.pone.0137306) |

## 8. Metrics to track (weekly dashboard)

Count each week's new numbers rather than running totals, and break them down by source (Meta, leaflet, banner QR code, website). If any stage stays flat for two weeks, change the script or the message ([Athletech](https://athletechnews.com/the-90-day-presale-playbook/)).

| Metric | Definition | What to watch |
|---|---|---|
| Sign-ups and people | New this week, by source; average people per sign-up | Drives wave sizing |
| Speed to first call | Median hours from sign-up to first attempt; % called the same day | Aim for ≥90% same day (our target) |
| Reached | % reached within 3 attempts | – |
| Backlog not yet called | Count of NEW | Triggers the pause switch |
| Time waiting | Median weeks from sign-up to invitation; % waiting more than 8 and more than 12 weeks | Interest falls after about 3 months |
| Re-check results | % yes / later / left / dormant | NHS Highland's hospital lists got 81% replies and 9% removals ([NHS Highland](https://www.nhshighland.scot.nhs.uk/news/2024/12/waiting-list-validation-shows-great-results/)); use only as a rough guide |
| Invite to booked | % booked within 48 hours and within 7 days, per wave | Feeds the wave formula |
| Confirmation | % replying YES to S7; % of medical forms done 2 days before | Early warning of no-shows |
| Missed first appointments | Missed ÷ booked, by SIMD, slot time, weekday, how far ahead booked, and source ([NHS England](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/reducing-did-not-attends-dnas-in-outpatient-services/)) | Feeds overbooking |
| Standby fill | Refilled ÷ freed slots | About 25% is the US median |
| Throughput vs inflow | People registered per week vs people joining per week | Inflow higher than throughput for 4+ weeks → cut ads |
| Conversion by sign-up week | % of each week's sign-ups who end up attending, by source | Shows true cost per registered patient |
| Opt-outs and complaints | LEAVE, STOP, complaints, abusive calls | Rising = a messaging problem |
| Email health | Gmail spam rate; bounces | Below 0.3%, aim for below 0.1% |
| Out-of-area share | % of sign-ups outside the target postcodes | Tighten ad targeting |
| SDAI progress (if applicable) | Registrations per surgery vs 1,500 in 12 months | Grant risk |
| Private (kept separate) | Implant enquiries and consultations from Stream B | Never linked to NHS status |
