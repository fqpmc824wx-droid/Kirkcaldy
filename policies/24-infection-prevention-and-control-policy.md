## 1. Purpose

This is the overarching infection prevention and control (IPC) policy for Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic. Its purpose is to reduce the risk of passing infective organisms between patients, staff, contractors and visitors, and to prevent and control healthcare associated infections (HAIs).

In dentistry the main sources of infective organisms are blood and saliva. IPC therefore covers hand hygiene, personal protective equipment (PPE), instrument decontamination, cleaning of the care environment, waste management, water safety and the management of exposure to blood and body fluids.

All team members have a duty to take appropriate precautions to protect patients, colleagues and visitors from the risk of infection during, or associated with, dental treatment. The GDC can treat failure to use adequate IPC methods as professional misconduct.

The PSM allows a practice to have one large IPC policy or several smaller ones. This practice uses this overarching policy plus a set of linked policies (section 8).

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- everyone who works at the practice: dentists (including implant surgeons and visiting clinicians), dental care professionals, dental nurses, trainees, reception and administrative staff, cleaners and locums;
- all areas of the premises: surgeries, the local decontamination unit (LDU), reception, the waiting room, patient and staff toilets, the staff room and storage areas;
- NHS and private treatment, including implant and other surgical procedures;
- domiciliary visits, if the practice provides them (see 33-domiciliary-visits-ipc.md);
- contractors and visitors, as far as they may be affected by our work.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Our commitments

The practice will:

1. Apply the **Standard Infection Control Precautions (SICPs)** in the NIPCM to every patient, every time, whether or not an infection is known.
2. Use **Transmission Based Precautions (TBPs)** in addition to SICPs when treating a patient with a known or suspected infection or colonisation (for example, a respiratory infection), following the current NIPCM chapter.
3. Decontaminate reusable instruments in a dedicated LDU, in line with SHTM 01-05 and GUID 5005.
4. Train every member of staff in IPC appropriate to their role, at induction and regularly after that, and record it.
5. Keep all IPC policies under regular review and tell staff about changes.

### 5.2 Standard Infection Control Precautions

The NIPCM sets out the SICPs. The table shows how each applies here and where the detail is.

| SICP (NIPCM) | What we do | Detail |

| Respiratory and cough hygiene | Tissues, hand rub and a lidded waste bin in the waiting area. Posters asking people to cover coughs and sneezes. Staff with respiratory symptoms follow current NIPCM and Occupational Health advice. | This policy |

### 5.3 Transmission Based Precautions

1. SICPs may not be enough to prevent spread of some infections. When a patient has a known or suspected infection or colonisation, the clinician decides, using the current NIPCM, whether to use TBPs.
2. TBPs may include: deferring routine care, booking the patient at the end of a session, using a surgery that can be closed off, enhanced PPE (e.g. respirator, gown), and extra cleaning after the appointment. [Practice decision: which surgery is used for TBP patients.]
3. Any respirator use (e.g. FFP3) requires fit-testing of the wearer. [Practice decision: fit-testing provider and record.]
4. Check the NIPCM for current guidance whenever a new infection alert is issued by the Scottish Government, the Chief Dental Officer or NHS Fife.

### 5.5 Confidentiality

1. Patients' medical histories, including any infection status, are confidential. They are held in Dentally and only seen by staff who need them for care.
2. A patient's infection status must not be discussed at reception or where others can hear.
3. SICPs are applied to every patient because infection status is not always known. A known bloodborne virus does not need a different decontamination process; the decision to use TBPs for other infections follows 5.3.
4. Staff health information (vaccination history, immunity, BBV status) is confidential. The practice keeps only the Occupational Health fitness-for-work notice (see 30-health-clearance-and-immunisation.md).

### 5.6 Training in IPC

1. Every new member of staff receives IPC training appropriate to their role at **induction**, before they start clinical or decontamination duties. The IPC Lead uses the PSM New Staff Induction Checklist.
2. Induction covers, as a minimum: this policy and the linked policies; hand hygiene technique; PPE (donning and doffing); surface cleaning and zoning; spillage management; sharps safety and what to do after an injury; waste segregation; and, for clinical staff, the LDU process and equipment tests.
3. Staff are not signed off for LDU duties until the IPC Lead has observed them carrying out each step competently.
4. GDC registrants must complete **5 hours of disinfection and decontamination CPD in each CPD cycle** (current GDC requirement – check current GDC guidance). Each registrant keeps their own CPD record and gives the Practice Manager a copy of certificates.
5. For the NHS Combined Practice Inspection (CPI), the practice must have completed **IPC training provided by the Public Services Delivery (PSD) Scotland Quality Improvement in Practice Training team** (Infection Control and Decontamination) and developed an **action plan**. The IPC Lead arranges this before the first inspection [date booked].
6. Staff are encouraged to use NES TURAS resources, including the Scottish Infection Prevention and Control Education Pathway (SIPCEP) modules.
7. Training is refreshed [practice decision: at least annually] and whenever procedures, equipment or guidance change.

### 5.7 Keeping up to date and review

1. The IPC Lead checks the NIPCM, SHTM 01-05 and the PSM updates page [practice decision: every 3 months] and after any national alert.
2. Changes are discussed at the next practice meeting and recorded in the minutes. Staff re-sign changed policies.
3. Each IPC policy carries a version number, date, who changed it and a short note of what changed (PSM MDDP advice).

### 5.8 Outbreaks and incidents

1. Any suspected link between the practice and an infection in a patient or staff member is reported to the Practice Owner at once.
2. The Practice Owner seeks advice from NHS Fife (East Region Health Protection Team 0300 790 6264, Mon–Fri 9am–6pm; out of hours, NHS Fife switchboard 01592 643355 and ask for the on-call public health doctor) and follows it.
3. IPC incidents (e.g. a decontamination failure, a sharps injury, a missed DUWL flush) are recorded and reviewed as significant events (see 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md).
