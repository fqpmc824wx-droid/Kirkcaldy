## 1. Purpose

A medical emergency is rare in a dental practice, but it can happen at any time, to any patient, visitor or member of staff. This policy makes sure that every member of the team at Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic:

- knows their role in a medical emergency;
- is trained and competent to use the emergency drugs and equipment;
- can find the emergency drugs, oxygen and AED immediately;
- works to a written protocol that is checked, practised and reviewed.

It also sets out how the practice stores and transports medical oxygen, and how it manages midazolam, which is a Schedule 3 controlled drug.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to everyone who works at the practice: dentists (including implant surgeons and visiting clinicians), dental nurses, hygienists and therapists, reception and management staff, trainees and locums. It covers all patients, escorts and visitors on the premises.

The PSM lists these medical emergencies that may arise in dental practice (from the British National Formulary): adrenal insufficiency, anaphylaxis, asthma, cardiac emergencies, epileptic seizures, hypoglycaemia and syncope (faint). Aspiration, choking and stroke may also be encountered.

The practice [does / does not] provide conscious sedation. If it does, the additional equipment and drugs in the SDCEP *Conscious Sedation in Dentistry* guidance apply and must be set out in a separate sedation policy. The practice [does / does not] provide domiciliary care; if it does, see section 5.9.

### 5.4 Oxygen supply

1. The practice holds enough oxygen for **at least 30 minutes at 15 litres per minute**: for example two size D, two size CD or one size E cylinder. We hold: 2 size CD cylinders (supplier: SOS Oxygen).
   - Size D: 340 litres, about 22 minutes.
   - Size CD: 460 litres, about 30 minutes.
   - Size E: 680 litres, up to 45 minutes.
2. A risk assessment of the number of cylinders needed is recorded by [Name] on [date]. Kirkcaldy town centre is not a remote location, but the assessment considers likely ambulance response times.
3. Cylinders must deliver at least 15 litres per minute, be at least 75% full, be in date, and be checked weekly and after every use.
4. Some cylinders have an integral regulator and others a bolt-on regulator. These are not interchangeable. Our cylinders are [integral / bolt-on – type]. Spare cylinders must match.
5. Cylinders and regulators are serviced or replaced as the manufacturer directs: currently every 5 years for standard steel/aluminium cylinders and standard regulators; carbon-fibre cylinders may need service or exchange every 3 years. Test certificates for cylinders and any separate regulators are kept in the medical emergencies file.

### 5.5 Storage of medical oxygen

Medical oxygen is non-flammable but a very strong oxidant. It can make oils, greases and many plastics burn violently. Therefore cylinders are:

- stored under cover, inside, dry and clean, and not exposed to extremes of heat or cold;
- kept away from stocks of combustible material;
- kept separate from industrial and other non-medical cylinders, and from other medical gas cylinders;
- stored so that full and empty cylinders are kept apart and clearly identified;
- used in strict rotation, earliest filling date first;
- stored as the manufacturer directs (size E and smaller stored horizontally; size F and larger vertically);
- never stored in a vehicle.

A notice prohibiting smoking and naked flames is posted at the cylinder storage area. The Fire Safety Lead informs Scottish Fire and Rescue Service of the cylinder store location (cylinders may explode in a fire) and records this in the fire risk assessment (see 19-fire-safety-policy.md). Staff do not handle oxygen equipment with oily hands or hand cream.

### 5.6 Transport of medical oxygen

Oxygen is transported only if the practice carries out domiciliary visits or moves cylinders between sites. Our cylinders with regulator, hose and mask form a "ready to use" set and are exempt from the ADR carriage rules, but leakage must still be prevented. Before any transport:

1. [Name] carries out and records a risk assessment of the hazards of transporting medical gas, and keeps it under review.
2. Staff who transport cylinders are trained in the properties of oxygen, how to operate the cylinder, the hazards in transit, and what to do in an emergency or leak.
3. The vehicle insurer is told that medical oxygen is carried.
4. In transit: the valve is properly closed; the cylinder is in a purpose-made bag or case and secured in the vehicle; it is removed from the vehicle as soon as possible. Oxygen is never left or stored in a vehicle.

### 5.8 What to do in a medical emergency (summary)

The full protocol is in the practice's adapted **Managing Medical Emergencies Policy and Procedure** (PSM template), which is kept in the emergency kit and the policy folder. It includes signs and symptoms of each emergency, how to manage each one, who does what, directions to the practice for the ambulance, and a handover note.

1. **Stop treatment.** Remove instruments from the mouth. Stay with the patient.
2. **Call for help.** Say "Medical emergency in Surgery [number]". The nearest free team member brings the emergency drugs, oxygen and AED.
3. **Assess** using the ABCDE approach (Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability, Exposure).
4. **Call 999** early if needed. The receptionist makes the call and gives the address: *Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic, 23–25 Tolbooth Street, Kirkcaldy KY1 1RW*, with directions: [entrance, parking, which door, floor]. The receptionist then meets the ambulance at the door.
5. **Treat** using the drugs and equipment as trained. If the patient is not breathing normally, start CPR and use the AED.
6. **Roles** (default; adjust to who is present):
   - Team leader: treating dentist.
   - Second rescuer: dental nurse (airway, oxygen, CPR, AED).
   - Runner / caller: receptionist (999 call, meet ambulance, keep other patients away).
   - Scribe: any other team member (times, observations, drugs given).
7. **Handover.** Give the paramedics the written handover note: patient details, medical history and medications, the dental procedure carried out (including any local anaesthetic, sedation or implant surgery), what happened, and treatment given with times.
8. **Record** everything in the patient's Dentally record the same day.
9. **Restock** the kit and refill or replace the oxygen cylinder at once.
10. **Review.** Carry out an SEA after **every** medical emergency (see 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md). Share the learning with all staff and make an action plan if needed. If a piece of medical equipment failed, report it through the national incident reporting system (Incident Reporting and Investigation Centre at Health Facilities Scotland).

If a medical emergency affects a patient's capacity to consent, see the PSM page on medical emergencies and consent and 03-consent-policy.md.

### 5.10 Midazolam (Schedule 3 controlled drug)

Midazolam is the only controlled drug that a primary care dental practice is justified to stock. It is used buccally to treat status epilepticus. It does not need to be kept in a locked CD cabinet or recorded in a CD register when held for emergencies, but ordering, prescription writing and denaturing are restricted, and it is an offence to possess it unless exempted under the Misuse of Drugs Regulations 2001. Dentists may possess it, but must have an SOP and controls that stop it falling into the possession of someone without these rights.

**General**

1. The Practice Owner / Principal Dentist [Nas] is the dentist with overall responsibility for midazolam.
2. The practice adopts the **Controlled Drug Standard Operating Procedure (SOP) Template for GDPs** (CDAO Network, NHS Scotland). It covers obtaining, recording, security, storage and denaturing, and names who does what. Adopted on [date].
3. NHS Fife CDAO (Director of Pharmacy and Medicines) via the Fife Controlled Drugs Governance Team: fife.cd@nhs.scot, 01383 565351 – kept in the SOP and in the key contacts list (38-business-continuity-plan.md).
4. Emergency midazolam is kept in the emergency drugs box with the other emergency drugs, so it can be reached at once. The drugs box is kept [out of public reach / in a staff-only area].
5. If the practice provides conscious sedation with midazolam, sedation stock is kept in a locked cupboard, and flumazenil (antagonist) is available. [Delete if no sedation.]

**Ordering and receipt**

1. Midazolam is ordered from a community pharmacy using a **Controlled Drug Requisition Form (CDRF)** from NHS Fife. Practice headed notepaper is used only in exceptional circumstances. Alternatively, an **Emergency Dental Box** may be obtained from NHS Scotland Pharmaceutical 'Specials' Service; this includes midazolam injection labelled for buccal use.
2. Order only the minimum quantity needed, based on previous use.
3. Keep records of orders for at least **2 years**.
4. On delivery, check that it matches the order. Leave the tamper-evident seal intact on full packs or in the Emergency Dental Box.
5. If there is a discrepancy, tell the supplier and do not accept the delivery unless exceptional circumstances prevent return. If so, record receipt and return the stock as soon as possible.
6. Sign the delivery note and keep a copy for at least **2 years**.
7. Put the midazolam in its designated place (the emergency drugs box) straight away.
8. Records: [practice decision – order and delivery records are filed in the CD folder in the Practice Manager's office and noted in the weekly log].

**Use**

1. Verify the patient's identity before giving midazolam.
2. Record in the patient's clinical record: date, time, strength, presentation, route (form of administration), dose, and the name and position of the team member who gave it.
3. The team has watched the NES training video on buccal administration of the injection solution (if the injection form is held).

**Destruction and disposal**

1. Used or expired Emergency Dental Boxes are returned to NHS Scotland Pharmaceutical 'Specials' Service, which denatures, disposes of and replaces the contents.
2. Any other expired or unwanted midazolam is denatured (rendered irretrievable) using a controlled drug denaturing kit from a community pharmacy or waste contractor.
3. The used denaturing kit goes in the pharmaceutical waste container (e.g. blue-lidded bin) for incineration (see 22-waste-management-policy.md).
4. Record each denaturing: date, drug, strength, quantity, reason, and who carried it out. [Practice decision: a second team member witnesses and countersigns – check the CDAO SOP template.]

**Incidents**

Any loss, discrepancy, suspected theft or other incident involving midazolam is reported to the NHS Fife CDAO using the **Controlled Drugs Incident Report to Controlled Drugs Accountable Officer** form, and is also recorded and analysed as an incident (see 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md).

## 7. Training

1. Every team member who might be involved in a medical emergency completes emergency life support training **at least annually**. Contact NHS Fife for details of suitable providers. Provider: [provider]; date booked: [date].
2. Training covers: ABCDE assessment; Basic Life Support for adults and children, including choking and the recovery position; manual airway opening, use of oxygen, oropharyngeal airways and bag-valve-mask ventilation; use of the emergency drugs; and practical AED training.
3. The GDC highly recommends at least 10 hours of medical emergencies CPD per CPD cycle, and at least 2 hours every year. Each registrant records this in their CPD log.
4. New staff are shown the kit location and their role on their first day, and attend training within [timescale].
5. **Scenario practice.** The whole team practises together in a simulated emergency every [3 months], rotating scenarios (e.g. collapse in reception, anaphylaxis after local anaesthetic, seizure, hypoglycaemia, choking, cardiac arrest in the implant surgery). Each scenario is timed and debriefed, and learning points are recorded.
6. Staff who transport oxygen are trained as in 5.6.
7. Useful resources: Resuscitation Council (UK) interactive CPR tool; NES *Medical Emergencies Revision* video; Walsall Healthcare NHS Trust *Medical Emergencies in the Dental Practice* poster.
