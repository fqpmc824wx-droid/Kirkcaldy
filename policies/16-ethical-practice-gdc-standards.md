## 1. Purpose

In the UK there is no formal code of ethics for dentistry. However, dental teams have a professional responsibility to account for their actions and a duty of care to apply ethical principles to all their work, to protect patients' interests and earn the trust of colleagues and the public. This policy sets out how Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic puts the General Dental Council (GDC) *Standards for the Dental Team* into practice, including professional registration, indemnity, honesty in private and implant treatment, and CPD.

## 2. Scope

All team members – GDC registrants (dentists, hygienists, therapists, dental nurses, technicians) and non-registrants (reception, management) – and all NHS and private services, including implant treatment, advertising and social media.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 The nine GDC principles in practice

The GDC Standards set out nine principles that all dental professionals must apply in their daily work. How the practice meets them:

| Principle | What it means here | Policy |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Put patients' interests first | Treatment is recommended only when in the patient's interest. NHS and private options are explained fairly. Patients are never pressured into private or implant treatment. Clinical need, not profit, guides advice. | This policy; 05 |
| 2. Communicate effectively with patients | Clear explanations, written treatment plans and estimates for every course of treatment, accessible information. | 08; 09 |
| 3. Obtain valid consent | Consent is voluntary, informed and given by a person with capacity; options, risks, benefits and costs are discussed and recorded. | 03-consent-policy.md |
| 4. Maintain and protect patients' information | Confidentiality, secure records, data protection. | 10; 11; 36 |
| 5. Have a clear and effective complaints procedure | Complaints are welcomed and handled promptly and fairly. | 01 |
| 6. Work with colleagues in a way that is in patients' best interests | Teamwork, appropriate delegation, referral when needed, respect. | 06 |
| 7. Maintain, develop and work within your professional knowledge and skills | CPD; working within competence and scope of practice; implant work only by appropriately trained clinicians. | Section 5.4–5.5 |
| 8. Raise concerns if patients are at risk | Speak up about risks, poor practice, abuse or neglect. | 07; 12 |
| 9. Make sure your personal behaviour maintains patients' confidence in you and the dental profession | Professional conduct in and outside work, including on social media. | Section 5.7 |

### 5.2 Professional registration

1. Every dentist, hygienist, therapist, dental nurse (and other registrant) must be on the GDC register before working and throughout their work at the practice. Trainee dental nurses must be on a GDC-recognised training programme [check current GDC requirements].
2. The Practice Manager checks the online GDC register at recruitment and after each annual renewal date (dentists: 31 December; dental care professionals: 31 July – check current dates) and records it.
3. Any registrant whose registration lapses must stop clinical work immediately until it is restored.
4. Registrants must tell the Practice Owner at once about any GDC investigation, conditions, or restrictions on their practice.
5. Dentists providing NHS care must be on the NHS Fife dental list before treating NHS patients.

### 5.3 Indemnity

1. Every GDC registrant must have appropriate indemnity or insurance cover for the work they do, in place before they start. [Check current GDC guidance on indemnity.]
2. Cover must suit the scope of work, including **implant surgery and restorative implant work**, sedation [if provided], and any private treatment. Clinicians must confirm with their provider that implant work is covered and at what level.
3. The Practice Manager keeps a copy or confirmation of each person's indemnity (provider, membership number, scope, renewal date) and checks it each year. Confirming current and adequate indemnity with defence organisations is a PSM-recommended risk-reduction measure.
4. Employed dental nurses and other employed staff may be covered by the practice's or employer's arrangements; this is confirmed in writing [practice decision].
5. The practice also holds: public liability insurance (covering staff assisting patients, see 09), employer's liability insurance (certificate displayed), and [practice/business insurance]. [Insurer, policy numbers, renewal dates.]
6. Clinicians contact their indemnity provider early for advice on adverse incidents, complaints that may lead to claims, requests for disclosure of information, refusal of subject access requests, and child or adult protection dilemmas.

### 5.4 CPD

1. Registrants complete verifiable CPD in line with the GDC enhanced CPD scheme, keep a personal development plan, and declare CPD to the GDC.
2. The GDC recommends CPD on **legal and ethical issues**, as well as on other topics including medical emergencies, disinfection and decontamination, radiography and radiation protection, safeguarding children and young people and vulnerable adults, complaints handling, oral cancer early detection, and raising concerns. [Check current GDC recommended topics.]
3. The practice provides team CPD at practice meetings and records attendance.
4. The Practice Manager keeps a CPD summary for each registrant and reviews it at appraisal.

### 5.5 Scope of practice and competence

1. Each registrant works within the GDC Scope of Practice for their registration and their own training and competence.
2. Implant treatment, CBCT prescribing and reporting, and other advanced treatments are only carried out by clinicians with appropriate training and experience, and records of that training are kept. [List clinicians and their implant training.]
3. Clinicians refer patients when treatment is beyond their competence (06-referrals-protocol.md).
4. Tasks are delegated only to people trained and competent to do them.

### 5.6 Honesty, fees and private treatment

1. Patients are told clearly whether treatment is NHS or private, and which options are available on the NHS.
2. Written treatment plans and cost estimates are given before treatment starts, for NHS and private treatment, and updated if the plan changes.
3. Fees and finance options for private treatment (including implants) are explained clearly; patients are given time to decide.
4. Advertising, the website and social media must be honest, accurate and not misleading; no guarantees of outcomes; before-and-after photos only with written consent (see 08-practice-information-and-advertising.md).
5. Conflicts of interest (for example financial links with laboratories or suppliers) are declared.

### 5.7 Personal and professional behaviour

1. Treat patients, colleagues and visitors with dignity and respect, without discrimination.
2. Be honest and act with integrity, including in records, claims and references.
3. On social media, do not discuss patients, post images of patients without consent, or post content that would damage confidence in the profession.
4. Tell the Practice Owner if you are charged with or convicted of an offence, or if your health may affect your fitness to practise.
5. Do not work under the influence of alcohol or drugs.
6. Be open and honest when things go wrong (02-duty-of-candour-procedure.md).

### 5.8 Raising concerns

Anyone who has a concern that patients are at risk – from a colleague's conduct, health, competence, the environment or systems – must raise it. See 07-raising-concerns-whistleblowing.md and 12-child-and-adult-protection.md. No one will suffer detriment for raising a genuine concern.
