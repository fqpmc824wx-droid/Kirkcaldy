## 1. Purpose

As a waste producer and waste handler, the practice has a legal **duty of care** to make sure all its waste is safely and legally managed from the point it is produced to its final disposal. This policy describes our local procedures for segregating, packaging, labelling, storing, uplifting and recording every type of waste, using the NHSScotland national colour-coded system. It also sets out how we minimise waste and increase recycling.

## 2. Scope

All waste produced at the practice, NHS and private, by all staff, self-employed clinicians, cleaners and contractors:
- **non-healthcare waste**: green stream (recyclates), black stream (general), waste electrical and electronic equipment (WEEE), confidential waste;
- **healthcare (special) waste**: orange, yellow and red streams, and gypsum.

In Scotland, all healthcare (clinical) waste is segregated and classified as **special waste** (called "hazardous waste" outside Scotland). Special waste is waste with one or more of the 15 hazardous properties categorised by SEPA, such as infectious, flammable or toxic.

The colour codes below are the NHSScotland national standard. Local variations may exist: the Health & Safety Lead checks them, and the containers and labels, with the **NHS Fife Waste Management Officer** [Name, contact] and gets the NHS Fife segregation flow chart.

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 General rules for all waste

1. **Segregate at source** – at the point where waste is made, into the correct container.
2. Have the right colour-coded containers for every stream in every area where that waste is produced. Containers and labels must comply with current Health Facilities Scotland guidance (confirm with NHS Fife).
3. Where containers are not pre-assembled, assemble them correctly before use and make sure lids are firmly secured.
4. **Never overfill**: bags no more than three-quarters full and no more than 4 kg; rigid containers not beyond the fill line and no more than 4 kg.
5. Make sure waste under our control cannot escape (lids on, bags sealed, bins locked).
6. Store healthcare and other special waste securely, away from public access: [location of internal store and lockable external bins].
7. Perform hand hygiene after handling waste.
8. Each uplift has the correct paperwork (section 5.10) and we keep it for the required period.
9. Reduce, reuse and recycle where safe (see 40-sustainability-policy.md). Check the waste classification on each product's Safety Data Sheet.

### 5.2 Summary – what goes where

| Stream | Container | Examples | Disposal route |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Green** (recyclates) | Recycling bags/boxes per local authority scheme | clean paper, cardboard, glass, cans, plastics | Fife Council or recycling contractor |
| **Black** (general) | Black/clear bag or labelled, secured box | uncontaminated paper towels and wipes, soiled packaging, human hygiene waste, sanitary towels, nappies | Fife Council or private contractor |
| **Orange** (low-risk infectious, special) | Orange bag with black biohazard symbol, in a bag holder | disposable PPE, dressings, swabs, cotton wool rolls, contaminated wipes, items contaminated with body fluids or visibly with blood, unrecognisable tissue | NHS Fife scheme – shredded and heat treated |
| **Yellow** (high-risk infectious, special) | 1 or 5 litre rigid yellow container (yellow, red or blue lid as NHS Fife advises) | needles, syringes, scalpel blades, burs, endo files, matrix bands, used/part-used LA cartridges, pharmaceuticals and vials, contaminated metal parts, broken instruments, broken glass, highly infectious waste, blood and contaminated liquids, infectious gypsum models | NHS Fife scheme – incinerated at 850°C minimum |
| **Red** (special, recovery) | Separate rigid red-lidded containers, one per type | amalgam waste; amalgam capsules; extracted teeth (with or without amalgam); aspirator filters; lead foils; radiographic chemicals | NHS Fife scheme – specialist recovery |
| **Gypsum** (uncontaminated) | Separate labelled container | clean plaster models and casts | Recycling or specifically designed landfill |
| **WEEE** | Kept aside, not in general waste | old computers, batteries, small electrical items | Authorised contractor |
| **Confidential** | Locked confidential waste bin / cross-cut shredder | paper with patient or staff data | Secure shredding contractor with certificate |

### 5.3 Green stream (recyclates)

1. The Waste (Scotland) Regulations 2012 require segregation of recyclates: mixed glass, paper and cardboard, plastics, food tins and drink cans, scrap metal and electrical items.
2. Check with Fife Council which colour-coded system is used ("Recycle for Scotland" or the NHS alternative) and label bins to match.
3. Keep uncontaminated recyclates in bags or boxes to suit the scheme, separate from other waste.

### 5.4 Black stream (general waste)

1. Black stream is what remains after recyclates are removed.
2. Use black bags or labelled, secured cardboard boxes. Fill no more than three-quarters and no more than 4 kg. Seal securely.
3. **Do not** use a healthcare (special) waste ratchet tag on a black bag.
4. Human hygiene waste, sanitary towels and nappies must not be compacted.
5. No healthcare waste, sharps, amalgam, batteries or confidential paper in black bags.

### 5.5 Orange stream (low-risk healthcare waste)

1. Use orange bags with the black biohazard symbol, in a bag holder sited to make segregation easy and spills visible.
2. If a holder has a front cover or foot-operated lid, clean it to a strict schedule because spills may not be seen. Never use orange bags as liners in domestic pedal bins.
3. Make liquids leak-proof using a self-setting compound.
4. Fill no more than three-quarters, to the fill line, maximum 4 kg. Do not compress.
5. **Seal** by twisting and folding the neck into a "swan neck" and fitting a ratchet clinical waste tag tightly. Once sealed, never re-open.
6. Label each sealed bag to identify the source (practice name and address / tag number).
7. Place in the colour-coded, labelled, lockable secondary bin (wheelie bin) in the secure store.
8. Collection interval is no longer than **1 week**. Our collection day: [day].

### 5.6 Yellow stream (high-risk healthcare waste)

1. Use 1 or 5 litre rigid yellow containers, labelled as dental waste, kept close to the point of use (in each surgery and the LDU), out of reach of patients and children.
2. Lid colour (yellow, red, or blue for pharmaceutical products) as advised by NHS Fife: [confirm].
3. All items in the yellow list (section 5.2) can go in the same container. Small amounts of blood/contaminated liquid and broken glass may also go here.
4. Make liquids leak-proof with a self-setting compound.
5. Do not fill beyond the fill line or above 4 kg.
6. Close with the permanent closure before collection. Containers are open for **no longer than 3 months** – write the date opened on the label.
7. Label the container with the source (practice name and address).
8. See also 29-occupational-exposure-and-sharps.md for safe sharps handling.

### 5.7 Red stream (specialist recovery)

Each type goes in its **own** container because they go to different treatment facilities. Containers are rigid, red-lidded (red or white body), labelled with the type of waste.

| Waste | Container | Label |
|---|---|---|
| Amalgam waste (with mercury-absorbing foam / suppressant) | 0.5, 1 or 5 litre rigid screw-top | "Amalgam Waste" |
| Used amalgam capsules (suppressant) | 0.5, 1 or 5 litre rigid screw-top | "Amalgam Waste – Capsules" |
| Extracted teeth, with or without amalgam (suppressant) | 0.5, 1 or 5 litre rigid screw-top | "Teeth with Amalgam and Infectious Material" |
| Filters from dental aspirating systems | Rigid red-lidded container | Filters |
| Lead foils from X-ray film packets (if film is used) – recycled | 5 litre rigid screw-top (or 5 litre flip-top in processing room) | "Lead Foils" |
| Radiographic chemicals – spent developer and fixer (if used), one container per chemical | 10 litre rigid opaque, red screw-top | Name of chemical |

1. Do not fill beyond the fill line or above 4 kg. Once sealed, label with the source.
2. An **amalgam separator** meeting BS EN ISO 11143 is fitted to the aspirating system. Collected amalgam from it goes in the Red stream. Separator: [make/model, service schedule].
3. Extracted teeth given to patients on request: [practice decision – follow current guidance].
4. For EWC codes for dental Red stream waste, see SHTN 3 Part D.

### 5.8 Gypsum

1. Gypsum must not go to normal landfill (it can produce hydrogen sulphide gas).
2. Store clean, uncontaminated gypsum (models, casts) in a labelled container and send for recycling or to specially designed landfill. Contractor: [Company] – this may need a separate contractor.
3. Study models and casts contaminated with potentially infectious material go in the **yellow** stream (incineration destroys the gypsum).

### 5.9 WEEE and confidential waste

1. WEEE is often non-clinical special waste (e.g. heavy metal batteries) and must not go in general waste. Establish whether the practice or the producer is responsible (depends on purchase before or after 13 August 2005). If the practice is responsible, use an authorised contractor and follow SEPA guidance, with a waste transfer note, or a consignment note if it is special waste.
2. Computers and devices that held patient data are wiped or destroyed securely with a certificate (10-data-protection-confidentiality-information-security.md).
3. Paper containing personal data is confidential waste under UK GDPR and Caldicott principles: put it in the locked confidential bin, never in recycling. Records are only destroyed when their retention period ends (36-clinical-record-keeping-and-retention.md).

### 5.10 Uplift, carriers and paperwork

1. **Healthcare (special) waste** (orange, yellow, red) is collected by **NHS Fife under the PCA(D)5 scheme** [confirm arrangements with the NHS Fife Waste Management Officer]. If any stream is not covered, we use a registered waste management contractor licensed for that category, under contract [Company, licence no.].
2. **Black and green stream**: Fife Council or a private waste/recycling contractor [Company].
3. **Waste transfer notes (WTN)** for black, green and non-special WEEE: one per uplift with the correct EWC code and tonnage, or an annual "blanket" WTN (there is usually a charge).
4. **SEPA special waste consignment notes (SWCN) and carrier schedules** for every uplift of orange, yellow and red stream waste and special-waste WEEE, confirming the number of containers, EWC code and tonnage.
5. The Health & Safety Lead checks each note is complete and correct before signing, and files it.
6. **Retention:** waste transfer notes at least **2 years**; consignment notes and carrier schedules at least **3 years**.

### 5.11 Pre-acceptance audit (PAA)

1. SEPA guidance requires dental practices to complete a **pre-acceptance audit**. The NHSScotland healthcare waste contractor (Tradebe) may not collect healthcare waste without a PAA in place.
2. The Health & Safety Lead completes the PAA **before the first collection** (before opening), with advice from the NHS Fife Waste Management Officer, using the SEPA *Guidance for the storage and treatment of healthcare waste* criteria and templates.
3. Repeat the PAA **every 2 years**, or as required by NHS Fife. Next due: [date].
4. Keep the PAA in the waste file.

### 5.12 Segregation audit

Producers must monitor and evaluate segregation regularly. The Health & Safety Lead carries out a waste segregation audit [every 6 months – practice decision], checking bins in each area for mis-segregation, overfilling, labelling, storage and paperwork. Results and actions are reported at the practice meeting.

### 5.13 Spills and incidents

Spills of healthcare waste are dealt with using the spill kit and the IPC policy (24-infection-prevention-and-control-policy.md); mercury spills under 18-coshh-policy.md. Sharps injuries follow 29-occupational-exposure-and-sharps.md. Incidents are recorded and investigated.
