## 1. Purpose

Impressions, appliances, equipment and specimens that leave the practice can carry blood, saliva and microorganisms. This policy makes sure that:

- impressions and appliances are cleaned and disinfected before they go to the dental laboratory, and the laboratory is told;
- items returned from the laboratory are disinfected before they go into a patient's mouth;
- instruments and equipment are decontaminated before repair (detail in 26-decontamination-of-reusable-instruments.md, 5.12);
- biopsy and pathology specimens are packaged and labelled so the specimen is protected and no one handling the package is put at risk.

## 2. Scope

All clinicians and dental nurses who take impressions, handle appliances (dentures, orthodontic appliances, splints, crowns, bridges, implant components and surgical guides), send items for repair, or take biopsies; and reception staff who arrange courier or postal collection.

## 3. Legislation and guidance

As cited in the PSM:

- **Control of Substances Hazardous to Health (COSHH) Regulations 2002** – the dentist is responsible for cleaning and disinfecting impressions and appliances before sending them to the laboratory, and has a statutory duty to render instruments and equipment safe before repair
- **Medicines and Healthcare products Regulatory Agency (MHRA)** requirements – inform the laboratory that items have been disinfected
- **UN3373, Packing Instruction P650** – packaging and transport of patient samples (Biological Substance, Category B)
- World Health Organization – Guidance on regulations for the transport of infectious substances 2023–2024

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Impressions and appliances – before dispatch

1. **Immediately** after removing an impression or appliance from the mouth, **rinse it under cold running water** to remove saliva, blood and debris. Keep rinsing until it is **visibly clean**. Wear gloves, apron and eye protection; avoid splashing.
2. **Disinfect by immersion** in a solution suitable for this purpose, following the manufacturer's instructions for concentration and contact time. Product in use: [product name], contact time [ ] minutes.
3. **Check compatibility.** Some materials (e.g. some alginates and polyethers) are dimensionally unstable when immersed. Consult the impression material manufacturer before use and record the method for each material in the Decontamination Methods register.
4. If a **spray disinfectant** is used, place the impression in a **plastic bag before spraying**, to avoid inhalation, and leave it **sealed in the bag** for the time recommended by the manufacturer.
5. Rinse again in water after disinfection if the product instructions say so, to remove residual disinfectant.
6. Place in a sealed bag inside a rigid box or the laboratory's container, with the laboratory prescription. Keep paperwork separate from the item (e.g. in an outer pocket) so it is not contaminated.
7. Label the package with the disinfection statement (5.2).
8. Record the dispatch in the laboratory log: date, patient identifier, item, laboratory, disinfected (Y), staff initials.

### 5.2 Telling the laboratory

1. To comply with MHRA requirements, the practice **informs the dental laboratory that each impression or appliance has been disinfected**.
2. The method is agreed with each laboratory: [practice decision – a label or standard form sent with each item, or a **blanket statement** using the PSM Decontamination Agreement template].
3. The practice obtains a **copy of each laboratory's policy for receiving and dispatching items** and checks that it is compatible with our procedures. Copies are kept in the laboratory file.

### 5.3 Items returned from the laboratory

1. Items returned from the laboratory are treated as **not disinfected** unless the laboratory's documentation confirms otherwise.
2. Before trying in or fitting, the dental nurse rinses and disinfects the item according to the material manufacturer's and laboratory's instructions [practice decision].
3. Items that will be sterilized (e.g. some implant components or surgical guides) are processed as the manufacturer specifies before use (see 26-decontamination-of-reusable-instruments.md).
4. After try-in, items going back to the laboratory are rinsed and disinfected again as in 5.1.

### 5.4 Instruments and equipment sent for repair or servicing

1. Decontaminate items, including handpieces, using the practice's **usual method** before sending them for servicing or repair.
2. For equipment that **cannot be sterilized**, clean and disinfect it according to the manufacturer's instructions.
3. **Tell the recipient** how the item has been decontaminated, using the PSM Decontamination Agreement template or a label with each item.
4. Record the item, date, supplier and decontamination method in the equipment repair log.

### 5.5 Biopsy and pathology specimens

All biopsy/pathology specimens sent for diagnosis must be **properly packaged and labelled**, so the specimen is protected and there is no risk of infection to anyone handling the package. Packaging must be **triple-layered** and conform to **UN3373, Packing Instruction P650**.

**Taking and labelling**

1. The clinician places the specimen in the container and fixative provided by the pathology laboratory [NHS Fife pathology / private laboratory – confirm which service and its requirements]. Screw the lid on firmly.
2. Label the primary container with the patient's full name, date of birth, CHI number (if available), specimen site, date and time taken.
3. Complete the laboratory request form in full, including clinical details and the clinician's name and contact details.

**Packaging – three layers**

4. **Primary packaging**: a **leak-proof, sealed container** (e.g. a plastic sample pot), wrapped in **enough absorbent material** to absorb all the contents if it leaks, and placed securely into the secondary packaging so the contents cannot be damaged or leak.
5. **Secondary packaging**: a **leak-proof sealed container** (e.g. a plastic specimen bag or box). Place it into the outer packaging, secured with suitable **cushioning** material.
6. **Paperwork** goes **between the secondary and outer packaging** (not in with the specimen) and includes:
   - date;
   - sender (practice details: Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic, 23–25 Tolbooth Street, Kirkcaldy KY1 1RW) and receiver details;
   - **emergency contact**: name and telephone number of the responsible person [Name, phone];
   - description of the contents.
7. **Outer packaging**: labelled **"BIOLOGICAL SUBSTANCE, CATEGORY B"** and carrying the **UN3373 diamond-shaped symbol**.
8. **Either the secondary or the outer packaging must be rigid.** Where specimens are collected by a vehicle fitted with rigid boxes, the box may count as the rigid outer packaging.

**Sending**

9. Send by [practice decision: NHS Fife specimen collection / courier / Royal Mail service compliant with UN3373]. Specimens awaiting collection are kept in [designated location], away from food and the public.
10. Record in the specimen log: date, patient identifier, specimen type, laboratory, method of dispatch, staff initials. Note the date the result is expected.
11. The clinician checks the result arrives, records it in Dentally and acts on it. Missing results are chased after [ ] days. Urgent suspected cancer referrals follow the referrals protocol (see 06-referrals-protocol.md).

### 5.6 Spills and damaged packages

If a specimen or impression container leaks or breaks, deal with it as a body fluid spill (see 27-cleaning-the-care-environment.md) and, if a specimen is lost, tell the clinician at once so the patient can be informed.

- Dental nurses and clinicians are trained at induction in impression and appliance disinfection, use of spray disinfectant in a bag, and labelling.
- Staff who package specimens are trained in UN3373 / P650 triple packaging and labelling.
- Training is refreshed [annually] and when products or laboratories change.
