## 1. Purpose

Major incidents that affect the running of a dental practice are rare. When they happen, a plan prepared in advance helps the team respond calmly and keep the impact on patients as small as possible. This plan sets out:

- who decides that the plan is activated and manages recovery;
- what to do for each likely incident;
- the resources, documents and contacts needed, and where they are kept.

## 2. Scope

This plan covers incidents that stop or seriously disrupt normal working at the practice, including:

- loss of, or no access to, the premises (fire, flood, structural damage, police cordon, bomb threat);
- utility failure: electricity, water, gas/heating, telephones, internet;
- IT failure, including Dentally (cloud), local workstations, imaging, card payment, cyber attack;
- dental equipment failure (compressor, suction, dental chairs, X-ray);
- decontamination failure (washer-disinfector, steriliser, LDU water supply);
- loss of staff (illness, outbreak, severe weather, sudden departure);
- severe weather;
- contamination of the workplace (e.g. mercury spill, sewage, infectious disease).

It applies to all staff. Day-to-day risks are managed through 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md.

## 3. Legislation and guidance

The PSM advises (it does not set a legal requirement) that every practice has a continuity plan, prepared with the PSM *Continuity Planning Tool* template. Related duties come from other PSM topics, for example data protection and back-ups (Data Protection Act 2018), decontamination (NHS Scotland guidance used in the IPC policies), fire safety and health and safety legislation, and NHS Fife arrangements for NHS patients' urgent care.

## 4. Responsibilities

**Continuity Lead – Practice Manager [Name]** (deputy: Practice Owner [Nas]; second deputy: [Name]) – assesses the impact of an incident, decides whether to activate this plan, leads the response and manages recovery of the service. Keeps the plan and contacts up to date, and keeps the emergency box stocked.

**Practice Owner / Principal Dentist [Nas]** – clinical decisions (which treatments continue, safe to treat or not), contact with NHS Fife, insurers and indemnity provider, and decisions on temporary premises.

**Policy leads** – act on incidents in their area: IPC/Decontamination Lead (decontamination failure), Fire Safety Lead (fire), RPS (X-ray), Data Protection Lead (IT and data incidents), Health & Safety Lead (premises).

**Reception staff** – patient communication: phoning or messaging patients, rebooking, signposting.

**All staff** – know this plan exists, know where it is, and know who to contact.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Activation

1. Any staff member who discovers a major incident makes people safe first (evacuate, call 999 if needed) and then contacts the Continuity Lead (or deputy) – see the key contacts table.
2. The Continuity Lead assesses: Is anyone hurt? Is the building safe? Which functions are affected? How long is it likely to last?
3. The Continuity Lead decides whether to activate the plan, and records the decision and time in the incident log.
4. The Continuity Lead allocates actions using the relevant section below.
5. A short team update is given [at the start of each day / every few hours] until normal service resumes.
6. After recovery, an SEA is carried out and this plan is updated (see 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md).

### 5.2 Priorities

1. Safety of patients, staff and visitors.
2. Patients mid-treatment (e.g. under local anaesthetic, mid-implant surgery or with temporary restorations) are made safe and given follow-up arrangements.
3. Access to urgent care for patients in pain, swelling, trauma or bleeding – NHS patients are signposted to the Fife Dental Advice Line 01592 226555 (Mon–Fri 8.30am–5pm) or NHS 24 on 111 out of hours if the practice cannot see them.
4. Protection of patient records and data.
5. Restore normal service.

### 5.3 Loss of premises (fire, flood, structural damage, no access)

1. Evacuate following 19-fire-safety-policy.md. Take the emergency box and the appointment list if safe to do so. Do not re-enter until the fire service, police or building surveyor allows.
2. Continuity Lead informs the Practice Owner, landlord/building insurer [Name], practice insurer, and NHS Fife primary care dental team.
3. Reception staff (working remotely, logging into Dentally from another device) contact patients booked for [the next 2 days], cancel or move appointments, and post a notice on the website and phone message.
4. Urgent patients are seen at the temporary arrangement: [practice decision – reciprocal arrangement with [nearby practice] / NHS Fife urgent care]. Agreement with [practice] dated [date].
5. Secure the premises (boarding, alarm, locksmith). Remove or secure medical oxygen, controlled drugs and any paper records when safe.
6. Notify Scottish Fire and Rescue Service of the oxygen cylinder store location if not already known.
7. Practice Owner decides on temporary premises and timescales; NHS Fife is kept informed.
8. Before re-opening: building, electrical, water and gas safety checks; legionella flush and checks; decontamination equipment revalidated; X-ray equipment checked by the RPA/engineer if affected; emergency drugs and equipment checked.

### 5.4 Power failure

1. Stop treatment safely. Use torches from the emergency box. Patients mid-treatment are made safe (temporary dressing, suture, etc.).
2. Check if the fault is in the building (trip switch, fuses) or the supply. Call the electricity network operator **SP Energy Networks – 105** for power cuts.
3. Dentally is cloud-based: while power is out, staff access it from charged laptops/phones on mobile data [if available] to see appointments and contact patients.
4. Decontamination: do not process instruments; any load interrupted mid-cycle is treated as not sterilised and reprocessed. Keep enough sterile instrument packs for [number] urgent appointments.
5. Fridge stock (e.g. any drugs or materials needing refrigeration): keep door closed; check temperature when power returns and discard stock outside range.
6. If the outage is longer than [1 hour], cancel and rebook sessions; signpost urgent patients.
7. On restoration: check equipment restarts correctly; run steriliser and washer-disinfector daily tests before use; check compressor and suction.

### 5.5 Water failure (or water contamination)

1. Without water the practice cannot safely provide hand hygiene, decontamination or toilet facilities. Stop aerosol-generating and wet-handpiece treatment.
2. Contact **Scottish Water – 0800 0778 778** to check for supply problems or boil notices; contact the plumber/landlord for internal faults.
3. Hand hygiene: use alcohol-based hand rub where hands are not visibly soiled. [Practice decision – stock of bottled water for emergency hand washing.]
4. Dental unit waterlines: use the bottled water system [if fitted]. Follow 28-legionella-and-dental-unit-waterlines.md before reuse after any interruption (flush lines).
5. Decontamination: stop processing if the RO/distilled water supply or LDU sinks cannot be used. Use the stock of sterile packs for urgent care.
6. If toilets are out of use for more than [a few hours], close the practice to patients.
7. After restoration or a boil notice: flush all outlets and waterlines; check with the legionella risk assessment; record in the water log.

### 5.6 IT failure, including Dentally

**Dentally (cloud) unavailable, or internet down**

1. Check whether the problem is local (practice internet, router, workstation) or Dentally-wide (check the Dentally status page / support [contact]).
2. Switch to the backup internet connection [practice decision – 4G/5G router or phone hotspot].
3. If Dentally still cannot be reached:
   - Use the **daily printed (or downloaded) appointment list** and patient contact list for the next [2] days, which reception prints/exports each evening [practice decision] and keeps securely.
   - Clinicians do not treat patients whose medical history cannot be checked, except in an emergency – take a paper medical history using the PSM/SDCEP Medical History form.
   - Record treatment on paper (ink, signed and dated – see 36-clinical-record-keeping-and-retention.md) and enter it into Dentally as soon as the system is back. Shred the paper notes after scanning [or keep, per policy].
   - Take payments using [standalone card machine on mobile data / record and take payment later].
4. Imaging: if the imaging software or server is down, do not take radiographs unless they can be viewed and evaluated. (An exposure that will not be clinically evaluated is not justified – see 35-radiation-protection-policy.md.)

**Workstation or server failure**

1. Move to another workstation. Call the IT provider [Name, phone].
2. Restore local data from the latest verified back-up (see the back-up protocol in 36-clinical-record-keeping-and-retention.md).

**Cyber attack or suspected data breach** (ransomware, phishing, lost device)

1. Disconnect affected devices from the network; do not switch them off unless the IT provider advises.
2. Tell the Data Protection Lead immediately. Follow the data breach procedure in 10-data-protection-confidentiality-information-security.md (ICO notification within 72 hours if reportable).
3. Change passwords for Dentally and email from a clean device.
4. Report to Police Scotland (101) and [Action Fraud / Scottish cyber reporting – check current guidance].

### 5.7 Loss of staff

1. If one person is absent, use the multi-skilled team and deputies named in each policy. The Practice Manager checks that at least two trained people are present whenever treatment is planned (see 34-medical-emergencies-policy.md). If not, treatment does not go ahead.
2. [Practice decision – a dental nurse or other appropriately trained team member is present for all clinical treatment, in line with GDC standards on chairside support.] If none is available, cancel and rebook.
3. Agency and locum contacts: [agency name, phone]; [locum dentist list]. Check GDC registration, indemnity, PVG and immunisation before any locum starts (see 13-pvg-scheme-and-safe-recruitment.md and 30-health-clearance-and-immunisation.md).
4. Key roles must not depend on one person: at least [two] people can run decontamination daily tests, open and close the building, use Dentally admin functions, restore back-ups and run the weekly emergency drugs check.
5. Long-term absence of the Practice Owner / Principal Dentist: [practice decision – named associate or locum arrangement; inform NHS Fife about patient continuity and the dental list; indemnity provider].
6. Infectious disease outbreak: follow current NHS Fife / Public Health Scotland guidance.

### 5.8 Decontamination failure

1. If a washer-disinfector, steriliser, ultrasonic bath or the LDU fails a daily, weekly or periodic test, or breaks down: **stop using it immediately**, label it "Do not use", and tell the IPC / Decontamination Lead.
2. Quarantine any instruments processed since the last satisfactory test. Reprocess them once equipment is passed as fit, or treat as not sterilised.
3. Call the service engineer [company, phone, contract number].
4. Continue treatment only with the stock of sterile packs available and single-use instruments. When stock runs low, reduce to urgent care only, then stop.
5. [Practice decision – reciprocal agreement with [nearby practice] for temporary instrument reprocessing, or hire/loan steriliser from [supplier]]. Any instruments processed elsewhere must be traceable.
6. Before re-use, the engineer certifies the repair, and the daily/weekly tests are repeated and recorded. Revalidation is arranged if required.
7. Record the incident and carry out SEA if patients may have been affected. See 26-decontamination-of-reusable-instruments.md.

### 5.9 Dental equipment failure

| Failure | Immediate action |
|---|---|
| Compressor | Stop handpiece and air/water use; call engineer; check pressure vessel inspection records |
| Suction | Stop aerosol-generating procedures; use portable suction for emergencies only |
| Dental chair | Move patient to another surgery |
| X-ray unit | Follow Local Rules contingency plan; take out of use; tell RPS; call engineer |
| Emergency drugs or AED | Replace at once; if not possible, Practice Owner decides whether treatment can continue safely |

### 5.10 Severe weather

1. The Continuity Lead checks weather warnings the evening before and decides by [time] whether to open.
2. Staff who cannot travel safely tell the Practice Manager by [time].
3. Patients are contacted by text/phone if sessions are cancelled. The website and phone message are updated.
4. Paths and entrance are gritted where safe (see 17-health-and-safety-policy.md).

### 5.11 Emergency box

An emergency box is kept at [location inside practice] with a copy at [off-site location, e.g. Practice Owner's home]. It contains:

- this plan and other emergency plans (fire, medical emergencies);
- key contacts table (printed) and staff contact list;
- blank paper medical history and treatment record forms; blank appointment sheets;
- torches and spare batteries; a battery or wind-up radio;
- replacement fuses;
- notepads and pens;
- materials for improvised signage (laminated sheets, waterproof markers, Blu Tack);
- a space blanket;
- a charged mobile phone and charger / power bank;
- [keys: spare building keys and alarm code – kept securely, not in an unlocked box].

The Continuity Lead checks the box every [6 months] and after use.

## 6. Records and monitoring

1. Decisions and actions during an incident are recorded in the incident log by the Continuity Lead.
2. After every activation: SEA, update this plan and the risk register.
3. The plan and the key contacts table are reviewed **every 12 months**, and whenever staff, suppliers or systems change.
4. A tabletop test of one scenario (e.g. Dentally outage, decontamination failure) is run [annually] with the team and recorded.

## 7. Training

1. All staff are shown this plan and the emergency box at induction.
2. The team discusses one scenario at a team meeting [annually].
3. At least [two] staff can carry out each key function listed in 5.7.4.
4. Reception staff are trained to access Dentally remotely and to use the paper fallback.
