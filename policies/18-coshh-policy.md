## 1. Purpose

This policy explains how the practice identifies, assesses and controls substances that could harm the health of staff, patients and others. It includes our procedures for the safe handling of mercury and dental amalgam.

## 2. Scope

It applies to everyone who uses, stores, orders or disposes of hazardous substances at the practice, and protects anyone who could be exposed: staff, patients, visitors, cleaners, engineers who service our equipment, and dental laboratory staff who handle our impressions and appliances.

Hazardous substances can be solids, liquids, gases, fumes, dust or biological agents. Exposure can be by swallowing, breathing in, skin puncture, or absorption through the skin or eyes.

COSHH covers:
- substances classed as dangerous under CHIP 4 and GB CLP (they come with a Safety Data Sheet);
- substances with a workplace exposure limit (WEL), e.g. nitrous oxide, mercury, detergent powder;
- biological agents (four hazard groups);
- dust in substantial quantities.

COSHH does **not** cover lead, asbestos or radioactive substances, which have their own regulations.

In this practice, substances needing assessment include: potentially infected blood and body fluids; cleaning and decontamination chemicals; disinfectants (surfaces and impressions); mercury and amalgam; natural rubber latex; acid etch, bonding agents, resins and monomers; bleaching/whitening agents; local anaesthetics and medicines; oxygen; any nitrous oxide used for sedation; X-ray processing chemicals (if film is used); gypsum dust; and products for cosmetic procedures (e.g. botulinum toxin), if offered.

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 Identify substances

1. The Health & Safety Lead walks round the practice (surgeries, LDU, store, cleaning cupboard, staff kitchen, X-ray area) and lists every substance that could harm health in the **COSHH register**.
2. For each product, collect the Safety Data Sheet (SDS/MSDS), read the packaging, and check the manufacturer's website or contact them if needed. The SDS should also show how the waste must be classified (see 22-waste-management-policy.md).
3. **No new product is used until** it has an SDS and a COSHH assessment, or has been added to an existing one.

### 5.2 Assess the risk (in writing)

We use the PSM COSHH Risk Assessment template (or the Healthy Working Lives form). For each substance or group, record:

1. How it is harmful (from the SDS and GB CLP hazard pictograms).
2. Who could be exposed, which tasks or processes lead to exposure, and how (inhaled, skin, eyes, swallowed, puncture).
3. Controls already in place, and extra controls that could remove or reduce the risk.
4. What to do after exposure (first aid, spillage, who to tell).
5. Whether health surveillance is needed.
6. Areas of concern – for example, check the accident book for previous incidents.

Keeping a copy of the SDS is **not** a COSHH assessment on its own. The assessment and SDS are kept together in a COSHH folder in the area where the substance is stored, so they can be found fast in an emergency: [locations – e.g. LDU, store, cleaning cupboard].

### 5.3 Control the risk

Use this order:
1. **Eliminate** the substance if it is not needed.
2. **Substitute** with a less hazardous product (e.g. powder-free gloves, encapsulated amalgam, digital radiography instead of processing chemicals).
3. **Engineering controls** – ventilation, aspiration, closed systems, automated instrument cleaning.
4. **Safe systems of work** – correct dilution, labelled containers, small quantities decanted, lids on.
5. **PPE** as the last line of defence (23-personal-protective-equipment-policy.md).

Staff are involved in choosing control measures so they fit the way the work is actually done. Controls are monitored and kept in good working order (e.g. aspirator and ventilation servicing).

### 5.4 Storage, labelling and spills

1. Store hazardous substances in their original labelled containers, in a cool, secure cupboard away from patients and children, per the SDS.
2. Any decanted product is in a container labelled with the product name and hazard.
3. Flammable products are kept in small quantities away from ignition sources (19-fire-safety-policy.md).
4. Chemical spillage: follow the SDS. Wear the PPE listed, contain and absorb, ventilate, dispose of as the SDS states, and record the incident.
5. Blood and body fluid spills: follow the IPC policy (24-infection-prevention-and-control-policy.md).

### 5.5 Emergencies

The COSHH assessment states the emergency action for each substance. Eye wash [location] and first aid kit [location] are available (20-first-aid-policy.md). For a serious exposure, call 999 and take the SDS with the casualty. Record in the accident book and consider RIDDOR (17-health-and-safety-policy.md).

### 5.6 Health surveillance

Where the assessment shows it is needed – for example skin checks for staff doing wet work or using gloves all day – the Health & Safety Lead arranges health surveillance [occupational health provider: Name]. See 21-latex-allergy-policy.md for skin checks and contact dermatitis.

### 5.7 Mercury and amalgam handling

Mercury is extremely toxic. It can be breathed in or absorbed through the skin and mucous membranes.

1. **COSHH assessment.** A specific COSHH assessment for mercury and amalgam is in the COSHH folder.
2. **Premises.** The Health & Safety Lead checks that:
   - there is good positive ventilation in the surgeries;
   - floor coverings are impervious with sealed joints;
   - there is no carpet near areas where amalgam is handled;
   - aspirators vent externally where possible. If not, a bacterial filter with activated carbon (to capture mercury vapour) is fitted in the exhaust line and changed per the manufacturer's instructions. Arrangement here: [external vent / carbon filter – details].
3. **Encapsulated amalgam only.** We use pre-dosed encapsulated amalgam (required by Regulation (EU) 2017/852). Bulk mercury is never kept.
4. **Amalgamator.** Place it on a tray lined with aluminium foil. Keep the mixing chamber cover closed during use. Check it regularly [monthly – practice decision] for signs of mercury contamination. If contaminated, remove it from service and send it for specialist decontamination or replace it.
5. **Handling.** Wear gloves, mask and eye protection. Use high-volume aspiration and water spray when placing, polishing or removing amalgam. Do not handle amalgam with bare hands.
6. **Mercury spillage kit.** A kit is kept at [location] and checked [monthly]. In a spill: keep people away, do not use a vacuum cleaner, follow the instructions on the kit, put waste in the correct Red stream container, ventilate, and record the incident.
7. **Waste.** Amalgam waste, used capsules, teeth with amalgam and aspirator filters go in separate labelled Red stream containers with suppressant. An amalgam separator (BS EN ISO 11143) is fitted. See 22-waste-management-policy.md.
8. **Training.** Everyone who uses amalgam gets in-house training in safe handling and spill procedure before they start.

### 5.8 Specific substances

- **Biological agents / blood and saliva:** see 24-infection-prevention-and-control-policy.md and 29-occupational-exposure-and-sharps.md.

- **Nitrous oxide (if used for sedation):** has a WEL; scavenging and room ventilation must be assessed [practice decision – applies only if inhalation sedation is offered].
- **Cosmetic products (e.g. botulinum toxin), if offered:** COSHH assessment before first use.

## 6. Records and monitoring

- COSHH register and assessments, with SDSs: COSHH folders at point of storage and master copy in the H&S file.
- Reviewed at least annually [practice decision], when a new product is introduced, after an incident, or if staff report a problem.
- Amalgamator contamination checks, spillage kit checks and aspirator filter changes: recorded in the equipment log.
- Exposure incidents: accident book; investigated through significant event analysis.
- Health surveillance records: kept confidentially by the Health & Safety Lead [and occupational health provider].

## 7. Training

- All staff at induction: what COSHH is, where the folders are, reading GB CLP pictograms and SDSs, spillage and emergency action.
- Clinical staff: mercury and amalgam handling and spill kit use, before first handling amalgam.
- Refresher when products change and [annually – practice decision] at a practice meeting.
- Training is recorded in staff files.
