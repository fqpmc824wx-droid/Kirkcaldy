## 1. Purpose

Decontamination of reusable instruments is a multistage process: cleaning, thermal disinfection, inspection and sterilization, followed by safe storage. It needs suitable facilities, the correct equipment (validated and tested) and trained staff.

This policy sets out the practice's arrangements for decontamination so that national standards are met every time, and so that we can show this to NHS Fife at the Combined Practice Inspection. It follows the PSM's Managing Decontamination in Dental Practice (MDDP) approach: written policy and procedures, records that show each step worked, and regular audit and review.

## 2. Scope

- All reusable dental instruments and devices, including handpieces, implant surgical kits and drivers, and any other reusable items used on patients.
- All decontamination equipment: [washer-disinfector(s)], [ultrasonic cleaner], [sterilizer(s) – type N / type B], [handpiece maintenance unit], [heat sealer, if used].
- The local decontamination unit (LDU), the surgeries and the movement of instruments between them.
- All staff who handle, process, store or supervise decontamination of instruments.
- Decontamination of items before they are sent for repair or servicing.

Single-use items (marked with the single-use symbol) are never reprocessed (see 32-procurement-of-ipc-items.md).

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Facilities

1. Instruments are decontaminated in a dedicated **LDU** [location – e.g. ground floor room], separate from the surgeries, designed to GUID 5005 and SHPN 13 Part 2. [Practice decision: confirm the LDU design with Health Facilities Scotland's advisory service before fit-out.]
2. The LDU has a clear one-way **dirty-to-clean flow**: receiving/set-down (dirty) → washing/cleaning → inspection → sterilization → packaging/clean set-down. Zones are labelled.
3. The LDU has a dedicated clinical hand wash basin, separate from the instrument sink(s) and rinse sink.
4. Surfaces are smooth, impervious, intact and easy to clean. Floors are sealed with coved edges.
5. Ventilation in the LDU is [practice decision: mechanical extract/supply as specified by the designer – check GUID 5005].
6. The LDU is used only for decontamination. No food, drink, personal items or patient records are allowed.

1. Equipment is purchased through suppliers on the **NP 143 national framework** (SHTM 01-05 Part A) where possible (see 32-procurement-of-ipc-items.md).
2. All equipment is **installed, commissioned and validated** by a competent person before first use, and revalidated at the intervals in SHTM 01-05 and after major repair or relocation.
3. Each sterilizer has a **Written Scheme of Examination** from a Competent Person (Pressure Systems), with examination at the intervals set in the scheme (typically at least every 14 months for sterilizers). Third party liability insurance for pressure vessels is in place [insurer, policy no.].
4. Equipment is maintained according to the manufacturer's instructions.

### 5.3 Decontamination methods register

The IPC Lead completes the PSM **Decontamination Methods for Instruments Used in this Practice** template, listing each category of instrument (e.g. hand instruments, burs, handpieces, implant kits, impression trays, orthodontic pliers) and the compatible cleaning and sterilization method for each, based on the manufacturer's reprocessing instructions. Hollow or lumened items (e.g. handpieces, implant drills with internal irrigation) need a **type B (vacuum)** sterilizer or a method validated by the manufacturer; type N sterilizers are only for solid, unwrapped instruments.

### 5.4 At the chairside (segregation and transport)

1. Immediately after treatment, the clinician and nurse:
   - dispose of needles, blades, matrix bands, endodontic files and other single-use sharps into the sharps container at the point of use;
   - dispose of other single-use items into the correct waste stream;
   - remove gross debris from instruments with a damp wipe if needed (do not hand scrub at the chairside);
   - keep instruments moist if there will be a delay before processing [practice decision: method, e.g. close the lid of the transport box with a damp wipe].
2. Used instruments are placed in a **rigid, leak-proof, lidded transport box** labelled "dirty/used". Clean instruments travel in a separate box labelled "clean". The two are never confused [practice decision: colour coding, e.g. red = dirty, green = clean].
3. Used instruments go to the LDU as soon as practicable after each patient or at the end of the session.

### 5.5 Personal protective equipment in the LDU

Staff wear: heavy-duty household gloves (for manual cleaning) or clinical gloves; a disposable plastic apron; eye protection or a visor; and a mask if splashing is likely. PPE is removed before leaving the LDU and hand hygiene performed (see 23-personal-protective-equipment-policy.md and SHTM 01-05 Part C).

### 5.6 Cleaning

The practice's **preferred method is the washer-disinfector**, because it is the most reliable, can be validated and reduces handling of sharp instruments. The ultrasonic cleaner and manual cleaning are used only for items the manufacturer says are not compatible with the washer-disinfector, or when the washer-disinfector has failed.

**Washer-disinfector**

1. Load instruments so that all surfaces are exposed: hinged instruments open, cassettes and racks not overloaded, handpieces on the correct connectors (if compatible).
2. Use only the detergent recommended by the manufacturer; check chemical levels before the first cycle.
3. Select the validated cycle. Do not interrupt it.
4. At the end, check the cycle display/printout or data logger shows the cycle completed within its parameters. If not, treat as a failure (5.11).
5. Unload into the clean zone with clean gloves.

**Ultrasonic cleaner**

1. Fill with fresh solution and detergent to the manufacturer's dilution at the start of each session; degas before first use if the manufacturer says so.
2. Suspend instruments in the basket, fully immersed, hinged instruments open; do not overload. Close the lid before switching on (to contain aerosol).
3. Run for the validated time. Do not put hands in the bath while it is running.
4. Rinse instruments in clean water [practice decision: rinse sink with RO/distilled or freshly drawn water] and drain.
5. Empty, clean and dry the tank at the end of each session, or sooner if the solution is visibly dirty.

**Manual cleaning (only when the above cannot be used)**

1. Use the dedicated instrument sink, with water at the temperature recommended by the detergent manufacturer (usually below 45°C to avoid fixing protein) and a measured volume of detergent.
2. Keep instruments under the water surface to avoid splashing. Use a long-handled, soft brush. Never use wire brushes or abrasives.
3. Rinse in the separate rinse sink, then drain.
4. Replace brushes [practice decision: daily, or decontaminate them], and change water when visibly dirty and at the end of each session.

### 5.7 Inspection

1. After cleaning, every instrument is inspected under an **illuminated magnifier** for residual debris, damage, corrosion, blunt edges and correct function.
2. Visibly dirty instruments go back for re-cleaning. Damaged instruments are taken out of use and replaced.
3. Handpieces are lubricated and maintained according to the manufacturer's instructions [practice decision: handpiece maintenance unit or manual lubrication] before sterilization.

### 5.8 Sterilization

1. Sterilization is by **steam sterilizer** only.
2. **Type N (non-vacuum)** sterilizers are used only for **solid, unwrapped** instruments. Instruments must not be wrapped or pouched before processing in a type N sterilizer.
3. **Type B (vacuum)** sterilizers are used for wrapped/pouched instruments, hollow and lumened items (e.g. handpieces, implant components where the manufacturer specifies) and any load the manufacturer specifies.
4. Load as the manufacturer instructs: do not overload; trays perforated; pouches on edge or paper side as directed.
5. Use the validated cycle (e.g. 134–137°C for a minimum holding time of 3 minutes – confirm against SHTM 01-05 and the sterilizer's validation report).
6. Water for the sterilizer reservoir is [practice decision: sterile/RO/distilled water as specified by the manufacturer]. Drain and refill the reservoir at the frequency the manufacturer specifies (at least daily).
7. At the end of each cycle, check the display, printout or data logger to confirm the cycle passed. A failed cycle is never released (5.11).
8. Allow loads to dry fully before removing. Wet packs are not acceptable and are reprocessed.
9. Each released load is recorded (date, sterilizer, cycle number, pass, operator initials). [Practice decision: whether load traceability is linked to the patient record in Dentally.]

### 5.9 Storage

1. Sterilized instruments are stored in the **clean area**, in clean, dry, covered storage (closed cupboards or drawers), away from aerosol and splash, and off the floor.
2. Instruments from a **type N** sterilizer are unwrapped, so they are used [practice decision: within the same session / stored covered and reprocessed if not used within the period set by SHTM 01-05 – check current guidance].
3. Instruments that are wrapped after type N processing, or wrapped and processed in a **type B** sterilizer, are labelled with the sterilization date and [use-by / reprocess-by date set by the practice in line with SHTM 01-05].
4. Stock is rotated (first in, first out). Packs that are damaged, wet, open or past their date are reprocessed.
5. Instruments in the surgery are kept in drawers or covered; only those needed for the current patient are set out.

### 5.10 Equipment testing and validation

Daily and weekly tests are done by trained LDU staff and recorded on the PSM test record templates. Periodic tests and validation are done by the competent person. The schedule below is the practice's working schedule; **the IPC Lead must confirm it against SHTM 01-05 and each manufacturer's instructions** and amend it before adoption.

| Equipment | Daily (or each session) | Weekly | Quarterly / annual / periodic (competent person) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Washer-disinfector | Check and clean filters/strainers and spray arms (rotate freely, jets clear); check detergent levels; record the automatic control test (cycle parameters) for each cycle | Safety checks (door seal, door interlock); cleaning efficacy test, e.g. protein residue test [confirm] | Periodic tests and annual revalidation per SHTM 01-05 [confirm frequency]; servicing |

| Type N sterilizer | Automatic control test: record temperature, pressure and holding time from the display/printout/data logger for the first cycle (and each cycle); drain and refill reservoir | Safety checks: door seal, door safety devices, general condition | Periodic tests and annual validation per SHTM 01-05 [confirm]; PSSR examination per the Written Scheme (typically at least every 14 months); servicing |

1. If any daily or weekly test fails, the equipment is **taken out of use**, labelled "Do not use", and the failure procedure (5.11) is followed.
2. The IPC Lead checks and initials the test records every week.
3. Validation, periodic test, service and PSSR examination reports are kept in the equipment file.

### 5.11 Decontamination process failures

The IPC Lead completes the PSM **Decontamination Process Failures Planning Tool** to set out what happens if each piece of equipment fails. The minimum steps are:

1. **Stop** using the equipment. Label it "Out of use" with the date and reason.
2. **Quarantine** the affected load. Do not release it. Instruments from a failed cycle are reprocessed through a working machine after the fault is fixed, or through the back-up method below.
3. **Check** whether any instruments from a suspect cycle have already been used on patients. If so, tell the Practice Owner at once. The Practice Owner considers the risk, seeks advice from NHS Fife if needed, and applies the Duty of Candour procedure where it is triggered (see 02-duty-of-candour-procedure.md).
4. **Call** the service engineer [company and phone number]. Record the call.
5. **Back-up arrangements** while equipment is out of use: [practice decision – e.g. use the second sterilizer; manual or ultrasonic cleaning in place of the washer-disinfector; reduce the number of patients; use single-use instruments; reschedule appointments].
6. After repair, the engineer confirms in writing whether the equipment is safe to use and whether revalidation is needed. Daily tests must pass before the equipment returns to service.
7. **Record** the fault, actions and outcome in the equipment fault log (part of the Process Failures Planning Tool). Review significant failures as a significant event (see 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md).

### 5.12 Decontamination before repair or servicing

COSHH places a statutory duty on the dentist to render instruments and equipment safe before repair.

1. Decontaminate items, including handpieces, using the practice's **usual method** (clean, disinfect and sterilize where compatible) before sending them for servicing or repair.
2. For equipment that **cannot be sterilized** (e.g. curing lights, scaler units, motors), clean and disinfect according to the manufacturer's instructions.
3. Tell the recipient how the item has been decontaminated, using the PSM **Decontamination Agreement** template or a label/form with each item.
4. For engineers working on site (e.g. on the dental chair or LDU equipment), clean and disinfect the equipment surfaces first and tell the engineer what has been done.
5. Record what was sent, when, to whom and how it was decontaminated.

### 5.13 New instruments

New reusable instruments are decontaminated through the full process before first use, unless supplied sterile and intended to be used as supplied. Manufacturer's reprocessing instructions are checked first and added to the methods register (5.3).

## 7. Training

1. Only staff who have been trained and signed off as competent may work in the LDU.
2. Training covers: this policy; LDU zoning and flow; PPE; transport; each cleaning method; inspection; sterilizer types and loading; daily and weekly tests; what to do if a test fails; storage; sharps safety in the LDU.
3. Competence is assessed by observation and recorded on the Staff Training in Decontamination template. The Example Job Description Including LDU Duties template is used for LDU roles.
4. The NES Framework to Support Staff Development in the Decontamination of Re-usable Medical Devices (TURAS) is used to plan development.
5. GDC registrants complete 5 hours of disinfection and decontamination CPD per CPD cycle.
6. Competence is reassessed [annually] and when equipment or procedures change.
