## 1. Purpose

Hard surfaces in surgeries and the local decontamination unit (LDU), such as work tops, equipment and containers, can become contaminated with blood, saliva and oral bacteria or viruses before, during and after treatment. This policy sets out how the practice cleans the care environment and care equipment, how the level of cleaning is chosen by risk, how blood and body fluid spills are managed, and the cleaning schedule for clinical and non-clinical areas.

## 2. Scope

All areas of the practice: surgeries, LDU, X-ray/imaging areas, reception, waiting room, patient and staff toilets, staff room, corridors and stores. It covers all staff who clean, including dental nurses and any contract cleaner [cleaning company, if used].

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 General principles

1. Keep all surgery surfaces **clear and clutter-free**. Store as many items as possible in cupboards or drawers, or covered.
2. Use cleaning products that are **compatible with the surfaces** and equipment (check the dental chair and equipment manufacturers' instructions) so they are not damaged.
3. Check that all surfaces (work tops, chair upholstery, flooring, cupboard doors) are **intact**. Report damage to the Practice Manager, who arranges repair as soon as possible.
4. Clinical areas are **clearly zoned** and labelled as **clean** or **dirty**.
5. Use **colour-coded** cleaning equipment (cloths, mops, buckets) so equipment from toilets is never used in clinical areas. [Practice decision: colour code – e.g. red = toilets/washrooms, blue = general areas, green = staff kitchen, yellow = clinical areas.]
6. Mop heads and reusable cloths are [practice decision: disposable / laundered at 60°C or above after each use], and buckets are cleaned, dried and stored inverted.
7. COSHH assessments are in place for every cleaning product (see 18-coshh-policy.md).
8. Staff wear appropriate PPE (gloves, apron, eye protection as risk-assessed) and perform hand hygiene after cleaning.

### 5.2 Start and end of each session

At the **beginning and end of each session**, clean all large surfaces in the surgery and LDU, including both clean and dirty zones, using **warm water and general purpose neutral detergent** (or detergent wipes). This includes work tops, the dental chair, bracket table, light handles, spittoon, cupboard fronts, sinks and taps.

### 5.3 Between patients – choose the level by risk

The level of cleaning between treatment episodes depends on the type of procedure. Routes of contamination to consider at all times are:

- airborne particles during and after use of rotary instruments (aerosol);
- splatter during surgical procedures;
- droplets from coughs and sneezes;
- touching surfaces and objects with contaminated hands and gloves.

| Risk level | Examples | Cleaning between patients |
|---|---|---|
| **Low risk** – surfaces in the clinical area not touched | Examination only, without touching surfaces | Clean surfaces with **detergent wipes** |
| **High risk** – aerosol produced, **or** surfaces touched, **or** surfaces used for putting down contaminated instruments or equipment during **any** procedure | Restorations, scaling, implant and other surgery; bracket table, light handles, chair controls, aspirator, curing light, X-ray tube head | Clean with **detergent wipes, followed by disinfectant wipes**, or use **combination (detergent and disinfectant) wipes** if compatible with the surfaces |

Procedure between patients:

1. Remove and dispose of single-use covers and barriers.
2. Take used instruments to the LDU in the dirty transport box.
3. Put on fresh gloves. Wipe from clean to dirty and from top to bottom, using one wipe per area and replacing it when dry or soiled.
4. Allow the contact time stated by the wipe manufacturer.
5. Flush DUWLs for 30 seconds (see 28-legionella-and-dental-unit-waterlines.md) and run the aspirator.
6. Remove gloves, perform hand hygiene and replace barriers for the next patient.

Wipes in use: [detergent wipe product], [disinfectant / combination wipe product] – chosen using NHS NSS guidance on wipes and checked for compatibility with the surfaces.

After aerosol generating procedures, allow [practice decision: fallow/settle time, if any, per current NIPCM guidance and the surgery's ventilation] before cleaning and the next patient.

### 5.4 Blood spillage

A spill kit is kept in each surgery and in the LDU [location]. For a blood spill:

1. Gather all the equipment needed.
2. Perform appropriate hand hygiene.
3. Cover any cuts or grazes with a waterproof dressing.
4. Wear appropriate PPE (disposable gloves, apron, and eye protection if there is a splash risk).
5. Either:
   - apply **chlorine-releasing granules** directly to the spill; **or**
   - contain the spill with disposable paper towels, then saturate the towels with a disinfectant solution of **10,000 ppm available chlorine** and leave in place as recommended by the manufacturer, or for **at least 3 minutes**.
6. Dispose of the towels (and granule residue) as **healthcare waste (orange waste stream)**.
7. Clean the area with fresh disposable towels and a solution of warm water and general purpose neutral detergent.
8. Discard paper towels and disposable PPE as healthcare waste.
9. Perform hand hygiene.

Chlorine-releasing agents can damage some surfaces and fabrics and must not be mixed with acids or used on urine (risk of chlorine gas). Check compatibility and the COSHH assessment.

### 5.5 Other body fluid spills

For other body fluids (e.g. urine, vomit), follow the NIPCM **Safe Management of Blood and Body Fluid Spillages** SICP. [Practice decision: insert the NIPCM flowchart for urine, faeces and vomit spills in the spill kit.]

### 5.6 Care equipment

1. Reusable patient care equipment that does not go through the LDU (e.g. curing light, intraoral camera, shade guides, X-ray sensor holders, chair controls, keyboards in surgeries) is cleaned between patients as for high-risk surfaces, following the manufacturer's instructions.
2. Barriers (single-use covers) are used on items that are hard to clean, such as X-ray sensors, curing light tips and keyboards, and changed between patients.
3. The aspirator and spittoon are cleaned and disinfected with a non-foaming product at the end of each session, and filters/traps changed as the manufacturer specifies.

### 5.7 Non-clinical areas

Non-clinical areas, such as the waiting room, patient toilets and the staff room, are cleaned according to the schedule in 5.8. Toys, magazines and leaflets in the waiting area are kept to a minimum and must be wipeable [practice decision].

- All clinical staff are trained at induction in surface cleaning, zoning, risk levels, wipe use and spillage management, and are observed before working unsupervised.
- Reception staff and cleaners are trained in the parts of the schedule they carry out, colour coding and how to report a spill.
- Refresher training [annually] and when products change.
