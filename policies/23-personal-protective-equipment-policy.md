## 1. Purpose

PPE reduces the risk of contact with biological and chemical hazards in dental care. This policy sets out how the practice assesses the need for PPE, what PPE is used for each task, how it is supplied, worn, removed, stored and disposed of, and how staff are trained. A PPE policy is a requirement of practice inspection; this is a stand-alone policy that sits alongside the Infection Prevention and Control policy.

## 2. Scope

All clinical and decontamination work, cleaning, waste handling and chemical handling at the practice, by employees, self-employed clinicians, trainees, locums and cleaners. It also covers PPE provided for **patients** (eye protection and bibs) and, where needed, for visitors and contractors.

PPE is the last line of defence. It is used together with other controls in 18-coshh-policy.md and 24-infection-prevention-and-control-policy.md.

## 3. Legislation and guidance

As cited in the PSM:
- Personal Protective Equipment at Work Regulations 1992, amended 2002, and the Personal Protective Equipment at Work (Amendment) Regulations 2022
- HSE *Personal protective equipment (PPE) at work* webpages
- National Infection Prevention and Control Manual (NIPCM), ARHAI Scotland / NHS National Services Scotland – PPE, donning and doffing, glove choice
- NES TURAS infection control resources (donning and doffing videos, hand hygiene)
- SHTM 01-05 (Health Facilities Scotland, 2024) Part C – PPE for instrument cleaning in a Local Decontamination Unit
- Management of Health and Safety at Work Regulations 1999 (risk assessment)

The Regulations require the employer to:
- assess whether PPE is required;
- provide PPE that is fit for purpose;
- maintain and store PPE properly;
- give instructions on how to use PPE safely;
- make sure PPE is used correctly.

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 PPE risk assessment

1. As part of the five-step health and safety risk assessment (17-health-and-safety-policy.md), the IPC Lead identifies, for each procedure, **when** PPE is needed and **what type**.
2. The assessment includes the risks to **patients** and sets out the suitable PPE to provide for them (eye protection, bibs).
3. It considers aerosol-generating procedures, chemicals (from COSHH assessments), sharps, laser use (if any), latex allergy and skin health.
4. The PPE matrix (section 5.2) is the result. It is displayed in each surgery and the LDU and reviewed annually or when procedures, products or national guidance change.

### 5.2 PPE matrix

| Task | Gloves | Apron/gown | Eye protection | Mask / visor | Patient |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Examination and all clinical procedures | Clinical gloves (powder-free), new for each patient | Plastic disposable apron [or clinical clothing per IPC risk assessment] | Eye protection with side protection | Fluid-resistant surgical mask; visor/face shield where aerosol or splash expected | Eye protection and bib |
| Aerosol-generating procedures (high-speed, ultrasonic scaling, air polishing) | Clinical gloves | Apron or gown | Eye protection with side protection or visor | Mask/visor/face shield for aerosol protection [type per current NIPCM] | Eye protection and bib |
| Surgical and implant procedures | Sterile surgical gloves | Sterile gown [per implant protocol] | Eye protection with side protection | Surgical mask; visor as needed | Eye protection, sterile drape |
| Instrument decontamination (LDU) | Heavy-duty household gloves | Plastic disposable apron | Eye protection with side protection (or visor) | Mask [per SHTM 01-05 Part C] | – |

| Waste handling | Gloves | Apron | – | – | – |

| Radiography | Clinical gloves | – | – | – | – (no lead aprons unless RPA advises) |

### 5.3 Using PPE correctly

1. **Gloves**
   - Clinical gloves are worn for all clinical procedures and are discarded after each patient, or straight away if torn or perforated.
   - Only powder-free gloves (nitrile or low-protein latex) are used (21-latex-allergy-policy.md). Gloves are selected using the NIPCM.
   - Perform hand hygiene before putting gloves on and after taking them off. Gloves are not a substitute for hand hygiene (25-hand-hygiene-policy.md). Never wash or reuse single-use gloves.
   - Household gloves for decontamination are single-person use, washed and dried after use, and replaced when worn, cracked or weekly [practice decision].
2. **Aprons and gowns**: single-use plastic aprons are changed between patients and between tasks.
3. **Eye protection** must have side protection. Reusable eye protection and visors are cleaned between patients according to the manufacturer's instructions. Single-use items are disposed of after use. Prescription glasses alone are not eye protection.
4. **Masks, visors and face shields** protect against aerosols and splashes. Masks are changed between patients, and when damp or damaged. They are not worn round the neck or pushed onto the forehead.
5. **Patient PPE**: every patient is offered eye protection and a bib for treatment. Patient eye protection is cleaned between patients.
6. **Donning and doffing**: staff follow the NIPCM order, and the NES TURAS donning and doffing videos are used for training. A donning and doffing poster is displayed in each surgery. PPE is removed in a way that avoids contaminating the wearer, with hand hygiene at the end.
7. PPE is removed before leaving the clinical area and is not worn at reception, in the staff room or outside.
8. Used disposable PPE goes in the **orange** waste stream (22-waste-management-policy.md).

### 5.4 Clinical clothing (uniforms)

1. Clinical clothing is worn only within the practice. It must **not** be worn outside the premises (including travelling to and from work, or on breaks outside).
2. Staff change at work in the changing area provided.
3. Clinical clothing is laundered following the care label instructions. [Practice decision: laundered by the practice / laundry service / at home – if at home, washed separately at the hottest temperature the label allows and carried in a sealed bag.]
4. Enough uniforms are provided for a clean set each day [number per person].
5. Bare below the elbows applies in clinical areas (see 24-infection-prevention-and-control-policy.md).

### 5.5 Supply, storage and maintenance

1. PPE is provided free of charge, in a range of sizes, so that each person has PPE that fits.
2. Stock is stored clean and dry, off the floor, away from direct sunlight and contamination, and in its packaging until use, so it stays fit for purpose. Stock is rotated and checked for expiry dates.
3. PPE is bought from reputable suppliers with the correct standards marking (see 32-procurement-of-ipc-items.md).
4. Damaged or defective PPE is removed from use and reported to the IPC Lead.
5. Minimum stock levels are set [e.g. 4 weeks] and checked [weekly] by the stock controller, so that PPE is always available.

### 5.6 Contracts and compliance

1. The requirement to wear PPE is included in staff contracts and agreements (including self-employed associate agreements) and in the Health and Safety Policy.
2. Failure to wear PPE when required is treated as a disciplinary matter after support and retraining.
3. Any staff member who has a problem with PPE (fit, skin reaction, discomfort) tells the IPC Lead, who will look for an alternative. Skin reactions are handled under 21-latex-allergy-policy.md.

## 6. Records and monitoring

- PPE risk assessment and matrix: IPC file; reviewed annually and on change.
- Training records (including donning and doffing): staff files.
- PPE audit: the IPC Lead observes PPE use in each surgery and the LDU [every 6 months – practice decision] using a simple checklist (correct items, changed between patients, correct removal, hand hygiene, uniform rules). Results and actions are recorded and discussed at the practice meeting. This can form part of the IPC audit programme (39-quality-improvement-and-audit.md).
- Stock checks: stock sheet.
- Incidents involving PPE failure (e.g. glove tear during a sharps injury): accident book and significant event analysis.

## 7. Training

- Induction for all clinical and decontamination staff, before working with patients: why PPE is needed, the PPE matrix, correct use, donning and doffing (NES TURAS videos), storage, disposal, and their legal obligation to wear it.
- Cleaners: PPE for cleaning and chemicals.
- Laser users (if any): laser eyewear under the local rules.
- Refresher [annually] and whenever products or guidance change.
