## 1. Purpose

Good records underpin safe, effective and shared patient care. They give a permanent record of the care each patient received, and they protect patients and the team if there is a complaint or a legal claim. This policy sets out:

- the standard every record entry must meet;
- what must be recorded for adults, children and emergency (unscheduled) patients;
- how computerised records are kept in Dentally, and how any paper records are handled;
- how records are stored, backed up, retained and securely disposed of;
- how record-keeping is audited;
- what happens to records if the practice closes.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to every member of the team who creates, reads or handles patient records: dentists, hygienists, therapists, dental nurses, reception staff, managers, locums and trainees. It covers all patient information in any form, including:

- the Dentally clinical record (notes, charts, medical histories, care plans, treatment, appointments, correspondence);
- radiographs, CBCT scans [if applicable], intra-oral scans and clinical photographs;
- consent forms, signed medical history forms (paper or electronic), referral letters, laboratory prescriptions;
- NHS forms and claims;
- any paper records received from other practices.

Complaint correspondence is kept separately from the clinical record (see 01-complaints-feedback-procedure.md). Data protection rights, privacy notices and subject access requests are covered in 10-data-protection-confidentiality-information-security.md and 11-privacy-notices.md.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Principles: every record must be

- **accurate**;
- **dated** (Dentally date-stamps each entry automatically);
- **confidential** and **secure**;
- **contemporaneous** – updated at each appointment, the same day;
- **comprehensive**;
- **legible**, in language others can read and understand, so care can be shared.

Rules for all staff:

1. Use the standard Dentally templates and forms for consistency and to avoid omissions. The practice set of Dentally templates is listed in [appendix / Dentally settings] and agreed by the Practice Owner.
2. **Never remove entries.** Corrections are made as a new, dated addendum explaining the correction.
3. Use only abbreviations on the practice's agreed list [location of list], which everyone understands.
4. No derogatory or personal comments about patients. Patients have a right to see their records.
5. Each patient is identified by full name, date of birth and CHI number (NHS patients), with a unique Dentally patient ID, so that the right record is always used.
6. Record discussions, phone calls, emails and texts with the patient in the notes.

### 5.2 Computerised records (Dentally)

The practice keeps its clinical records electronically in Dentally (cloud-based).

1. **Passwords and logins.** Every user has an individual Dentally login with a strong password and [multi-factor authentication]. Passwords are never shared or written down. Users change passwords [practice decision – as Dentally / IT policy requires]. Screens are locked whenever a workstation is left unattended.
2. **Role-based access.** Staff only access the information they need for their role. Access levels are set by the Practice Manager and reviewed [every 6 months]. Leavers' accounts are disabled on their last day.
3. **Audit trail.** The Practice Owner has confirmed with Dentally (in writing, date [date]) that the system keeps a full audit trail and that records cannot be erased or modified without the change being recorded and attributed to the user.
4. **Identification of the user.** Every entry shows who made it. Ideally the team member who enters appointment data logs it under their own login.
5. **Signatures.** Medical histories are completed by the patient [on a tablet with electronic signature in Dentally / on paper and scanned]. Electronically signed patient-completed forms are acceptable as legally binding. Where paper is used, the patient signs and the practitioner countersigns, and the form is scanned into Dentally.
6. **Software protection.** All practice computers have up-to-date antivirus and firewall protection and operating system updates [managed by IT provider [Name]].
7. **Back-ups.** The PSM requires back-ups at least daily, stored off-site, more substantial weekly back-ups, verification that back-ups work, more than one person knowing where they are, and a documented back-up protocol. Because Dentally is a cloud system:
   - Dentally holds and backs up the data in its data centres. The Data Protection Lead keeps on file Dentally's written description of its back-up frequency, off-site / multi-site storage and restore arrangements, and its data processing agreement. Date checked: [date].
   - Locally held data that is **not** in Dentally (for example imaging software on a local server [if applicable], scanned documents, practice files) is backed up [daily] to [encrypted off-site/cloud back-up], with a fuller back-up [weekly].
   - A test restore is carried out [every 6 months] and recorded, with advice from the software supplier.
   - At least [two] named people – [Name] and [Name] – know how back-ups work and how to restore.
   - The back-up protocol is written down and kept with the business continuity plan (see 38-business-continuity-plan.md).
8. **Long-term access.** Before changing software, the practice makes sure historical records (including images) can be exported and remain readable for the full retention period.
9. **Imaging.** Radiographs, CBCT scans, photographs and intra-oral scans are stored in [imaging software] linked to the Dentally record, and are part of the patient record.

### 5.3 Written paper records (where used)

If paper is ever used (e.g. records transferred from another practice, or during an IT outage – see 38-business-continuity-plan.md):

1. Write in ink.
2. Sign and date each entry.
3. Correct errors with a single line through the error, and date the amendment.
4. Use only agreed abbreviations; no derogatory comments.
5. Keep entries complete, clear, accurate and legible.
6. Scan the record into Dentally as soon as possible, then store or dispose of the paper original as set out in 5.7 and 5.9 [practice decision – whether paper originals are retained after scanning].

### 5.4 Records required for all patients (adults)

**At registration** record:

- **Personal details** – full name, date of birth, address, contact details, parent/carer/next of kin (for children and vulnerable patients), GP name, CHI number, any special requirements (e.g. access needs, interpreter, communication needs).
- **Social history** – smoking, alcohol, diet (eating habits, sugar and dietary acid intake), contact sports, occupation.
- **Dental history** – oral hygiene habits, grinding, attendance pattern, previous treatment (orthodontic, implant, periodontal), acceptance of local anaesthetic, reason for attending.
- **Medical history** – existing conditions, current medication, allergies (e.g. penicillin, latex), relevant family history (e.g. diabetes).
- **Anxiety** – ask every new patient whether they are anxious about any aspect of visiting the dentist, and record the answer.

Check the patient understood the questions. Record the date each form was completed and which practitioner recorded the details.

**At each recall** check and update all personal information and the medical history; record and date any changes. Record cancelled and missed appointments.

**Complaints** – note in the record that a complaint was made and the action taken; keep the complaint correspondence separately.

**Assessment of oral health status** – record each element of the assessment, including significant findings **and** where there are no significant findings:

- extra-oral (head and neck);
- intra-oral: oral mucosa (including oral cancer screening), periodontal tissues (BPE), teeth, dentures;
- radiographs taken and the report for each (justification, findings, image grade – see 35-radiation-protection-policy.md);
- any other tests or investigations;
- diagnoses made;
- copies of referral letters, and that the patient was told how their information will be shared and had the chance to refuse;
- whether consent to share has been given;
- the agreed **care plan**: preventive and operative treatments, costs (NHS and private), advice, referrals, recall interval and date agreed. The patient is given a copy. It is advisable for the patient and practitioner to sign it;
- the date and the name of the practitioner who carried out the examination or test.

**Treatment** – for every treatment record:

- date;
- what is being treated, the type of treatment and the reason based on the diagnosis;
- tests carried out;
- materials used, with **batch numbers** of drugs and materials;
- discussion of options, risks and benefits, including the "do nothing" option;
- advice given (verbal, written or by phone);
- the patient's consent (see 03-consent-policy.md), or refusal of treatment or advice;
- any concerns of the patient or the team;
- any medication given and any adverse reaction. For drugs such as local anaesthetic: generic name, concentration, any other agent present (e.g. adrenaline 1:80,000) and dose;
- changes to the treatment plan;
- results of treatment (prognosis);
- the name of the practitioner.

**Implant treatment** (practice decision, building on the above) – also record: pre-operative assessment and imaging; implant system, sizes, lot/batch numbers and component labels; bone grafts or membranes with batch numbers; surgical notes; post-operative instructions; and the implant passport or record given to the patient.

### 5.5 Children's records

For every child, record everything in 5.4, plus:

1. Whether appointments were attended, cancelled, or the child **"was not brought"**.
2. The child's deprivation level (SIMD) and date of birth/age, for payment and to support preventive care.
3. In mixed dentition, whether the teeth recorded are primary or permanent.
4. In the interests of child protection:
   - who accompanied the child and, if not a parent, their relationship to the child;
   - observations of behaviour, not only physical signs;
   - a summary of discussions with the child and parent/carer.
5. If non-accidental injury is suspected:
   - observations and the reasons given, even for seemingly trivial injuries (a pattern may build up over time);
   - diagrams as well as written descriptions, or clinical photographs (with consent) labelled with the child's name and date;
   - observations written so that colleagues will understand them;
   - any differences between the facts given and your opinion.
   See 12-child-and-adult-protection.md.
6. The child's **caries risk category** and the factors it is based on (e.g. caries present, previous extractions, diet, oral hygiene).
7. Measures taken to address problems, including preventive programmes, and the care plan.
8. Consider the BSPD dental neglect policy and SDCEP caries guidance to decide whether a child protection referral may be needed.

The child record continues into the adult record.

### 5.6 Emergency patients (unscheduled visits)

Emergency care may be given to registered and non-registered patients. Comprehensive records are kept in both cases:

1. Record a **medical history** – particularly important after trauma.
2. If the patient is in pain and it is their first visit, focus on resolving the presenting complaint and record what was done.
3. Carry out and record the examination.
4. Record the diagnosis and any tests.
5. Agree treatment with the patient and record the details in writing.
6. Non-registered patients are set up in Dentally as [emergency/casual patients] with full personal details.

### 5.7 Storage and security

1. Electronic records are stored in password-protected systems with individual logins and role-based access (5.2). Unattended computers are screen-locked.
2. IT security measures – firewalls, virus protection and encryption (including encryption of any laptops or portable devices) – are in place.
3. USB drives are not used for patient data [practice decision]. If any portable media is unavoidable it must be encrypted and approved by the Data Protection Lead.
4. Any paper records are kept in lockable, fire-proof cabinets in [location], locked when authorised staff are not present.
5. Windows and doors are secured at the end of each day.
6. All staff receive data protection training. The contact details of the practice's Data Protection Officer [Name / service – NHS practices should have these readily available] are displayed in [location].
7. Contracts with third-party suppliers (Dentally, IT provider, imaging provider, laboratories, confidential waste contractor) set out confidentiality requirements.

### 5.8 Retention

The practice keeps records for the periods in the PSM (based on the *Records Management Code of Practice for Health and Social Care* 2024), and does not keep records longer than necessary.

| Record | Retention |
|---|---|
| Adult records | 10 years from last contact |
| Child records | 10 years from last contact (the child record evolves into the adult record) |
| Patient who has died (adult) | 3 years after death |
| Patient who died before their 17th birthday | Until the date they would have turned 25 |
| Radiographs and images | As part of the patient record (same periods) |
| Radiation incident reports | See 35-radiation-protection-policy.md (5 to 30 years) |
| Complaint files | [Practice decision – check the Records Management Code of Practice] |
| Midazolam order records and delivery notes | At least 2 years (see 34-medical-emergencies-policy.md) |

The Data Protection Lead reviews records due for destruction [annually], using a Dentally report of patients by date of last contact. Destruction is approved by the Practice Owner and logged. Check current guidance before destroying any record linked to a complaint, claim or investigation – those are kept until the matter is closed.

### 5.9 Disposal

1. The practice has a written policy for disposal of data (this section, and 10-data-protection-confidentiality-information-security.md).
2. **Paper records** are disposed of by shredding or incineration, preferably through a recognised confidential disposal company that confirms disposal with a receipt/certificate. Contractor: [company]. NHS Fife can advise.
3. **Electronic records** are disposed of by overwriting or destroying the data and storage devices. Deleting files or reformatting drives is **not** enough. Old computers, hard drives and devices are destroyed by [certified IT disposal company], with a certificate of destruction.
4. Deletion of records from Dentally follows Dentally's process [confirm with supplier], and is logged.
5. All disposal certificates are kept in [location].

### 5.10 Practice closure

The practice adopts the PSM **Practice Closure: Disposal of Patient Records Policy** template, adapted, so that if the practice ever closes:

1. NHS Fife and patients are informed as required.
2. Records continue to be stored securely and remain accessible for the full retention periods, including export from Dentally in a readable format.
3. A named person is responsible for the records after closure: [Name / successor practice / records storage company].
4. Records are only transferred to another practice with appropriate safeguards and, where needed, patient agreement.
5. Records are securely destroyed only when retention periods end.
Date adopted: [date].

### 5.11 Patient access

Patients have a right to access their records under the Data Protection Act 2018. Requests are handled by the Data Protection Lead as set out in 10-data-protection-confidentiality-information-security.md.

## 6. Records and monitoring

1. **Record-keeping audit** – the Practice Owner leads an in-house audit of a sample of [e.g. 20] records per clinician [every 12 months], checking:
   - patients' personal details for administration;
   - medical histories completed, dated and signed;
   - radiographs properly recorded (justification, report, grade);
   - periodontal screening (BPE) documented;
   - oral cancer screening documented;
   - care plans recorded adequately;
   - children's caries risk status recorded;
   - date of next bitewing radiographs recorded, if applicable;
   - preventive measures noted.
   Results, action plan and re-audit are recorded (see 39-quality-improvement-and-audit.md).
2. **Dentally access review** – [every 6 months].
3. **Back-up test restore** – [every 6 months], recorded.
4. **Retention review and destruction log** – [annually].
5. This policy is reviewed every 12 months.

## 7. Training

1. All new staff are trained on this policy, Dentally use and data protection at induction, before accessing patient records.
2. Clinicians are trained in the practice's Dentally templates, agreed abbreviations and the record standards in 5.4 to 5.6.
3. All staff complete data protection and information security training [annually].
4. Feedback from the record-keeping audit is shared with each clinician and at a team meeting.
