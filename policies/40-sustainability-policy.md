## 1. Purpose

Climate change, planetary health and sustainability are emerging areas in health care, including dentistry. Dental practices can reduce their environmental impact by cutting emissions, reducing waste and managing resources well. This often also saves money, is more convenient, and improves the well-being of patients and the team.

The World Health Organisation defines environmental sustainability in health care as a system that "improves, maintains or restores health, while minimizing negative impacts on the environment and leveraging opportunities to restore and improve it, to the benefit of the health and well-being of current and future generations".

As a new practice, Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic has the chance to build sustainable habits in from the start. This policy sets out how we will do that, without ever compromising patient safety or infection control.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to all staff and all practice activity: travel, procurement, energy, waste, water, clinical practice, patient advice and team development. Nothing in this policy overrides infection prevention and control, decontamination, waste regulations or health and safety requirements.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 First steps (from the PSM)

1. Appoint a practice sustainability champion: [Name], appointed [date].
2. Research additional resources (section 3).
3. Include sustainability as a standing item at team meetings [quarterly], to support positive behaviour change.
4. Agree which actions to introduce in the short, medium and longer term (5.2 to 5.8, and the action plan in 5.9).
5. Set targets and measure outcomes, using quality improvement methods (see 39-quality-improvement-and-audit.md).

### 5.2 Travel

- Encourage staff and patients to use public transport or active travel (walking, cycling). Kirkcaldy railway station and bus station are [distance] from the practice; this is stated on the website and appointment information.
- Provide bicycle parking for staff and patients [where the building allows – check with Fife Council, as the building is listed].
- Support car sharing among staff.
- Tell patients about nearby electric vehicle charging points [location].
- Offer appointments that fit public transport timetables where possible.
- Offer joint appointments for families and groups to reduce trips.
- Avoid wasted appointments: confirm laboratory work has arrived before the patient's appointment; use digital or remote consultations where clinically appropriate (e.g. implant consultation follow-ups, reviews of photographs).
- Reduce laboratory collections and deliveries; use postal services where possible; consider digital impressions (intra-oral scanning) to reduce physical transport [if applicable].

### 5.3 Procurement

- Buy supplies and materials that enable high-quality care, and use them appropriately.
- Consider co-operative buying schemes (e.g. DenPro).
- Ask suppliers about biodegradable and lower-impact products, and products with less packaging.
- Buy recycled products where possible: printer paper, printer cartridges, toilet paper, hand towels.
- Use a rotational stock system (first in, first out) so stock does not expire. Order realistic quantities.
- Buy sustainable products for the staff room (e.g. Fairtrade tea and coffee).
- Choose energy-efficient equipment when buying new (A-rated appliances, LED lights).
- All IPC items are still procured as set out in 32-procurement-of-ipc-items.md.

### 5.4 Energy

- Fit energy-efficient technology: low-energy (LED) lighting; sensor lights in toilets, corridors and store rooms.
- Switch to a green energy supplier where practicable. Supplier: [supplier / tariff].
- Switch off lights, radios and equipment when surgeries and the LDU are not in use.
- Do not leave appliances on standby where possible.
- Shut down monitors and workstations at the end of the day. **Do not** shut down servers or network back-up devices.
- Run washer-disinfectors and sterilisers with full loads whenever possible, without overloading and without delaying instrument processing beyond IPC limits.
- Run the staff dishwasher with full loads; fill the kettle only as needed.
- End-of-day close-down checklist includes switch-off of non-essential equipment [Name responsible].

### 5.5 Waste and water

- Provide recycling bins for non-confidential paper, plastics and food waste. Confidential paper goes to the confidential shredding service (see 36-clinical-record-keeping-and-retention.md).
- Reduce paper: use email and text for appointment reminders, treatment plans, receipts and patient information leaflets; use QR codes in reception for leaflets; print double-sided when printing is necessary. Use Dentally's electronic forms and signatures for medical histories.
- Encourage patients to recycle toothbrushes and toothpaste tubes [practice decision – collection point in reception].
- Label dual-flush toilets so users know which button is the half flush.
- Make sure every single-use item is needed for the appointment – e.g. provide a patient rinse cup only when needed or requested. Do not open items "just in case".
- Wear the appropriate PPE for each task – no more and no less (see 23-personal-protective-equipment-policy.md).
- Reduce chemical waste – the practice uses digital radiography, so there are no processing chemicals.
- Review waste segregation regularly so items go in the right stream – e.g. non-contaminated packaging goes in trade or recycling waste rather than healthcare waste, reducing the amount incinerated (see 22-waste-management-policy.md).
- Avoid washing dishes under running water.

### 5.6 Health and well-being

- Promote disease prevention as good for the individual and the environment: diet and oral hygiene advice reduce the need for treatment.
- Encourage sustainable choices by patients: sustainable oral hygiene products, recyclable products, "spit, don't rinse", turn off the tap while brushing, fewer appointments, public transport and active travel.
- Offer smoking cessation advice, signposting and support to patients and staff.
- Encourage the team to bring healthy meals and snacks.
- Place plants in non-clinical areas.

### 5.7 Team engagement and development

- Include sustainability in staff induction.
- Lead by example and develop best practice.
- Keep up to date with new sustainability developments (the PSM will add sustainability points to other topics over time).
- Attend online CPD where possible to reduce travel.

### 5.8 Nitrous oxide [if applicable]

The practice [does not / does] provide inhalation sedation with nitrous oxide/oxygen. If it does, the following apply, because nitrous oxide is a potent, long-lived greenhouse gas (298 times the global warming potential of carbon dioxide, lifetime 114 years):

1. Consider all anxiety management techniques first, including non-pharmacological behaviour management (SDCEP *Conscious Sedation in Dentistry*).
2. Use the minimum effective dose at an appropriate flow rate.
3. Check equipment and breathing circuit tubing for leaks and faults, including that the Bodok seal is present and not perished.
4. Use a correctly fitting nasal hood with a working flapper valve.
5. Turn off cylinders / disconnect from wall outlets after use.
6. Record the nitrous oxide percentage at sedation endpoint, flow rate and duration in the patient record / sedation log.
7. Record receipt date, size, expiry date and serial number of each cylinder (*Nitrous Oxide Stock Record* template). Use cylinders with the shortest remaining shelf life first; review stock to avoid excess.
8. Store cylinders safely, away from ignition sources and secure from theft.
9. Consider a protocol to minimise residual gas left in cylinders.
10. Periodically review supply versus clinical use (*Nitrous Oxide Clinical Usage Review* template or *SDCEP Sedation Logbook*) and act on any losses found.

## 6. Records and monitoring

1. Action plan updated after each team meeting.
2. Measures tracked [quarterly] where data is available: electricity and gas use (from bills); healthcare waste and recycling volumes (from contractor records); paper purchased; proportion of patients receiving digital communications.
3. A sustainability QI project may count towards dentists' QI hours (see 39-quality-improvement-and-audit.md).
4. This policy and the action plan are reviewed every 12 months.

## 7. Training

1. All staff are introduced to this policy at induction.
2. Staff are encouraged to complete the NHS England e-Learning for Health *Environmental sustainability in dentistry* module or equivalent [within 12 months].
3. The Sustainability Champion shares new resources and ideas at team meetings.
4. If nitrous oxide sedation is provided, all staff involved in supply, management and use are made aware of the sustainability issues.
