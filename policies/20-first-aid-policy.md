## 1. Purpose

This policy makes sure that anyone who is injured or becomes ill at the practice gets prompt first aid, and that an ambulance is called when needed. It sets out our first-aid needs assessment, who our first-aiders and appointed persons are, the equipment we keep, and how we record incidents.

## 2. Scope

The Health and Safety (First-Aid) Regulations 1981 require first aid provision for **employees**. They do not place a legal duty on us for non-employees, but the PSM describes it as best practice to consider them, and we do: our provision covers staff, self-employed clinicians, patients, visitors and contractors.

This policy covers first aid for injuries and illness (cuts, burns, eye splashes, falls, fainting). **Medical emergencies** during dental care (collapse, anaphylaxis, cardiac arrest, etc.) are managed under 34-medical-emergencies-policy.md. Sharps and splash injuries also follow 29-occupational-exposure-and-sharps.md.

Note: the medical emergencies CPD that dental professionals must do does **not** equal a formal first aid at work qualification.

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 First-aid needs assessment

1. The Health & Safety Lead completes a first-aid needs assessment before opening, and reviews it annually or when staffing, layout or services change. It considers:
   - the number of staff on site at any time and the number of floors;
   - hazards from our work (sharps, chemicals, hot surfaces and sterilizers, slips, manual handling);
   - patients and visitors, including those who are elderly, disabled, children, or having surgery or sedation;
   - opening hours, lone working and absences;
   - distance to emergency services (Victoria Hospital, Kirkcaldy, for A&E [check]).
2. The result sets the number of first-aiders and appointed persons and the contents of the first-aid kit. Result: [e.g. 1 EFAW first-aider per session plus an appointed person at all times].

### 5.2 First-aiders and appointed persons

1. An **appointed person** is required in every workplace and must be available **whenever people are at work**. They do not have to be a qualified first-aider.
2. There is no legal requirement for every employer to have a trained first-aider, but the PSM advises it, so the practice trains at least [number] staff as first-aiders.
3. A first-aider holds a valid certificate in first aid at work (FAW), emergency first aid at work (EFAW), or other workplace-specific training. Certificates last **three years**. We arrange an **annual refresher** for first-aiders, as recommended.
4. The training provider is chosen using HSE guidance [Provider].
5. The Practice Manager plans the rota so that first-aid cover continues during annual leave and planned and unplanned absence. If the named appointed person is off, the deputy [Name] takes over and this is noted on the daily huddle sheet.

### 5.3 First-aid equipment

1. A fully stocked first-aid box, suitable for the number of staff, is kept at [location – e.g. reception], clearly marked with a white cross on a green background. [Second kit in LDU / upstairs – if needed.]
2. The box **does not contain medication** (as recommended). Emergency drugs are kept separately under 34-medical-emergencies-policy.md.
3. Eye wash (sterile water or saline) is kept at [LDU, surgeries] for chemical splashes.
4. The appointed person checks the contents [monthly], replaces used or out-of-date items and signs the check sheet inside the box. Anyone who uses an item tells the appointed person.

### 5.4 Notices and information

1. A first-aid notice is displayed at [reception, staff room, LDU] naming the appointed persons and first-aiders and stating where the first-aid equipment is.
2. New staff are told the first-aid arrangements at induction. First-aid information is also in the Health and Safety Policy (17-health-and-safety-policy.md).

### 5.5 What to do when someone is injured or unwell

1. Make the area safe. Do not put yourself in danger.
2. Call for the first-aider or appointed person (shout or use [phone extension / panic button]).
3. If the person is seriously ill or injured, call **999** at once. The appointed person calls the ambulance and sends someone to meet it at the front door. If they are not breathing normally or collapse, start the medical emergency procedure (34-medical-emergencies-policy.md): CPR and AED.
4. The first-aider gives first aid within their training. Wear gloves. Dispose of contaminated dressings in the orange waste stream (22-waste-management-policy.md).
5. Specific situations:
   - sharps or splash injury: follow 29-occupational-exposure-and-sharps.md straight away;
   - chemical in the eye or on skin: irrigate with water or eye wash and check the COSHH assessment / SDS (18-coshh-policy.md);
   - mercury or amalgam exposure: see 18-coshh-policy.md;
   - burns from sterilizers or hot water: cool under running water for at least 20 minutes [check current first aid guidance].
6. Do not give medicines as first aid.
7. Stay with the person until they are recovered, collected or handed to paramedics. Tell paramedics what happened and what was done.

### 5.6 Recording and reporting

1. Every accident is recorded in the data protection compliant accident book (BI510) by the injured person or the first-aider. Completed pages are stored securely.
2. The first-aider also notes what treatment was given.
3. For a patient, a note is added to the Dentally record.
4. The Health & Safety Lead checks whether the incident is reportable under RIDDOR – for example a patient taken directly to hospital for treatment, or a staff injury causing more than seven days' absence (17-health-and-safety-policy.md, section 5.11).
5. Incidents are investigated and lessons shared at the practice meeting (37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md).
6. Accident records are kept for at least 3 years.

## 7. Training

- First-aiders: FAW or EFAW (or workplace-specific) from a provider chosen using HSE guidance; renewed every 3 years with an annual refresher.
- Appointed persons: trained to the level needed for their role (managing the kit, calling the emergency services, basic first aid within training).
- All staff: first-aid arrangements at induction; medical emergency training under 34-medical-emergencies-policy.md.
