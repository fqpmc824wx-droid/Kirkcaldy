## 1. Purpose

Everyone who works at Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic has a duty to protect patients and an obligation to raise concerns about risks of harm or wrongdoing. This includes concerns about patient safety, quality of care, malpractice or regulatory breaches that may endanger public safety. This is known as raising concerns, speaking up or whistleblowing.

The practice provides NHS services and so must have a policy and procedure that meets the **National Whistleblowing Standards (2020)**. This policy sets out how staff can raise concerns, how concerns are handled under the two-stage procedure, and how they are recorded and reported to NHS Fife.

The practice wants staff to feel confident that their concerns will be taken seriously, managed properly, and that they will be told the outcome.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to anyone who delivers NHS services at the practice, including:

- employees, associates, self-employed dentists and hygienists/therapists;
- locums, agency and bank staff;
- students and trainees;

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 What you can raise

Examples include:

- risks to patient safety (for example infection control failures, unsafe equipment, radiation safety);
- poor quality of care;
- a colleague practising while impaired or outside their competence;
- fraud, including NHS claims irregularities;
- breaches of regulations or law;
- attempts to cover up any of the above.

### 5.2 Business as usual – raising a concern informally

1. Many concerns can be resolved quickly by speaking to the Practice Manager or a senior dentist. The INWO calls this "business as usual".
2. Raise the concern in person, by phone or in writing.
3. The person receiving it listens, records it briefly, agrees what will happen, and tells you the outcome.
4. Concerns dealt with as business as usual are **not** part of the two-stage whistleblowing procedure.
5. If the concern cannot be resolved informally, or you want it handled formally, the NHS whistleblowing procedure is followed.

### 5.3 Confidentiality and anonymity

- A concern raised under the whistleblowing procedure is treated **in confidence**. It is **not anonymous**: the person handling it will know who you are, but your identity will only be shared with your agreement or where legally required [check current guidance in the Standards].
- You can raise a concern **anonymously** within the practice. It will then **not** be handled under the NHS whistleblowing procedure. This may limit the support, legal protection and feedback you receive, and the INWO will not be able to review how it was handled.
- An alternative to raising a concern anonymously is to raise it with **Healthcare Improvement Scotland** through its "Responding to Concerns" programme. HIS will raise the concern with the practice without revealing who you are. This is separate from the NHS whistleblowing procedure.

### 5.4 The two-stage whistleblowing procedure

If the concern and the person raising it meet the whistleblowing definitions in the Standards, the two-stage procedure **must** be followed.

**Stage 1 – Early resolution**

1. For simple and straightforward issues that can be resolved in **5 working days or less**.
2. The Raising Concerns contact talks to you, agrees what the concern is and what you hope will happen, and takes action.
3. You are told the outcome and any action taken.
4. Record the concern (see 6.1).

**Stage 2 – Investigation**

1. For more complex issues that take more than 5 days to address. The practice must respond to you **within 20 working days**.
2. A concern also moves to Stage 2 if Stage 1 did not resolve it.
3. The Raising Concerns contact [or an independent investigator – practice decision; consider NHS Fife support for a small practice] investigates. Acknowledge the concern and agree the points to be investigated [check Part 3 of the Standards for acknowledgement timescales].
4. Keep you informed. If more time is needed, explain why and agree a revised timescale [check current guidance].
5. Give you a written response with the findings, the outcome and any action taken.

Full details of the procedure are in Parts 2 and 3 of the Standards. Follow them.

### 5.5 Protection from detriment

- The practice will not tolerate any bullying, harassment or other detriment towards anyone who raises a concern in good faith.
- Any such behaviour will be treated as a disciplinary matter.
- Staff who raise concerns are offered support throughout [practice decision: e.g. regular check-ins, employee assistance].
- Staff who are the subject of a concern are also treated fairly and supported.

### 5.7 If you are not satisfied

After the two-stage procedure, if you are not satisfied with how the concern was handled, the outcome, or how you were treated, you can ask the **INWO** to review it. If the practice did not allow a concern to be raised through the two-stage procedure, you can also ask the INWO to investigate that refusal and/or the concern.

## 6. Records and monitoring

### 6.1 Confidential record

The Raising Concerns contact keeps a **confidential record** of every whistleblowing concern and the action taken, using the PSM Raising Concerns Reporting Form (including INWO Whistleblowing Stage 1 and Stage 2). For both stages record:

- details of the concern;
- the date received;
- any request to keep it confidential;
- the outcome;
- actions taken.

For Stage 2 concerns, also record any actions taken during Stage 1. Full recording requirements are in Part 5 of the Standards.

Records are kept [location: secure, access limited to the Raising Concerns contact and Practice Owner] and separately from personnel files.

### 6.2 Reporting to NHS Fife

- Report whistleblowing concerns to NHS Fife **quarterly**, including the **10 key performance indicators** specified in the Standards.
- A quarterly report is **not** required if no whistleblowing concerns have been raised. However, an **annual report** must be submitted.
- Follow NHS Fife's reporting system and be aware of the information it may request [contact – check current].

The Practice Owner reviews this policy annually against the INWO checklist.
