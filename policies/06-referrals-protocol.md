## 1. Purpose

Effective communication between professionals is essential for good continuity of care. This protocol sets out how Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic makes routine and urgent referrals, how it follows them up, how it receives referrals (for example for implants) and how it shares information with other professionals.

## 2. Scope

- All dentists and dental care professionals who refer patients or receive referrals.
- Reception and administrative staff who send, track and file referrals.
- Referrals to NHS secondary care (NHS Fife hospital and specialist services), the Public Dental Service, specialist practitioners, general medical practitioners and other health and social care professionals.
- Referrals received from other dentists, for example for implant treatment.

## 3. Legislation and guidance

As cited in the PSM:

- General Dental Council, *Standards for the Dental Team* (2013) – communication and team working.
- Referrals between Scottish primary and secondary NHS care are made via the secure electronic **NHS SCI Gateway**.
- Radiographs should be shared to lessen the need for further exposures (see the radiation protection policy).

Other guidance the practice should check (not set out in the PSM page): the current national urgent suspected cancer referral guidance for Scotland, and NHS Fife referral criteria for oral surgery, orthodontics and restorative dentistry. Check current guidance.

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 Referral services list

1. The practice keeps an up-to-date list of referral services, with contact details and waiting times [location: shared drive / Dentally notes].
2. The list includes: NHS Fife oral and maxillofacial surgery, orthodontics, restorative dentistry, paediatric dentistry, the Public Dental Service, specialist practitioners, oral medicine, and urgent suspected cancer pathways. [Practice decision: complete names and contacts.]
3. The referrals administrator reviews the list [every 6 months] and when services change.

### 5.2 Before any referral

1. Explain to the patient why referral is needed, where they will be seen and the likely waiting time.
2. Obtain consent to the referral and to sharing information. Consent to an examination does not include consent to referral.
3. Record the discussion in Dentally.

### 5.3 Routine referrals

1. The clinician completes the referral within [5 working days – practice decision] of the decision to refer.
2. NHS referrals to secondary care go by **SCI Gateway**. Other referrals use the PSM Referral Form or the receiving service's own form, sent securely [practice decision: NHS.net email or secure portal].
3. Include:
   - patient name, date of birth, address, phone number and CHI number;
   - GP details;
   - referring clinician name, GDC number and practice details;
   - the reason for referral and the question to be answered;
   - relevant dental and medical history, medications and allergies;
   - findings and treatment so far;
   - copies of relevant radiographs and images (with dates), so that exposures are not repeated;
   - any additional needs (e.g. interpreter, access, anxiety, need for sedation);
   - whether the patient is NHS or private.
4. Log the referral (see 6.1).
5. Give the patient a copy or summary, and tell them what to do if they do not hear within [x weeks].
6. Continue to manage the patient's symptoms while they wait.

### 5.4 Urgent referrals

Urgent referrals include suspected oral cancer and other conditions needing rapid assessment [practice decision: list, e.g. suspicious lesions, spreading infection, suspected fractures].

1. The clinician makes the referral **the same day**, using the urgent suspected cancer pathway or urgent category on SCI Gateway.
2. Where the situation is an emergency (for example spreading facial swelling affecting the airway or eye), phone the on-call service at NHS Fife or advise the patient to attend A&E or call 999. Record the call: who was spoken to, time and advice.
3. Tell the patient clearly, and sensitively, why the referral is urgent and when they are likely to be seen.
4. Confirm the patient's phone number and address are correct.
5. The referrals administrator checks for an acknowledgement within [2 working days]. If none, chase the receiving service.
6. Check the patient has been given an appointment within [2 weeks] for suspected cancer [check current national target]. Record each follow-up.
7. The referring clinician is named in the log as responsible for following up to make sure the patient is seen.

### 5.5 Following up referrals

1. The referrals administrator reviews the referral log [weekly].
2. Chase any referral not acknowledged or not appointed within the expected time.
3. When a reply or report is received, the referring clinician reads it, acts on it and records it in Dentally.
4. If the patient does not attend the hospital appointment, contact them and record the outcome.

### 5.6 Receiving referrals (including implant referrals)

1. Acknowledge receipt to the referring practitioner [within 5 working days – practice decision].
2. Tell the patient when they will be seen.
3. After assessment, write to the referrer with findings and the proposed plan. After treatment, send a summary, including any maintenance needs for implants.
4. Make clear whether the patient will return to the referrer for routine care.

### 5.7 Communication with other professionals

1. Keep records legible and comprehensible so that others can understand them.
2. Share radiographs and images rather than re-exposing patients where possible.
3. Contact the patient's GP or pharmacist when needed, for example about medical history or medication. Record the conversation.
4. Share only the information needed, securely, and in line with the data protection policy.
5. Where there are safeguarding concerns, follow the safeguarding policy and contact the Safeguarding Lead.

## 6. Records and monitoring

### 6.1 Referral log

The practice keeps a referral log [Dentally referral tracking or a spreadsheet – practice decision] recording:

- patient name and CHI;
- date of referral, routine or urgent;
- service referred to and method (SCI Gateway, form, email);
- referring clinician (responsible for follow-up);
- date acknowledged, date of appointment, date report received;
- follow-up actions.

### 6.2 Monitoring

- Copies of referrals and replies are kept in the patient's Dentally record.
- [Practice decision: audit referrals every 12 months for completeness and timeliness, especially urgent referrals.]
- Any delay that may have caused harm is reviewed as a significant event and considered under `02-duty-of-candour-procedure.md`.

## 7. Training

- All referring clinicians are set up on SCI Gateway and trained to use it.
- Reception staff are trained in logging and chasing referrals.
- Clinicians keep up to date on referral criteria and oral cancer recognition through CPD.
