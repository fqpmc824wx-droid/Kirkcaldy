## 1. Purpose

All dental team members are in a position to notice signs of abuse or neglect and to support a child's or adult's wellbeing. This policy sets out how Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic recognises, records and shares concerns about children and adults at risk of harm, in line with NHS Fife and Fife Child Protection Committee procedures.

A child protection policy is an essential requirement for the NHS Combined Practice Inspection. The PSM does not provide a child protection policy template because procedures are set by each Health Board and Child Protection Committee. **This policy must be checked against the current Fife procedures before adoption.**

## 2. Scope

- All staff, including clinicians, nurses, reception, management, locums, trainees and volunteers.
- All children (under 18) and all adults who may be at risk of harm who use the practice, or who come with patients.
- Concerns arising inside or outside the practice, including about a member of the dental team.

Team members are **not** responsible for diagnosing abuse or neglect. They **are** responsible for sharing concerns appropriately and following local procedures.

### 5.3 Recognising signs of child abuse or neglect

There are four categories:

- **Physical abuse** – hitting, shaking, throwing, poisoning, burning or scalding, drowning, suffocating or otherwise causing physical harm; also fabricating or deliberately causing illness.
- **Emotional abuse** – persistent emotional maltreatment causing severe and persistent effects on emotional development; including bullying and online bullying.
- **Sexual abuse** – forcing or enticing a child to take part in sexual activities, whether or not the child is aware of what is happening.
- **Neglect** – persistent failure to meet a child's basic physical and/or psychological needs, likely to seriously impair health or development. Faltering growth may be linked to chronic neglect.
  - **Dental neglect** is a failure to respond to a known significant dental problem.

Concerns can come to the dental team through:

- a direct allegation or disclosure by the child, a parent or another person
- signs and symptoms seen by a team member that suggest physical abuse or neglect (for example facial, oral or dental injuries with an inconsistent explanation; untreated decay or pain despite advice)
- the child's behaviour or the interaction between parent/carer and child.

Take every concern seriously, however it arises.

### 5.4 What to do if you are worried about a child

**If a child is in immediate danger, phone 999.**

If there is reasonable concern that a child might be at risk of significant harm, this **always overrides** the duty to keep information confidential. The main responsibility is to the child.

1. **Follow the Fife child protection procedures.**
2. **Listen and talk to the child.** Let them volunteer information and record their responses in their own words. Do not ask leading questions.
3. **Do not promise to keep a secret.** Explain that you might have to share the information, and tell the child who will be told and when.
4. **Clinician: examine and record.** Conduct a full dental examination. Note the child's general appearance and behaviour and their interaction with the parent/carer.
5. **Document fully** (see 5.5).
6. **Do not jump to conclusions.** A diagnosis of physical abuse is never made on one sign: some diseases can be mistaken for abuse, and injuries can have other causes (for example playground fights).
7. **Do not act alone.** Share your concern the same day with the Safeguarding Lead (or, if unavailable, the deputy or a senior experienced colleague), or seek advice from the NHS Fife child protection team, a social worker, child protection nurse or doctor.
8. **If you still have concerns**, the clinician (with the Safeguarding Lead) explains the concern to the child and parent/carer, says that you intend to make a referral to local services, and seeks consent to share information.
9. **Exception**: on the rare occasion that talking to the parent/carer is likely to put the child at greater risk (for example child sexual abuse or fabricated or induced illness), or might hamper a police investigation or social work enquiry, or if consent to share is withheld – discuss with the indemnity provider or senior colleagues before proceeding. Where the child is at risk of significant harm, a referral can be made without consent.
10. **Refer** to Fife Council Social Work or the police, following the Fife procedure. Phone referrals are followed up in writing as the procedure requires [form/route].
11. **Medical examination**: if required, refer the child to a medical practitioner for physical examination.
12. **Dental neglect**: follow the BSPD staged approach – raise concerns with the parent, offer support and a clear plan, monitor attendance, and escalate (for example to the health visitor, school nurse, GP or social work) if there is no improvement. [Practice decision: when to involve the health visitor/school nurse.]
13. Tell the Safeguarding Lead what was done and record it in the concerns log.

### 5.5 Recording

Record in Dentally (clinical notes) and in the confidential safeguarding concerns log:

- the history, including what the child and parent/carer said happened, in their words
- details of any injury or presenting complaint, using diagrams or body maps that colleagues can understand
- past dental and medical history, and family and social circumstances where known
- reasons given for seemingly trivial injuries (over time these may show a pattern)
- changes or inconsistencies in the history, or between the history and the examination
- delays in seeking care, missed or cancelled appointments ("was not brought")
- signs of inappropriate development, and any previous concerns about the child or siblings
- who you discussed the concern with, advice given, decisions and actions, with dates and times
- clearly separate **facts** from **opinion**.

Records are built up over time so that, even if no single team member knows the child well, a picture forms. Use the National Guidance list of information to record. Safeguarding records are kept securely and shared only with those who need to know.

### 5.6 Adult support and protection

An "adult at risk" may be unable to protect themselves from harm because of disability, illness, mental health, infirmity or capacity [check the current definition in Fife adult protection guidance]. Harm includes physical, psychological, financial and sexual harm, neglect and self-harm. All adults are "protected adults" for PVG purposes while receiving dental treatment.

Signs may include unexplained injuries, fearfulness or withdrawal, poor hygiene or untreated pain where a carer is responsible, a carer refusing to let the adult be seen alone, or sudden unexplained changes in finances (for example a third party pressing for expensive private treatment, or not allowing needed treatment).

What to do:

1. **Immediate danger: phone 999.**
2. Listen; do not ask leading questions; do not promise confidentiality.
3. Where possible, speak to the adult alone and ask what they would like to happen. Respect their wishes where they have capacity, unless others are at risk or there is a serious crime.
4. Record as in 5.5.
5. Share the concern the same day with the Safeguarding Lead.
6. Refer to Fife Council Adult Social Work [phone] or the police, following the Fife adult protection procedure. [Check current guidance: the 2007 Act places duties on public bodies, including health bodies, to co-operate and report concerns.]
7. If the adult may lack capacity for treatment decisions, follow the Adults with Incapacity procedures in 03-consent-policy.md.

### 5.7 Concerns about a member of staff

Dental teams have an ethical duty to make sure children are not at risk from members of their own profession.

1. Report any concern about a colleague's behaviour towards children or adults at once to the Safeguarding Lead or Practice Owner. If the concern is about one of them, go to the other or to the NHS Fife Dental Practice Adviser, or use 07-raising-concerns-whistleblowing.md.
2. Follow the Fife procedure; the police or social work may need to be informed.
3. Consider whether the GDC must be informed.
4. If the person is dismissed, would have been dismissed, or is permanently moved away from regulated work because of harmful behaviour, the practice **must** make a referral to Disclosure Scotland within three months (see 13-pvg-scheme-and-safe-recruitment.md).

### 5.8 Confidentiality and information sharing

- There is no data protection barrier to reporting suspected abuse of a child or adult at risk.
- Share only what is necessary, with those who need to know, and record what was shared, with whom and why.
- Safeguarding information is never discussed in public areas.
