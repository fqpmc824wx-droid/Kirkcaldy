## 1. Purpose

This policy makes sure that any CCTV at Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic is used lawfully, fairly and only for clear, justified reasons, and that the privacy of staff, patients and visitors is respected.

## 2. Scope

- All CCTV and video surveillance equipment at 23–25 Tolbooth Street, including cameras, recorders, cloud storage and viewing apps.
- Everyone recorded: staff, patients, people accompanying patients, contractors and the public.
- Everyone who installs, manages or views the system.

## 3. Legislation and guidance

- Data Protection Act 2018 (DPA 2018)
- UK General Data Protection Regulation (UK GDPR) – CCTV images of identifiable people are personal data
- ICO guidance on video surveillance (including CCTV) and the ICO CCTV checklist

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Reasons for using CCTV

Individuals attending the practice have a right to privacy. The practice must consider the impact on them and take account of their concerns. We must be able to explain our reason. Our purposes are: [for example – to deter and record aggression towards reception staff; to protect the premises and equipment from crime outside opening hours; delete or add as appropriate].

CCTV will not be used for other purposes, such as routine monitoring of staff performance.

### 5.2 Before CCTV is used (all must be done)

1. **ICO**: the practice is registered with the ICO as a data controller and the annual data protection fee is paid.
2. **DPIA**: if CCTV is likely to result in a high risk to individuals (for example continuous monitoring of staff), a Data Protection Impact Assessment is required. [Practice decision: we will complete a DPIA for any CCTV installation.] The DPIA describes the processing, assesses risks to individuals, identifies how risks will be reduced, and records the outcome.
3. **Signs**: display a sign or poster where people can see it before entering the recorded area, stating that CCTV is in use, the reasons, and who to contact with queries (Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic, info@kirkcaldydental.co.uk, [phone]).
4. **Camera positions**: check each camera angle so it records only what is intended. **No CCTV in private areas** such as toilets and changing rooms. [Practice decision: no cameras in surgeries or the decontamination room.] Avoid recording neighbouring property or the street beyond what is necessary.
5. **Audio**: turn off or disable any sound recording. Audio is recorded only in exceptional circumstances that can be justified (none currently).
6. **Privacy notices**: update the patient and staff privacy notices (11-privacy-notices.md) to say CCTV is used, why, the lawful basis, and the retention period.
7. **Policy**: this CCTV policy is completed and signed. Use the ICO checklist for a CCTV policy.
8. **Retention**: decide the retention period and record it here and in the privacy notices. Retention period: **[number] days** [practice decision – keep as short as needed for the purpose]. Images are not kept longer than this unless needed for a specific incident (see 5.4). There is a process for deletion: [automatic overwrite after [x] days].
9. **Security**: images are kept secure (for example password protected, encrypted, recorder in a locked area) and only designated people can view them. Default passwords on the recorder and any app are changed; two-factor authentication on any cloud viewing app.
10. **Retrieval**: the system must allow images to be found and exported, because people have a right to a copy of their personal information, including CCTV images.

### 5.3 Day-to-day operation

1. Only authorised viewers may view live or recorded images, and only for the stated purposes.
2. Monitors are not visible to patients or the public.
3. The Data Protection Lead checks [monthly] that the date and time are correct, cameras work and point where intended, and signs are in place, and records the check.
4. Access to the system (who has logins) is reviewed [6-monthly] and when staff leave.

### 5.4 Viewing, copying and disclosing footage

1. Every viewing or export of recorded footage is recorded in the **CCTV access log**: date, who viewed, reason, what was viewed, whether copied, to whom given.
2. Footage linked to an incident (for example an assault on staff) may be kept beyond the normal retention period until the matter is concluded, then deleted. Record this.
3. **Police requests**: follow the disclosure procedure in 10-data-protection-confidentiality-information-security.md (written request, identity check, decision by the Practice Owner, only what is necessary, record in the disclosure register).
4. **Subject access requests**: anyone recorded can ask for a copy of footage of themselves, free of charge, within one month. Verify identity. Obscure (blur) other identifiable people where needed before release, or explain why their images cannot be disclosed. Follow the SAR procedure in policy 10.
5. Footage is never posted on social media or shared informally.

### 5.5 Breaches and concerns

1. If images are viewed by someone not authorised, lost, or shared wrongly, this is a personal data breach. Report it to the Data Protection Lead immediately. The ICO must be notified where required, within 72 hours (see policy 10).
2. Staff (and others) can contact the ICO if they feel CCTV is being used unfairly. They can also raise concerns internally (07-raising-concerns-whistleblowing.md).

### 5.6 Removal or change

Any new camera, change of position or new purpose requires the DPIA, signs and privacy notices to be reviewed first. If CCTV is removed, delete stored images securely and remove signs.
