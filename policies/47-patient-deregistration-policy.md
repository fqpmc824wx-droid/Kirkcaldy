## 1. Purpose
To set out fairly and clearly when and how a patient may be deregistered from Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic, in line with NHS (Scotland) rules and the standards used across the Saint Visage Dental Group.

## 2. Scope
All patients registered at the practice (NHS and private).

## 3. Reasons for deregistration
The practice may deregister a patient for:
- **Persistent late cancellations and missed appointments:** cancelling with less than 48 hours' notice on **three** occasions **and** failing to attend **two** appointments without notice.
- **Abusive, threatening or violent behaviour** towards staff or other patients (see 04-zero-tolerance-violent-patients.md).
- **Non-payment** of charges due.

Deregistration is never used because of a patient's age, health, disability, treatment needs, a complaint they have made, or any protected characteristic.

## 4. Before deciding
- Check the record: dates of late cancellations and missed appointments, and any reasons given.
- Consider whether the patient is vulnerable, a child, or has a health or social reason (a pattern of missed appointments for a child may be a safeguarding concern – see policy 12).
- Where possible, the patient will already have been told about the rule (leaflet, website, reminder letter).
- The Practice Owner authorises the decision.

## 5. Violence or threats
Patients who are violent, or who make the dentist or staff fear for their safety, will be reported to the police, and a copy of all communication will be kept. In serious cases deregistration may be immediate, following NHS rules and policy 04.

## 6. Notification
- The patient is told of the decision by phone or email, and written confirmation is sent **within 7 days**.
- For NHS patients, the practice informs **NHS Fife** using the required process and gives the notice period required under the NHS (General Dental Services) (Scotland) Regulations [check the current notice period with NHS Fife – usually 3 months].
- The letter explains the reason, the date registration ends, how to appeal, and how to find another dentist (Fife Dental Advice Line 01592 226555).

## 7. Emergency care during the notice period
After the deregistration notice is issued, the patient remains entitled to emergency dental care from the practice for **three months**. After that, they need to find another dental service.

## 8. Appeals
A patient may appeal in writing to the Practice Manager **within 14 days** of the notice, giving: full name, date of birth, contact details, and the grounds for appeal. The Practice Manager (with the Practice Owner) reviews the appeal and replies in writing **within 14 days**. Patients can also use the complaints procedure (policy 01).

## 9. Records and data protection
Deregistration notices and correspondence are kept in the patient record and handled under UK GDPR and the Data Protection Act 2018; access is limited to authorised staff.

## 10. Communication
This policy is given to patients on registration, is available at reception on request, and is published on the practice website.
