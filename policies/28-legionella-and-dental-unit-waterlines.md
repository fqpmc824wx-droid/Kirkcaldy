## 1. Purpose

Legionella bacteria can grow in water systems and cause Legionnaires' disease, a potentially fatal pneumonia, usually by breathing in small droplets of contaminated water. Legionella thrive between **20°C and 45°C**. In dental practices, **dental unit waterlines (DUWLs)** carry a particular risk from biofilm.

It is a legal requirement to carry out a legionella risk assessment and to put in place a written control scheme. This policy sets out who is responsible, how the risk is assessed and controlled, how DUWLs are managed, what is recorded and what happens if control fails.

## 2. Scope

- All water systems at 23–25 Tolbooth Street: cold water supply and any storage tanks, hot water generation and storage, pipework, sinks and taps, toilets, any showers, air-conditioning units, and the LDU water supply (including any RO/distilled water unit).
- All dental chair units and DUWLs (including water bottles, handpiece and 3-in-1 lines, ultrasonic scaler lines and cup fillers) and any surgical irrigation equipment used for implants.
- All staff, contractors and visitors.

The practice owns the premises, so the practice is the dutyholder for the whole water system.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Risk assessment

1. Before the practice opens, the dutyholder commissions a **legionella risk assessment** from a competent person to **BS 8580-1:2019**. It **must include the dental chair units and DUWLs**.
2. The risk assessment considers the Health Protection Scotland criteria:
   1. Is water stored or recirculated as part of the system?
   2. Is the water temperature in all or part of the system between 20°C and 45°C?
   3. Are there deposits that support bacterial growth (e.g. biofilm)?
   4. Can water droplets be produced and dispersed (e.g. aerosols)?
   Any of 1–3 together with 4 means there could be a foreseeable risk, heightened if staff or patients are more susceptible (e.g. over 45, smokers, heavy drinkers, chronic respiratory or kidney disease, impaired immunity).
3. The risk assessment includes: management responsibilities and the responsible person; a description of the water system; potential risk sources; existing controls; monitoring, inspection and maintenance procedures; records; and the date for review and triggers for an unscheduled review.
4. If any part of the system cannot be accessed (e.g. because of the fabric of the building), this is recorded and additional controls put in place.
5. The risk assessment is **reviewed regularly** [practice decision: at least every [2] years – confirm with the assessor] and **straight away** if:
   - the water system changes (e.g. new surgery, new chair, plumbing work);
   - checks show control measures are no longer effective;
   - there is a significant legionella colonisation, or a suspected or confirmed case linked to the practice.

### 5.2 Written control scheme

The written control scheme [held in the legionella file] sets out:

1. A description of the water system (pipework, pumps, valves, cold water storage, hot water generation/storage, sinks, toilets, showers, air conditioning, DUWLs).
2. The person responsible for the risk assessment and for managing the scheme.
3. Significant findings of the risk assessment and any changes made to remove risks.
4. Safe and correct operation of the system.
5. Control measures and how they are monitored (e.g. using a **calibrated thermometer**, and how often).
6. Checks to confirm risks are managed, and their frequency.
7. An **action plan** if the scheme proves ineffective or there is significant colonisation, including who is notified (NHS Fife contact).

### 5.3 Control measures

The practice minimises the release of droplets and keeps water conditions that do not favour legionella:

1. **Minimise spray and aerosol**: high-volume aspiration at the chair; lower-speed handpieces where clinically suitable.
2. **Temperature control**: cold water below 20°C; hot water stored at **at least 60°C** and distributed so it reaches **55°C within one minute** at outlets (health care setting) – HSE recommendation. [Practice decision: if point-of-use or thermostatic mixing valves are fitted, record the arrangements in the scheme.]
3. **Prevent stagnation**: run all hot and cold outlets each day; follow the DUWL flushing and draining regime; remove redundant pipework and dead legs; flush little-used outlets (e.g. a spare toilet or shower) at least weekly [confirm with risk assessor].
4. **Suitable materials**: use fittings, materials and appliances approved for use in the UK (WRAS).
5. **Keep systems clean**: anti-retraction valves on instruments attached to the DUWL/chair; inspect DUWL bottles each day when filling or decontaminating; filters as specified.
6. **Water treatment**: biocide in DUWLs and dental chair units as recommended by the manufacturer.

### 5.5 Dental unit waterlines (DUWLs)

Water is delivered to the chairs by [practice decision: self-contained bottle system / mains feed via holding tank – depends on chair model [make/model]]. Procedures follow the chair and biocide manufacturers' instructions. Wear appropriate PPE (gloves, eye protection) throughout.

**Start of each day**

1. Check the water bottle and fittings; fill with [fresh water type specified by manufacturer, e.g. RO/distilled/freshly drawn] and the recommended biocide dose.
2. **Flush each DUWL** (handpiece lines, 3-in-1, scaler, cup filler) **for 2–4 minutes** with instruments removed, into the spittoon or aspirator.
3. Record on the daily DUWL record.

**Between patients**

4. **Flush each DUWL used for 30 seconds** with the instrument attached, to remove material that may have been drawn back.

**End of each day**

5. **Flush each DUWL for 2–4 minutes**.
6. Remove, empty, clean and decontaminate the water bottle as the manufacturer instructs, and **store it dry and inverted overnight** [or as manufacturer instructs]. Purge lines with air if the chair allows.
7. Record completion.

**Biocide**

8. Use the biocide [product] at the dose and frequency recommended by the dental chair unit and biocide manufacturers, including any weekly or periodic shock-dose treatment. Record each treatment.

**Periods of non-use**

9. Before weekends, holidays or other significant periods of non-use, drain and/or treat DUWLs as the manufacturer instructs. On return, flush thoroughly (and shock-dose if instructed) before treating patients. [Practice decision: define "significant period" – e.g. more than [3] days.]

**Surgical procedures**

10. Implant and other surgical procedures use **sterile irrigant** delivered through a separate single-use or sterilizable system, not the DUWL.

**Monitoring**

11. [Practice decision: the chair manufacturer's recommended DUWL water quality testing, e.g. dip-slide testing at [frequency] – check current HPS guidance.] Microbiological testing is not usually needed but **must** be done when control deficiencies are found or there is doubt about efficacy (e.g. water develops an unpleasant odour or taste, routine failure to meet temperature targets, or a significant legionella breach).

### 5.6 When to notify NHS Fife

The Practice Owner confirms with NHS Fife when and to whom the practice should report legionella contamination [contact name and number]. Examples include:

- the water temperature **consistently fails** to meet the parameters in the written control scheme;
- **prolonged failure** to carry out control measures in the scheme, such as flushing DUWLs.

### 5.7 Action if control fails

1. Any member of staff who notices a problem (no hot water, water too warm from the cold tap, odour, discolouration, missed flushing, biocide not available) tells the responsible person the same day.
2. The responsible person investigates and starts remedial action (e.g. repair, extra flushing, shock-dosing, temporary withdrawal of a chair).
3. If there is an **increased risk of colonisation**, or a **suspected or confirmed case** of legionella infection, follow the action plan in the written control scheme, including **notifying NHS Fife without delay**. Arrange microbiological testing through the water contractor.
4. Record the event and actions; review the risk assessment; handle as a significant event (see 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md).
