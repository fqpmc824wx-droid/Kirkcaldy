## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how the practice prevents fire, assesses fire risk, and makes sure everyone in the building can get out safely if there is a fire or other emergency. It includes our written fire emergency and evacuation procedure.

## 2. Scope

It covers the whole of 23–25 Tolbooth Street and everyone in it: staff, self-employed clinicians, patients (including sedated patients and people with disabilities), visitors and contractors. It applies at all times the building is in use, including early, late and weekend sessions.

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 Fire safety risk assessment

The Fire Safety Lead completes a written fire safety risk assessment using the Scottish Government forms and guidance, **before the practice opens**. Where there are five or more employees, the significant findings must be recorded and available for SFRS inspection; we record them regardless of staff numbers.

The five stages are:

1. **Identify the people at risk** – staff, patients and visitors, and where they are. Note people at extra risk: patients under sedation or local anaesthetic, wheelchair users, people with sight or hearing loss, children, anyone working alone.
2. **Identify the fire hazards** – the three elements needed for a fire:
   - ignition: electrical equipment, lighting, kitchen appliances, heaters, smoking, open flames, any laser or electrosurgery equipment;
   - fuel: paper, records, packaging, waste, alcohol-based hand rub and wipes, flammable liquids and gases;
   - oxygen: air, ventilation and air conditioning, draughts, and **medical oxygen cylinders**.
3. **Evaluate the risk** and decide if existing measures are enough. Consider the likelihood of a fire and the consequences for people. Apply the risk reduction principles (required by law): avoid risks; evaluate risks that cannot be avoided; combat risks at source; adapt to technical progress; replace the dangerous with the non- or less dangerous; develop a coherent fire prevention policy; give collective measures priority over individual measures; give appropriate instruction to employees.
4. **Record** the findings and the action plan (owner and date for each action).
5. **Review** regularly – at least annually [practice decision] – and whenever there are significant changes to the premises, layout, equipment, opening hours or staffing.

### 5.2 Fire prevention

1. No smoking or vaping anywhere in the building or at the entrance.
2. Electrical safety checks are done as set out in 17-health-and-safety-policy.md. No overloaded sockets or daisy-chained extension leads.
3. Waste is not allowed to build up; bins are stored away from escape routes and the building (22-waste-management-policy.md).
4. Flammable products are kept in small amounts in a closed cupboard away from heat (18-coshh-policy.md).
5. **Medical oxygen** is stored under cover, dry and clean, away from combustible material and away from heat, with "no smoking, no naked flames" notices at the store. The location of the cylinder store is notified to the emergency services. Location: [location].
6. Lasers, if used, have fire prevention measures in their local rules.
7. Last person out checks that appliances are off and fire doors are closed.

### 5.5 Fire emergency and evacuation procedure

Displayed as the fire action notice and taught to all staff.

**If you discover a fire:**
1. Raise the alarm at once – operate the nearest call point [or shout "Fire!" / ring the bell].
2. Call 999 (Scottish Fire and Rescue Service). Give the address: **Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic, 23–25 Tolbooth Street, Kirkcaldy KY1 1RW**. Tell them if oxygen cylinders are on site.
3. Only tackle a fire if it is small, you are trained, you have the right extinguisher, and your escape route is behind you. If in doubt, get out.

**When the alarm sounds:**
1. Clinicians stop treatment safely: remove instruments and rubber dam from the mouth, sit the patient up, and lead them out by the nearest safe exit. A patient under sedation is accompanied by the clinician and nurse at all times.
2. Do not stop to collect belongings. Do not use the lift [if there is one].
3. Close doors behind you to slow the fire. Turn off equipment only if safe and quick.
4. Fire wardens check their areas (surgeries, toilets, LDU, staff room, store) on the way out if safe, and report to the person in charge.
5. The receptionist takes the day list from Dentally (printed or on a device), the staff rota and the visitors/contractors book.
6. Everyone goes to the assembly point and stays there. The receptionist and fire wardens do a roll call of staff, patients and visitors.
7. The person in charge [Fire Safety Lead or most senior person present] meets the fire service and tells them if anyone is missing and where they might be, and where the oxygen is stored.
8. Nobody goes back in until the fire service says it is safe.

**People who need help to evacuate.** Reception notes in Dentally (alert) any patient who would need help to escape. Each such patient has a named member of staff to assist them during their visit. [Practice decision: personal emergency evacuation plans (PEEPs) for staff who need them; evacuation chair if any treatment rooms are upstairs, with training.] Audiovisual alarms or other aids are considered in the risk assessment. See 09-disability-equality-reasonable-adjustments.md.

**After the event.** Record the incident, review what happened, report as needed (17-health-and-safety-policy.md – an electrical fire is a RIDDOR dangerous occurrence), and use the business continuity plan (38-business-continuity-plan.md).

### 5.6 Fire drills

1. A fire drill is held **at least once a year** [practice decision: twice a year, with one during a patient session]. Patients are told in advance where practical.
2. The Fire Safety Lead times the evacuation, notes problems and records the drill in the fire log book. Actions are followed up.

### 5.7 Contractors and hot work

Contractors sign in at reception and are told the fire procedure and assembly point. No hot work (welding, soldering, blowtorches) without the Fire Safety Lead's permission and a check of the area afterwards.

## 6. Records and monitoring

The Fire Safety Lead keeps a **fire log book** [location] with records of:
- staff fire training (who, when, what);
- fire drills (date, time taken, problems, actions);
- checks, maintenance and servicing of fire detection and warning equipment (including weekly alarm tests);
- firefighting equipment (extinguisher service certificates);
- fire doors;
- emergency lighting.

The fire safety risk assessment and action plan are kept in the fire log book and reviewed annually or after significant change. Records are available for SFRS inspection.

## 7. Training

- Fire training at induction (before a new starter works with patients): fire hazards, the procedure, alarm points, exits, assembly point, roles.
- All staff are trained in when and how to use the fire extinguishers we have.
- Fire wardens and the Fire Safety Lead receive additional training [provider].
- Refresher training [annually – practice decision], plus the annual drill.
- Staff assisting with evacuation chairs (if provided) are trained to use them.
