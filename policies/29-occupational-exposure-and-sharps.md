## 1. Purpose

Inoculation injuries can expose staff to blood and body fluids that may carry bloodborne viruses (BBVs) such as hepatitis B, hepatitis C and HIV. The most common is the **sharps injury** from needles (needlestick) or other sharp objects, for example during treatment or instrument decontamination. Splashes of blood or body fluids to the eyes, mouth or broken skin are also inoculation risks.

This policy sets out how the practice prevents exposure, and exactly what to do if an injury or splash happens: first aid, reporting, risk assessment of the source patient, Occupational Health follow-up, recording and learning.

**Quick reference – post this in every surgery and the LDU (see 5.4 and the box at the end).**

## 2. Scope

All staff, including dentists, implant surgeons, DCPs, dental nurses, trainees, reception staff who may handle sharps containers or specimens, cleaners and locums. It covers all sharps (needles, scalpel blades, sutures, burs, endodontic files, matrix bands, orthodontic wires, scaler tips, implant drills, broken glass) and all splash exposures, in the practice and on domiciliary visits.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Risk assessment

1. The IPC Lead carries out a **risk assessment following COSHH principles** for exposure to blood and body fluids, covering each task that uses sharps (local anaesthetic, surgery and implant placement, suturing, endodontics, scaling, instrument decontamination, disposal).
2. The risk assessment is reviewed [annually] and after any injury.

### 5.2 Prevention

1. **Avoid unnecessary use of sharps.** Do not use a medical sharp unless the task requires it and there is no alternative.
2. If a medical sharp is needed, use **safer sharps** where reasonably practicable, for example safety syringe systems for local anaesthetic. [Practice decision: record which safer sharps are used and, where a conventional sharp is used, why.]
3. **Cover any breaks in your skin** with a waterproof dressing.
4. Wear **appropriate PPE** (gloves, eye protection, mask/visor, apron) for treatment and decontamination (see 23-personal-protective-equipment-policy.md).
5. **Avoid recapping needles.** If recapping is assessed as the safest option, use a **suitable device** that allows safe **one-handed** recapping [device used]. Never recap two-handed.
6. The **user of the sharp is responsible for its disposal** immediately after use, at the point of use. Sharps are never passed hand to hand.
7. Do not bend or break needles before disposal. Remove matrix bands, files and blades from instruments with forceps or a removal device, not fingers.
8. Hand-scrubbing of sharp instruments is avoided; the washer-disinfector is the preferred cleaning method (see 26-decontamination-of-reusable-instruments.md).
9. Make sure all staff who may be exposed have been offered **hepatitis B vaccination** and know their status (see 30-health-clearance-and-immunisation.md).

### 5.3 Sharps containers

Sharps boxes are available **as close to the work area as practicable** (each surgery and the LDU). Each sharps box must:

- be **wall-mounted or placed on a stable surface at waist height**;
- have a **visible aperture** when disposing of sharps;
- have a **temporary closure mechanism**, used when not in use;
- be **labelled with the date of assembly and closure, and point of origin** (e.g. "Surgery 1", practice name);
- be **disposed of when the "max fill" line is reached**, locked and stored securely for collection (see 22-waste-management-policy.md).

**Never try to retrieve an item from a sharps container.** Never press down on the contents.

### 5.4 If a sharps injury occurs – first aid (do this immediately)

1. **Stop** what you are doing and make sure the sharp object is **disposed of safely**.
2. **Encourage bleeding** by squeezing above the wound (not directly on it). **Do not suck or scrub** the wound.
3. **Wash** the injury thoroughly with **warm running water and soap**.
4. **Cover** the injury with a **waterproof plaster**.

### 5.5 If the eyes, mouth or damaged skin are splashed with blood or body fluids

1. **Rinse/irrigate with copious amounts of water.** Water used for mouth rinsing **must not be swallowed**.
2. If **contact lenses** are worn, **remove the lens and then irrigate** the eye.
3. An eyewash station / sterile eyewash pods are kept in [location] in each surgery and the LDU.

### 5.6 Reporting and follow-up (the same session)

Speed matters, because post-exposure treatment may be time-critical. Follow these steps straight after first aid:

1. **Report the injury to the Practice Owner / employer** (or, if absent, [deputy]). The patient's treatment should be completed safely by a colleague if possible.
2. **Record the details accurately in the practice accident book**: date, time, place, name of injured person, how it happened, the type of sharp or fluid, whether the sharp was used/contaminated, first aid given, the source patient's identifier (not in the accident book entry itself if this would breach confidentiality – record a reference instead), and witnesses.
3. **Refer to the clinical record of the source patient** to identify any risk factors (e.g. known HIV, hepatitis B, hepatitis C). The treating dentist may ask the source patient, sensitively and with their consent, about risk factors and whether they would agree to a blood test through Occupational Health or their GP. The patient's information remains confidential.
4. **Contact the local Occupational Health Service (OHS) without delay** to discuss the incident and ask for advice and follow-up, for example post-exposure prophylaxis, hepatitis B booster, and **serological surveillance**.
   - NHS Fife Occupational Health Service: [phone number, hours]
   - Out of hours: [NHS Fife emergency department – Victoria Hospital, Kirkcaldy / NHS 24 111 – confirm the route with NHS Fife OHS]
5. Where there is a risk of exposure to BBVs or other serious infection, **arrange counselling** if the injured person feels it would help.
6. **Investigate** the incident (IPC Lead) to see whether changes to practice would reduce the risk of future injuries, and **amend procedures**.
7. **Inform staff** of any changes to procedures (practice meeting; record in minutes).
8. **Report to HSE under RIDDOR** if required (Practice Owner as the responsible person – see 5.7).

### 5.7 RIDDOR – when a sharps injury is reportable

As set out in the PSM RIDDOR page:

| Situation | RIDDOR status |
|---|---|
| Employee injured by a sharp **known to be contaminated with a BBV** (e.g. hepatitis B or C, HIV) | Reportable as a **dangerous occurrence** |
| Employee receives a sharps injury and **sero-converts** to a BBV acquired this way | Reportable as a **disease** |
| Sharp **not contaminated** with a BBV, or source **cannot be traced** | Not reportable, **unless** the injury causes over-7-day incapacitation, or the employee develops a disease attributable to the injury |

Reports are made using the HSE online form. All reportable incidents are also entered in the accident book.

### 5.8 Injured person who is a self-employed clinician

Self-employed associates and locums follow the same first aid and reporting steps. They are responsible for their own OHS follow-up [practice decision: confirm NHS Fife OHS eligibility for self-employed dentists with an NHS list number], and the practice records the incident in its accident book and investigates.

## 7. Training

- All staff are trained at induction in: the risks from inoculation injuries; prevention (safer sharps, no recapping, disposal at point of use, sharps containers); first aid; and reporting requirements.
- Staff know where the quick-reference poster, eyewash and accident book are.
- Training is refreshed [annually] and after any incident that leads to a change in procedure.

## Quick reference poster: sharps or splash injury

**1. First aid – now**

- Sharps injury: stop; dispose of the sharp safely; **encourage bleeding** by squeezing above the wound – **do not suck or scrub**; **wash** with warm running water and soap; **cover** with a waterproof plaster.
- Splash to eyes, mouth or broken skin: **rinse with plenty of water** – do not swallow; remove contact lenses, then irrigate.

**2. Tell** the Practice Owner [Nas] or [deputy] straight away.

**3. Phone** NHS Fife Occupational Health: [number]. Out of hours: [route].

**4. Record** in the accident book.
