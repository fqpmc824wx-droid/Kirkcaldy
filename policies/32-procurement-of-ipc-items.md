## 1. Purpose

For effective infection prevention and control, including decontamination, new items and equipment must be of suitable quality and fit for purpose. Reusable items must be compatible with the decontamination methods the practice uses. Single-use items must never be reprocessed.

This policy sets out how the practice chooses and buys reusable instruments, single-use items, decontamination equipment and IPC products (e.g. hand hygiene products, PPE, wipes, disinfectants). It matters especially now, as the practice is equipping its surgeries and LDU before opening.

## 2. Scope

- Reusable instruments and devices (hand instruments, handpieces, implant surgical kits and drivers, impression trays, etc.).
- Single-use items (e.g. endodontic files, matrix bands, plastic impression trays, prophy cups and brushes, face masks, implant drills where specified as single use).
- Decontamination equipment: washer-disinfectors, ultrasonic cleaners, sterilizers, handpiece maintenance units, water treatment units, heat sealers, data loggers.
- Dental chairs and other equipment with surfaces to be cleaned, and DUWL systems.
- IPC consumables: hand hygiene products, PPE, wipes, disinfectants, biocides, spill kits, sharps containers, transport boxes.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Before buying – set out the requirement

1. The IPC Lead writes down the **practice's specific requirements** for the item (what it must do, volume of use, compatibility, space, services such as water, drainage and power).
2. Obtain **price comparisons** and **advice from several manufacturers or suppliers**. Record these in the procurement file.
3. For decontamination equipment, buy through suppliers on the **NP 143 framework** listed in SHTM 01-05 Part A where possible, and consider using the **Health Facilities Scotland advisory service**. [Practice decision: record whether this service was used for the LDU set-up.]

### 5.2 Selection criteria

Every item must be:

1. **Fit for purpose** and of suitable quality.
2. **Compatible with existing equipment** (e.g. handpiece connectors for the washer-disinfector, instrument cassettes for the sterilizer chamber).
3. **Easy to decontaminate or clean** – for reusable instruments, compatible with the practice's cleaning and sterilization methods (washer-disinfector; type N or type B sterilizer). Check the manufacturer's **reprocessing instructions** before purchase. Hollow, lumened or wrapped items need a type B sterilizer unless the manufacturer validates otherwise.
4. **Easy to maintain**, including smooth, cleanable hard surfaces on decontamination equipment, chairs and furniture.
5. **UKCA or CE marked** where it is a medical device. The mark signifies compliance with the UK Medicines and Medical Devices Act 2021 and the Medical Devices (Amendment) (GB) Regulations 2023. New devices placed on the GB market carry a UKCA mark; for items procured before Brexit the CE mark remains valid.
6. For hand hygiene products: meet the BS EN standards listed in the NIPCM Hand Hygiene Products literature review (see 25-hand-hygiene-policy.md).
7. For wipes, disinfectants and biocides: effective for the intended use, compatible with the surfaces and DUWL system, with a COSHH safety data sheet.
8. For sharps: **safer sharps** where reasonably practicable (see 29-occupational-exposure-and-sharps.md).
9. For PPE: meets the relevant standards and comes in a range of sizes (see 23-personal-protective-equipment-policy.md).

### 5.3 Single-use items

1. Items marked with the **single-use symbol** (a "2" with a line through it) are disposable and **must not be reprocessed** for use on the same or another patient. The symbol may appear only on the packaging, not on each item, so packaging is checked before items are decanted.
2. Examples: endodontic instruments (hand and rotary), matrix bands, plastic impression trays, prophy cups and brushes, face masks.
3. Re-use of a single-use device has **legal implications**: anyone who reprocesses or re-uses a device intended for single use bears full responsibility for its safety and effectiveness. This practice does not do so.
4. When single-use items are decanted from bulk packs, the storage container is labelled "single use".

### 5.4 When a new device arrives

1. Check the delivery: correct item, undamaged, UKCA/CE mark, instructions for use and reprocessing included.
2. **Review current decontamination procedures** for devices purchased for the first time. Add the item to the **Decontamination Methods for Instruments Used in this Practice** register.
3. New decontamination equipment is installed, commissioned and **validated** before use; Written Schemes of Examination are obtained for pressure vessels (see 26-decontamination-of-reusable-instruments.md).
4. Train staff on the new item before it is used.
5. File the instructions for use, warranty and service details.

### 5.5 Stock management

1. Stock is stored clean and dry, off the floor, in its packaging, and rotated (first in, first out).
2. Expiry dates (e.g. on wipes, disinfectants, sterile implant components) are checked [monthly].
3. Minimum stock levels are set for critical IPC items [practice decision] so the practice never runs out of gloves, hand rub, wipes or sterilizer test materials.

### 5.6 Safety alerts

The Practice Manager registers for medical device safety alerts [practice decision: e.g. MHRA / Incident Reporting and Investigation Centre (IRIC) Scotland – check current arrangements] and acts on any alert affecting items in use.
