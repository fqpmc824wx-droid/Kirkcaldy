## 1. Purpose

Dental radiography gives a very small radiation dose, but some risk always exists. The practice has a duty of care, and a legal obligation, to protect patients, staff and the public from harm from ionising radiation. This policy sets out how Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic meets the Ionising Radiations Regulations 2017 (IRR17) and the Ionising Radiation (Medical Exposure) Regulations 2017 (IR(ME)R), so that:

- every exposure is justified and optimised (doses as low as reasonably practicable for the intended purpose);
- everyone involved is appointed, entitled and trained for their role;
- equipment is registered, tested, maintained and quality assured;
- incidents are investigated and reported to the right authority on time;
- all evidence is kept in one Radiation Protection File.

This policy is a summary. The legal detail sits in the Local Rules, the Employer's Procedures and the Protocols in the Radiation Protection File. It does not replace proper radiography training or the advice of the practice's Radiation Protection Adviser (RPA). If in doubt, ask the RPA.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Before the first exposure (new practice set-up)

The Employer completes the following **before** any X-ray equipment is used:

1. **HSE registration.** Apply online to HSE to register work with a radiation generator, before work with ionising radiation begins. Registration reference: [number], date [date]. Keep the certificate in the Radiation Protection File.
2. Appoint the RPA and MPE in writing.
3. Appoint the RPS in writing and record the name in the Local Rules.
4. Ask the RPA to examine the plans for the X-ray rooms and equipment (including any CBCT/OPG room), including shielding of partition walls if needed.
5. Make sure all equipment carries a CE or UKCA mark and that the supplier/installer gives full information on proper use, testing and maintenance.
6. Arrange the installer's critical examination and acceptance (initial) testing of each unit by a qualified person. Compare the representative patient dose with the local DRL at acceptance.
7. Complete the radiation risk assessment with the RPA (5.3).
8. Write the Local Rules with the RPA (5.4).
9. Adopt and adapt the Employer's Procedures and write the exposure Protocols (5.5).
10. Entitle all duty holders and record their scope of practice (5.2).
11. Set up the QA programme (5.9) and the X-ray equipment inventory.
12. Brief all staff and get signatures for the Local Rules.

### 5.2 Entitlement of duty holders

1. Each duty holder is assessed against the registration, qualifications, training and experience needed for the tasks they will carry out. This defines their scope of practice.
2. Entitlement is recorded using the duty holder competency record in example Employer's Procedure **EP1** (Entitlement of Duty Holders), signed by the Employer.
3. Locums and new staff are entitled before they take, justify or evaluate any radiograph. Evidence of their training is checked first.
4. CBCT [if applicable]: only duty holders with the additional training to justify, perform or interpret CBCT examinations are entitled for CBCT. Referrers for CBCT do not need extra training but it is advised.
5. The register of entitled staff is reviewed annually and whenever staff change.

### 5.3 Radiation risk assessment and protection of staff and the public

1. A written radiation risk assessment is completed **before** work with X-rays starts, in consultation with the RPA, and discussed with staff. It includes estimates of dose to members of the public. The significant findings are recorded (a legal requirement for 5 or more staff, and practice policy whatever the staff number).
2. The risk assessment is reviewed **every 3 years**, and sooner if equipment, working methods or legislation change. Next review: [date].
3. **Controlled areas** are defined with the RPA and recorded in the Local Rules. Where possible, the whole room is the controlled area while the equipment is ready to emit X-rays; otherwise 1.5 m from the tube head and patient in all directions and within the primary beam until attenuated. For CBCT the whole room is the controlled area. Controlled areas are physically demarcated where practicable, with warning signs and lights as advised by the RPA.
4. Control panels, exposure switches and isolators are outside the controlled area where possible. The Operator can see the tube warning light and the patient during exposure.
5. During exposure only the patient (and a carer or comforter if needed) is in the controlled area. The Operator stands well outside it – at least 2 m from the tube head and patient – and out of the primary beam.
6. **Dose investigation level** is set at [value, e.g. 1 mSv per year as recommended for employees directly involved with radiography] and recorded in the Local Rules. If any staff member receives, or may have received, a dose above it, the RPA is consulted, a formal investigation is carried out and the result recorded in the file.
7. **Personal dosimetry**: the RPA advises whether it is needed [decision and justification]. If used, staff are trained in the care and use of dosemeters and reports are kept in the file.
8. **Pregnant staff**: a staff member who is pregnant tells the Employer in writing. The Employer, with the RPA, reviews her work so that the dose to the foetus is unlikely to exceed 1 mSv during the declared pregnancy.
9. **Contingency plans** for foreseeable accidents (e.g. failure of the exposure control circuitry, or of the rotational movement of the panoramic/CBCT unit) are in the Local Rules and rehearsed **annually**.

### 5.4 Local Rules

1. Local Rules are written with the RPA using the PSM *Local Rules* template, for each controlled area. They include at least: the RPS's name and contact details; identification and description of each controlled area and how access is restricted; a summary of working instructions; contingency arrangements for equipment malfunction or accidental exposure; and the dose investigation level.
2. They also include: RPA name and contact; the name of the Employer; personal dose monitoring arrangements; arrangements for pregnant staff; a reminder of employees' legal duties; and a note that the following are in the Radiation Protection File: maintenance and testing arrangements, risk assessment findings, training arrangements, incident arrangements, and Local Rules review arrangements.
3. Local Rules are document-controlled: practice name, date, version, authorisation, review date and page numbers.
4. A copy is displayed at or near each controlled area, and a copy is kept in the file.
5. All staff read and sign the Local Rules on starting, and re-read them **annually**.

### 5.6 Justification, authorisation and patient identification

1. Referral criteria are available to all Referrers.
2. Before any radiograph, the Referrer records a history and clinical examination. If the Referrer and Practitioner are different people, the Referrer gives enough information for the Practitioner to identify the patient and decide on justification.
3. Every exposure is **justified and authorised by an IR(ME)R Practitioner before it takes place**. The benefit to the patient must outweigh the risk, and the exposure would normally be expected to give new information to help manage the patient. The justification is recorded in Dentally.
4. If it is known beforehand that the image will not be clinically evaluated, the exposure is not justified and must not take place.
5. If the Referrer and Operator are the same person, the Operator makes sure the correct patient record is open in Dentally before exposure. If they are different, the patient is asked to confirm their name, address and date of birth before exposure (EP4).
6. Exposures with no direct clinical benefit (e.g. employment or insurance) are critically assessed; written consent is recommended (EP15).
7. All patients are given information on the benefits and risks of the exposure (EP17) – for example a short explanation by the clinician, supported by [leaflet / website page].

### 5.7 Pregnancy enquiries

1. In dental radiography the pelvic area is not usually exposed, so pregnancy is not a contraindication to dental radiographs.
2. Practice approach (EP5): [practice decision – e.g. "The practice does not make direct pregnancy enquiries before intra-oral or panoramic radiographs. For CBCT, the Operator asks female patients of child-bearing age whether they are or may be pregnant and records the answer; the Practitioner reconsiders justification if so."]
3. A poster in reception and each surgery asks patients to tell the dentist if they are pregnant before any dental treatment, so treatment can be planned (PSM example poster in Communication During Examination and Treatment).

### 5.8 Optimisation of dose and equipment use

1. Every exposure is kept as low as reasonably practicable for the diagnostic purpose.
2. Equipment meets the PSM specification:
   - intra-oral units operate above 50 kV (optimum 60–70 kV);
   - panoramic units operate at 60–90 kV and have positioning aids and light beams;
   - rectangular collimation not exceeding 40 × 50 mm; circular collimation not used;
   - exposure switch operable at least 2 m from and behind the tube head;
   - exposure only while continuous pressure is kept on the switch;
   - mains-on light on the control panel; visible and audible exposure indicators;
   - equipment installed after February 2018 shows the parameters needed to assess patient dose.
3. Operators always use rectangular collimation and beam-aiming devices, and a minimum focus-to-skin distance of 200 mm. (If film were used: fastest film speed available.)
4. Local DRLs are set with the MPE (EP7), taking account of national DRLs. At acceptance and each routine test the representative patient dose is compared with the local DRL. If doses are consistently above the DRL, radiographic practice is reviewed.
5. If a non-standard exposure is used, the exposure factors are recorded so the dose can be estimated later (EP6).
6. **Clinical evaluation** (EP8): every radiograph is clinically evaluated and the findings, and the image quality grade, are recorded in Dentally with enough detail for audit.
7. Lead aprons are not needed for dental patients. If any are kept, they are hung unfolded and inspected annually.
8. **Carers and comforters** (EP16): if an adult must hold or support a patient (e.g. a child), dose constraints and guidance apply. The carer must not be a member of staff. The carer is given information and the exposure is recorded.

### 5.9 CBCT and OPG for implant planning [if applicable]

1. CBCT and OPG are used only when justified for the clinical question (e.g. implant site assessment), following selection criteria and the Guidance Notes on CBCT.
2. The smallest field of view and lowest exposure that answers the clinical question are used. Protocols for each field of view and resolution are in the file.
3. Only duty holders with additional CBCT training justify, perform or interpret CBCT. The whole volume is reported by an appropriately trained person [practice decision – in-house or external radiologist reporting service: [provider]]; the report is stored in Dentally.
4. The whole CBCT room is a controlled area.
5. Rotational movement failure is covered in the contingency plan.
6. CBCT/OPG has its own QA tests (advised by the MPE) and its own image quality analysis, reported separately from intra-oral images.
7. If CBCT is not installed, patients are referred to [provider]; the Referrer gives enough information for the external Practitioner to justify the exposure, and the practice shares relevant information with that employer.

### 5.10 X-ray equipment: inventory, testing, maintenance and disposal

1. An inventory of all radiographic equipment – X-ray units, detectors, plate readers, monitors and software – with their specification is kept (PSM *X-ray Equipment Inventory* template) and updated whenever equipment changes.
2. All testing is by a qualified person. Equipment has initial testing by the installer, then routine testing. Routine test frequency: [as advised by RPA/MPE and manufacturer – record frequency for each unit]. Test provider: [company].
3. Formal written reports of each QA test detail the checks, results and recommendations. Recommendations are actioned and signed off. A log of tests and any routine or special maintenance is kept (*X-ray Machine Routine Testing and Maintenance Record*).
4. Frequent checks of safety features (warning lights, audible alarms) are carried out [weekly / monthly – frequency] and recorded (*X-ray Machine Routine Surveillance Record*).
5. Servicing follows the manufacturer's, supplier's and RPA's advice.
6. Faulty equipment is taken out of use, labelled "Do not use", and reported to the RPS.
7. **Disposal**: the electrical supply cable is cut close to the machine so it cannot be reconnected; radiation hazard markings are obscured; WEEE and special waste duties are considered (lead, beryllium, mineral oil); evidence of safe disposal is obtained from any contractor; safe use and testing information is passed on if equipment is sold or transferred; the inventory is updated.

### 5.11 Image processing and viewing (digital)

1. Written protocols cover use of digital sensors, phosphor plates and plate reader.
2. Sensors, phosphor plates and monitors have regular visual and radiographic checks, recorded on the *Digital Detector Check Record* and *Digital Monitor Check Record*. Frequency: [as advised by MPE]. Damaged plates or sensors are removed from use.
3. Images are stored in [imaging software], linked to the patient's Dentally record, and backed up as set out in 36-clinical-record-keeping-and-retention.md.
4. If film is ever used: light-tightness test at least every 12 months; film and chemical stock and storage checks; step-wedge monitoring; cleaning and chemical change records (PSM templates). [Delete if not used.]

### 5.12 Quality assurance programme

The QA programme (PSM *Example Radiology Quality Assurance and Audit Programme* template) is kept in the file and covers:

1. **Image quality.** Images are graded A (diagnostically acceptable) or N (diagnostically not acceptable) as they are viewed, and the grade recorded. At least every **6 months**, a sample of at least **100 images** is analysed (*Quality Assessment of Radiographic Images* template). Target: **not less than 95%** of digital images graded A (90% for film). Intra-oral, panoramic and CBCT results are reported separately. For every N image, record the date, nature of the fault, known or suspected cause and number of repeats. Rejected images are analysed at the 6-monthly review. Day-to-day surveillance picks up sudden deterioration.
2. **Patient dose and equipment** (EP18) – routine tests, DRL comparison, safety checks, as above.
3. **Image processing and viewing** – detector and monitor checks, as above.
4. **Training** (EP9) – the training register (section 7).
5. **Review of Employer's Procedures and Protocols** (EP12).
6. **Radiology audit** (EP13) – see 5.13.

### 5.13 Radiology audits

1. The overall QA systems are audited regularly – at least [annually] – to check the programme works and corrective action is taken. This may simply check logs and reject analyses, or be a deeper review of patient records (e.g. justification and clinical evaluation recorded), compliance with procedures and improvement.
2. Audits are recorded on the PSM *Record of Audit* template and kept in the file. Collaborating with other local practices is encouraged. Radiology audit can count towards QI activity (see 39-quality-improvement-and-audit.md).

### 5.14 Incidents and accidents

1. Anyone who suspects that a patient, staff member or member of the public has received an accidental or unintended dose tells the RPS and the Employer **immediately**. The RPA or MPE is consulted immediately.
2. The Employer carries out an **immediate preliminary investigation**. If it cannot be shown beyond reasonable doubt that the exposure does not meet the reporting criteria, it is reported.
3. **Patients** – notify **Healthcare Improvement Scotland (HIS)** if the dose is "significant" as defined in *Significant Accidental and Unintended Exposures under IR(ME)R*, **no later than 2 weeks** after discovering the incident.
4. **Staff or public** – notify **HSE** if the dose exceeds the dose limit.
5. For a notifiable incident, carry out a detailed investigation with the RPA/MPE to establish what happened and how, estimate the dose, identify the failure and decide preventive actions. Submit the detailed report **no later than 12 weeks** after the incident to HIS (patients) or HSE (staff/public). Make sure the preventive actions are carried out.
6. **Equipment failure** – also inform **Health Facilities Scotland (HFS)**. (A purely private practice would report to the MHRA; as an NHS practice we report to HFS.)
7. Near misses are also investigated and recorded (EP10), and every incident goes through the practice incident system and SEA (see 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md). Consider Duty of Candour (see 02-duty-of-candour-procedure.md).
8. **Retention** of investigation reports (in the Radiation Protection File or patient notes):
   - significant incidents involving patients: at least 5 years; clinically significant incidents: at least 30 years (Guidance Notes);
   - incidents involving staff or public: immediate investigation reports at least 2 years; detailed investigation reports at least 30 years (IRR17).

## 7. Training

1. All relevant staff are trained in: the health risks of X-rays; the precautions to take; the duty to tell the Employer in writing if pregnant; the importance of complying with legal requirements; and use of the equipment within their entitlement.
2. Staff are told of their IRR17 duties (section 4.1).
3. IR(ME)R Practitioners and Operators have theoretical knowledge and practical experience, covering Schedule 3 topics relevant to their role, **before** carrying out any exposure or practical aspect. Additional training is needed to justify, perform or interpret CBCT.
4. CPD: the GDC recommends at least 5 hours of radiography and radiation protection CPD per 5-year cycle. Courses should cover, as relevant: radiation physics; risks; doses in dental radiography and factors affecting them; principles of radiation protection; statutory requirements; selection criteria; and QA.
5. Non-clinical staff, carers and comforters are given enough information for their safety.
6. The RPS has evidence of RPS training.
7. A **training register** (PSM *Radiography Training Record* or EP9 example records) lists every person involved in any aspect of radiography with: name; responsibility (scope of practice); date and nature of training; recommended date for review. It covers both IRR17 and IR(ME)R training and supports each person's entitlement (e.g. evidence of training in clinical evaluation). It is available for inspection.
