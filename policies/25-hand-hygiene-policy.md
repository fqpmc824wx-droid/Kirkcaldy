## 1. Purpose

Hands are the most common way that microorganisms are spread. Good hand hygiene is one of the most important ways to prevent infection in the practice. This policy sets out when and how staff clean their hands, the products and facilities the practice provides, and how compliance is checked.

Hand hygiene is one of the Standard Infection Control Precautions (SICPs) in the NHS NSS National Infection Prevention and Control Manual (NIPCM).

## 2. Scope

All staff, including dentists, DCPs, dental nurses, trainees, reception and admin staff, cleaners and locums, in all areas of the practice and on any domiciliary visit. It covers hand washing, hand rub, glove use in relation to hand hygiene, and skin care.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Facilities

1. Every surgery and the LDU has a dedicated **clinical hand wash basin**, separate from instrument sinks. Instruments are never washed in the hand wash basin, and hands are never washed in instrument sinks.
2. Each hand wash basin has: [practice decision: elbow, wrist or sensor operated] taps; wall-mounted liquid soap; wall-mounted disposable paper towels; a foot-operated waste bin; and the NIPCM "How to hand wash" poster.
3. Alcohol-based (or non-alcohol based) hand rub dispensers are placed at the point of care in each surgery, in the LDU, at reception and at the patient entrance.
4. Bar soap, cloth towels and refillable "topped-up" dispensers are not used.
5. Emollient hand cream is provided in wall-mounted or pump dispensers (not communal tubs) to avoid cross-contamination.

### 5.2 Products

1. Hand wash and hand rub products must meet the obligatory and additional **BS EN standards** listed in the NIPCM Hand Hygiene Products literature review.
2. Hand rub may be alcohol-based or non-alcohol based, provided it meets those standards.
3. If a product does not meet the required standards, the IPC Lead must complete a **risk assessment** to ensure safe systems of work before it is used.
4. Products in use at the practice: [hand wash product], [hand rub product], [emollient]. The IPC Lead keeps the manufacturer's evidence of BS EN compliance in the IPC folder.
5. Products are stored according to the manufacturer's instructions; hand rub is stored away from heat and ignition sources. COSHH data sheets are held in the COSHH file.

### 5.3 When to clean hands

Staff follow the NIPCM **5 moments for hand hygiene**:

1. Before touching a patient.
2. Before a clean or aseptic procedure (e.g. before putting on sterile gloves for implant surgery).
3. After exposure to body fluids (blood, saliva).
4. After touching a patient.
5. After touching the patient's surroundings (e.g. the dental chair, bracket table, light handle).

In addition, staff clean their hands:

- before putting on gloves and **after removing them** – gloves are not a substitute for hand hygiene because microorganisms can multiply under gloves;
- before and after decontamination duties in the LDU;
- after removing PPE;
- after using the toilet, before eating, and at the start and end of each shift;
- whenever hands are visibly dirty.

### 5.4 Choosing hand wash or hand rub

| Situation | Method |
|---|---|
| Hands visibly dirty or contaminated with blood or body fluids | Hand wash with liquid soap and warm running water |
| After using the toilet | Hand wash |
| Caring for a patient with known or suspected diarrhoea/vomiting infection | Hand wash (alcohol rub is not effective against some organisms – see NIPCM) |
| Hands visibly clean, between patients and during care | Hand rub (or hand wash) |
| Before surgical procedures (implants, surgical extractions) | Surgical hand antisepsis following the NIPCM and the product manufacturer's instructions [practice decision: product and technique] |

### 5.5 Preparing for hand hygiene

In clinical areas and the LDU, all staff:

1. Are **bare below the elbows**: short sleeves or sleeves rolled up; no wrist watches, bracelets or wrist-worn devices.
2. Wear no rings, except a single plain band [practice decision: confirm against current NIPCM].
3. Keep nails short and clean, with no nail varnish, false nails, nail extensions or gels.
4. Cover any cuts or breaks in the skin with a waterproof dressing.

### 5.6 How to hand wash

Follow the NIPCM "How to hand wash" poster (40–60 seconds):

1. Wet hands with warm running water.
2. Apply enough liquid soap to cover all hand surfaces.
3. Rub palm to palm; right palm over the back of the left hand with fingers interlaced, and vice versa; palm to palm with fingers interlaced; backs of fingers to opposing palms with fingers interlocked; rotational rubbing of each thumb; rotational rubbing of fingertips in each palm.
4. Rinse hands under running water.
5. Dry thoroughly with a single-use paper towel.
6. Turn off the tap using the elbow/wrist/sensor, or with the paper towel if the tap is hand-operated.
7. Put the paper towel in the correct waste stream (5.8).

### 5.7 How to hand rub

Follow the NIPCM "How to hand rub" poster (20–30 seconds):

1. Apply a palmful of hand rub to cupped hands.
2. Rub using the same six steps as hand washing, covering all surfaces.
3. Continue until hands are dry. Do not wipe off.

### 5.8 Drying and waste

1. Hands are dried with soft, good-quality disposable paper towels.
2. Paper towels are disposed of in the **correct waste stream**: domestic/non-hazardous waste unless contaminated with blood or body fluids, in which case healthcare (orange stream) waste. [Practice decision: confirm the stream for each bin with the waste contractor – see 22-waste-management-policy.md.]

### 5.9 Skin care

1. Frequent hand washing and glove use can cause work-related contact dermatitis, which is RIDDOR reportable if confirmed by a doctor.
2. Staff use emollient hand cream regularly, especially at the end of a session and after work.
3. Hand rub is generally kinder to skin than repeated hand washing when hands are not visibly dirty.
4. Hands are dried fully before putting on gloves.
5. Staff report redness, cracking or soreness to the IPC Lead at once. The IPC Lead seeks advice from the staff member's GP or the NHS Fife Occupational Health Service and, where needed, changes products (see 21-latex-allergy-policy.md).
6. [Practice decision: the IPC Lead carries out a quick skin check with each clinical member of staff every [3/6] months.]

### 5.10 Patients and visitors

1. Hand rub is available at the entrance and reception, with a sign asking patients and visitors to use it.
2. Patients who need help (e.g. children, people with a disability) are assisted.

- All staff are trained in effective hand hygiene technique at induction, before working in clinical areas.
- Staff are encouraged to complete the SIPCEP Hand Hygiene module on TURAS.
- Technique is refreshed [annually] and observed during audits.
- Training covers skin care and the use of emollients.
