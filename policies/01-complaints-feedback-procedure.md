## 1. Purpose

Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic welcomes feedback, comments, concerns and complaints. We see them as a chance to improve. This document sets out how we receive, handle, record and learn from them.

It has two parts in one document:

- **Part A – Staff procedure**: how the team handles feedback and complaints.
- **Part B – Patients' version**: a plain summary for patients. Part B is printed separately, given to anyone who wishes to complain, displayed in the waiting area and published on kirkcaldydental.co.uk.

The practice follows the NHS Scotland Model Complaints Handling Procedure (NHSS CHP), which has two internal stages (Early Resolution and Investigation) and an external stage (independent external review by the Scottish Public Services Ombudsman, SPSO).

## 2. Scope

This procedure applies to:

- all staff, including dentists, dental care professionals, reception and management staff, locums and trainees;
- feedback, comments, concerns and complaints from NHS and private patients, carers, relatives and anyone acting on a patient's behalf (with the patient's consent);
- complaints made in person, in writing, by telephone, by email or online, including anonymous complaints.

The NHS complaints route (Health Board and SPSO) applies to NHS care. Complaints about private treatment (including implants) are handled by the practice in the same way, but the external routes are different (see 5.9).

Concerns raised by staff about patient safety or wrongdoing are handled under `07-raising-concerns-whistleblowing.md`, not this procedure.

# Part A – Staff procedure

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 Know what you are dealing with

The PSM uses these definitions:

- **Feedback** – views given in a survey or patient experience questionnaire, via the Patient Advice and Support Service (PASS) or online portals. It may describe good practice or things that could be better.
- **Comments** – compliments or observations about how a patient felt about the service.
- **Concerns** – usually about proposed treatment or appointment timing. The patient wants to be kept informed rather than expressing dissatisfaction.
- **Complaints** – an expression of dissatisfaction about an action, a lack of action, or the standard of care.

Use your judgement. If unsure, treat it as a complaint and speak to the Complaints Lead. The NHS Scotland Model CHP contains a decision matrix; keep a copy at reception. If a person is not satisfied with the response to feedback, a comment or a concern, consider whether it should now be logged as a complaint.

### 5.2 First contact (any member of staff)

1. Listen. Be courteous and sympathetic. Express empathy and acknowledge any distress, and apologise for it.
2. Move the conversation somewhere private if possible.
3. Find out what the person wants to happen as a result. Be honest if this cannot be met and explain why.
4. Check whether the patient has an urgent oral health need. If so, make sure it is met (book an appointment or refer to emergency care).
5. If the person is complaining on behalf of a patient, check whether the patient has consented. Use the consent form in Appendix 9 of the NHS Scotland CHP.
6. Try to resolve the complaint there and then. If you cannot, ask whether the person wants it handled under the practice complaints procedure, and pass it to the Complaints Lead the same day.
7. Ask for the person's preferred method of communication (letter, email, phone).
8. Offer a copy of the patients' version of this procedure (Part B) and information on support: PASS, NHS Inform, interpreting and advocacy services.
9. Write down what was said, who said it and when. Give this to the Complaints Lead.

Points to note:

- First consideration must always be given to whether the complaint can be resolved at Early Resolution.
- If the person insists they do not want to make a formal complaint, record it as resolved at Early Resolution.
- Offer to apply the complaints procedure even if the person has said in writing that they intend to take legal proceedings.
- Follow the procedure as far as possible for anonymous complaints, so that the practice can learn and the records are complete.
- The complaints procedure does not provide for compensation. Direct the person to PASS for advice about compensation.
- A person who does not want to complain to the practice directly can approach NHS Fife (for NHS care). The Health Board can act as an "honest broker" but does not investigate.
- For complaints about an individual's fitness to practise, the person may wish to approach the relevant professional body (the GDC).

### 5.3 Timescales – how to count days

Day 1 is the day the complaint is received, or the next working day if it is received on a non-working day. Only working days count. [Practice decision: working days are Monday to Friday excluding the practice's public holidays.]

### 5.4 Stage 1 – Early Resolution (5 working days)

For straightforward complaints that need little or no investigation. Any member of staff may deal with a complaint at this stage.

1. Aim to resolve within **5 working days**, preferably on the spot.
2. In exceptional circumstances, and only if the complaint is still likely to be resolved at this stage, extend by a further **5 working days**, with the agreement of the person complaining. Extensions should not become the norm. Record the reason.
3. The response should include: an apology where appropriate, an explanation, and an indication of any improvement identified.
4. A straightforward complaint resolved to the person's satisfaction within 5 working days, with the outcome given face-to-face, by phone or email, does not need further written confirmation. Record the outcome in the complaints log.
5. **Escalate to Stage 2** if the person is not satisfied and asks for an investigation, or if satisfactory early resolution is not possible because the person insists on an investigation.

### 5.5 Stage 2 – Investigation (acknowledge in 3, respond in 20 working days)

Go straight to Investigation where the complaint is serious, complex or high risk and clearly cannot be resolved without investigation, or where the person is unhappy with the Stage 1 response or insists on an investigation.

**Acknowledgement within 3 working days**, in writing, using the person's preferred method of communication. Use the practice acknowledgement letter [template to be prepared from the PSM list]. It must include:

- thanks to the person for raising the complaint;
- empathy and acknowledgement of any distress;
- contact details of the Complaints Lead (Feedback and Complaints Officer);
- a copy of the patients' complaints procedure (Part B);
- confirmation of the method of communication, or a request for their preferred method;
- a statement that the complaint will normally be investigated within 20 working days or as soon as reasonably practicable (if not possible, why, and a revised timetable where possible);
- a summary of our understanding of the complaint and the outcome the person wants;
- an outline of how the complaint will be investigated, stressing rigour and impartiality;
- details of advice and support, including PASS;
- information on the role and contact details of the SPSO;
- information on mediation (alternative dispute resolution) and other support such as advocacy;
- an offer to meet to discuss the issues;
- a request for evidence of consent if someone is complaining on behalf of a patient.

**Investigation**

1. The Complaints Lead [or Practice Owner for complaints about clinical care – practice decision] investigates. Gather the clinical record from Dentally, appointment history, statements from staff involved and any other evidence.
2. Keep the person informed during the investigation.
3. Any meeting should take place within the 20-day limit. Record the meeting and give the person a copy of the record.
4. Consider what support staff need if they are the subject of the complaint.
5. Contact the practice's dental defence organisation [Name of defence organisation] at any point if in doubt about how to proceed.
6. Consider whether the complaint reveals a duty of candour incident or a significant event.

**Response within 20 working days.** Day 20 is the latest day for a decision to be issued, unless an extension has been agreed with the person for clear and justifiable reasons. Respond in writing, and also by their preferred method if different. The response must:

- be clear, easy to understand, person-centred and non-confrontational;
- apologise if things have gone wrong;
- reply to every point raised, explaining any technical terms;
- for each issue, explain what has been or will be done to prevent it happening again, showing that action has been taken;
- highlight any area of disagreement and explain why no further action can be taken;
- explain what the person can do next if they are not satisfied (see 5.8 and 5.9);
- offer to talk through anything they do not understand.

The response is signed by [Practice Owner / Principal Dentist [Nas] – practice decision].

### 5.6 Complaints about the use of personal data

Handle these through this procedure as well, following the NHS Model as the PSM advises in the interim. Involve the Data Protection Lead. Tell the person they may also contact the Information Commissioner's Office (ICO). Check current guidance on the Data (Usage and Access) Act 2025 complaints requirement.

### 5.7 Unreasonable or abusive behaviour

If a complainant becomes violent or abusive, follow `04-zero-tolerance-violent-patients.md`. A complaint itself is never a reason to deregister a patient.

### 5.8 Unresolved NHS complaints

- Either the patient or the practice may ask **NHS Fife** to assist. NHS Fife's Feedback and Complaints team (Patient Experience) NHS Fife Patient Experience Team: fife.patientexperience@nhs.scot, 01592 648153 can act as "honest broker" to help communication but has no responsibility to investigate. Mediation may be considered if both parties agree. Where agreement cannot be reached, NHS Fife decides how the complaint should be managed.
- If the person is unhappy with the outcome or with how the complaint was handled, they may ask the **Scottish Public Services Ombudsman (SPSO)** to investigate. The SPSO normally only considers a complaint once it has been through the practice procedure. It must be made in writing with copies of all previous correspondence, normally within **1 year** of the event.
- The SPSO cannot look at complaints that go to court or tribunal, or at private treatment complaints.

### 5.9 Unresolved private complaints (including implants)

- Private treatment complaints may be taken to the **GDC Dental Complaints Service** (dcs.gdc-uk.org) or **Healthcare Improvement Scotland**, which regulates private clinics.

## 6. Records and monitoring

### 6.1 Recording each complaint

Record every complaint, including those resolved at Early Resolution and anonymous ones, **within 5 working days**, using the PSM Record of Complaint template. Keep it up to date at each stage. Record:

- name, address and email (if preferred) of the person complaining;
- patient's name and details, including CHI number where relevant;
- confirmation of consent if someone acts on the patient's behalf;
- date received and the staff member who received it;
- how it was received (in writing, verbally, etc.);
- subject matter and the date it occurred;
- date closed at Early Resolution, or date escalated to Investigation;
- action taken at Investigation and date closed;
- outcome at each stage;
- underlying cause and any remedial action.

Keep all correspondence with the complaints record, **not** in the patient's dental record. [Practice decision: complaints file location – e.g. a restricted folder outside Dentally, access limited to the Complaints Lead, Practice Manager and Practice Owner.] Retention: [practice decision – check current guidance].

Also record feedback, comments and concerns in the feedback log [location].

### 6.2 Reporting to NHS Fife

- **Quarterly**: complaints information to NHS Fife's Feedback and Complaints Manager.
- **Annually**: feedback, comments and concerns information.
- The Complaints Lead keeps NHS Fife's Feedback and Complaints Team contact details at reception and in this file: NHS Fife Patient Experience Team: fife.patientexperience@nhs.scot, 01592 648153.

### 6.3 Learning

- Review all complaints and feedback (including anonymous ones) at practice staff meetings [practice decision: at least quarterly] to identify improvements and recognise good performance.
- Consider a suggestion box and a patient experience questionnaire (PSM example available).
- Make sure every response shows the action taken.
- The Complaints Lead carries out an annual review of the log for trends and reports to the Practice Owner.

**How to give feedback or make a complaint – Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic**

We want to hear from you. Your feedback, comments, concerns and complaints help us improve.

**How to contact us.** You can tell any member of staff in person, phone us on 01592 214225, email info@kirkcaldydental.co.uk, or write to the Feedback and Complaints Officer, [Name], Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic, 23–25 Tolbooth Street, Kirkcaldy KY1 1RW. Someone else can complain for you if you give your consent. You can also complain anonymously, although we may not be able to reply to you.

**Stage 1 – Early resolution.** We will try to sort out your complaint straight away, or within 5 working days. Sometimes we may ask you to agree to a further 5 working days.

**Stage 2 – Investigation.** If your complaint is complex, or you are not happy with our Stage 1 answer, we will investigate. We will acknowledge your complaint within 3 working days and aim to give you a full written response within 20 working days. If we need longer, we will tell you why and agree a new date with you. We are happy to meet you to discuss your complaint.

**Help and support.** The Patient Advice and Support Service (PASS) gives free, confidential advice and can help you make a complaint. NHS Inform has information about making a complaint about NHS care. Ask us if you need an interpreter, large print or other help.

**If you are still not happy – NHS treatment.** You can ask NHS Fife's Feedback and Complaints team to help NHS Fife Patient Experience Team: fife.patientexperience@nhs.scot, 01592 648153. If you remain unhappy after our final response, you can ask the Scottish Public Services Ombudsman (SPSO) to look at your complaint, normally within 12 months of the event. Contact the SPSO at [check current contact details].

**If you are still not happy – private treatment.** You can contact the GDC Dental Complaints Service (dcs.gdc-uk.org) or Healthcare Improvement Scotland.

Making a complaint will not affect your care with us.
