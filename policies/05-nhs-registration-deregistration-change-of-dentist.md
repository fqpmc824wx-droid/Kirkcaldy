## 1. Purpose

This policy sets out how Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic:

- accepts and registers new NHS and private patients;
- arranges appointments and handles cancellations and missed appointments;
- deals with emergency patients;
- agrees recall intervals and runs the recall system;
- tells patients about a change of dentist;
- terminates an NHS capitation arrangement (deregistration), with the correct notice.

It aims to make sure patients are well informed and that the practice meets the Terms of Service for Dentists.

## 2. Scope

- All reception, management and clinical staff.
- All NHS patients registered with a dentist at the practice (capitation and continuing care arrangements with NHS Fife), and private patients where stated.

## 5. Policy / procedure

### 5.1 Accepting new patients

1. Practice policy on accepting new patients: [practice decision – e.g. accepting NHS adults and children; private patients; any waiting list].
2. Make the public aware when the practice is accepting new patients (website, leaflet, notice) and make sure all staff know the procedure.
3. Deregistration and refusal to register must never be based on race, gender, social class, age, religion, sexual orientation, appearance, disability or medical condition.

### 5.2 Registering a patient

1. Reception registers the patient in Dentally and records personal details: full name, date of birth, address, contact details, parent/carer/next of kin contact (for children or vulnerable patients), GP name, CHI number and any special requirements.
2. The patient completes a medical history and social and dental history before the first examination. Ask whether the patient is anxious.
3. Complete NHS registration with NHS Fife for NHS patients [practice decision/check current process: NHS registration form and claim through the NHS dental claims system].
4. Explain whether care will be NHS, private or a mix, and the charges.
5. Give a **New Patient Pack**: welcome letter (PSM New Patients Letter template) and the Patient Information Leaflet, including information on charges.
6. Explain the importance of regular care and that the patient must tell the practice of changes in address, medical history or medication.
7. Ask about additional requirements for attending (access, interpreter, hearing loop, large print) and record them.

### 5.3 Arranging appointments

Before each appointment, tell the patient:

- who the appointment is with;
- how long it is likely to last;
- what to bring (e.g. medications list, proof of exemption from NHS charges);
- costs and how to pay;
- the practice policy on cancelling and the charges for missed appointments;
- how the appointment will be confirmed (e.g. Dentally text or email reminders).

Practice decisions to fill in:

- Cancellation notice required: [e.g. 24 hours / 48 hours for long or implant appointments].
- Missed appointment charge: [amount and how applied; NHS and private].
- Deposits for private or implant treatment: [policy].

Respect privacy at reception. Discuss medical information, payment and exemption status discreetly. If a patient is kept waiting beyond their appointment time, tell them why, apologise where needed and offer alternatives.

### 5.5 Agreeing the recall interval

1. The dentist agrees the recall interval with the patient **on the basis of oral health need** (NICE recall guidance) and records it in Dentally.
2. The interval is recorded in the patient's personal care plan; the patient receives a copy.
3. Recall procedures encourage attendance: book the next appointment in advance where possible, and send reminders by [Dentally text / email / phone – practice decision].
4. Remind patients to keep the practice informed of changes to address or circumstances, including medication.
5. **Audit** the recall system [practice decision: annually] as a quality improvement activity, and make changes so it works efficiently.

[Practice decision: e.g. after [two] missed appointments without notice, the Practice Manager writes to the patient; repeated failure may lead to termination of the capitation arrangement with 3 months' written notice under 5.8.] Consider safeguarding concerns where a child or vulnerable adult repeatedly misses appointments, and discuss with the Safeguarding Lead.

### 5.7 Change of dentist

The PSM asks practices to have a policy on how to communicate a change of dentist to patients (PSM template: Patient Notification of a Change of Dentist Policy).

1. When a dentist leaves, reduces hours, or a patient's care moves to another dentist, the Practice Manager arranges for affected patients to be told [practice decision: in writing, at least [x] weeks before the change where possible].
2. The notice gives the name of the new dentist, the date of change, whether NHS registration continues with the practice, and who to contact with questions.
3. Update the NHS registration with NHS Fife where the named dentist changes [check current process with NHS Fife].
4. Make sure any treatment in progress is handed over with a clear note in Dentally.
5. Update the patient information leaflet and staff display (names and GDC numbers).

### 5.8 Deregistration of NHS patients (termination of capitation arrangement)

**Standard termination – 3 months' notice**

1. Only the Practice Owner may authorise termination. Record the reason in the patient's file.
2. A capitation arrangement cannot be terminated solely on grounds relating to race, gender, social class, age, religion, sexual orientation, appearance, disability or medical condition.
3. Give the patient **3 months' notice in writing** of the termination.
4. On giving notice, try to **complete any outstanding care** to manage the patient's oral health.
5. **Notify NHS Fife**, giving details of outstanding treatment and arrangements made to try to complete it.
6. Exception: where termination is due to **non-payment of charges**, the practice is not required to complete outstanding treatment.

**Termination in less than 3 months**

1. Ask NHS Fife to terminate the arrangement, giving the reasons and details of outstanding treatment or arrangements made to complete it.
2. Complete **Form GP200** and send it to NHS Fife.

**Immediate termination – violence**

1. De-register with immediate effect any patient who has committed an act of violence or made a dentist or staff fearful for their safety.
2. Report the incident to the police and keep copies of all communication.
3. Notify NHS Fife that the practice wishes to terminate immediately due to violent conduct.
4. Notify the patient by phone or email, and send **written confirmation within 7 days**. Email does not count as written confirmation.
5. NHS Fife should then take reasonable steps to notify the patient.
6. See `04-zero-tolerance-violent-patients.md`.

Check NHS National Services Scotland *Withdraw a patient* guidance for the current claims-system process.
