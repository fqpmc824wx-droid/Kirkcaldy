## 1. Purpose

NHS dentists are Scottish public authorities under the Freedom of Information (Scotland) Act 2002 (FOISA). This policy explains how Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic adopts and maintains a publication scheme and responds to requests for information within the legal time limits.

## 2. Scope

- Information the practice holds in relation to its **NHS work**, in any recorded form: paper, computer files including emails, photos and videos, microfiche – however old.
- All staff who may receive a request, by letter, email, social media message or any other recordable format.
- Requests for environmental information under the Environmental Information (Scotland) Regulations 2004 (EIRs).

FOISA covers **non-personal** information. Requests by people for their **own** personal information are subject access requests under the Data Protection Act 2018 – see 10-data-protection-confidentiality-information-security.md. It will not generally be appropriate to release an individual's dental records in response to a FOISA request.

Private (non-NHS) treatment information is outside the publication scheme unless the practice chooses to publish it. [Check current guidance if unsure whether information relates to NHS work.]

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 Adopting the Model Publication Scheme

1. The practice adopts the Scottish Information Commissioner's current approved **Model Publication Scheme (MPS)**. The practice does not need to tell the Commissioner it has adopted it.
2. To adopt it, the practice must:
   - make the MPS available **exactly as provided**
   - produce a **Guide to Information** describing the information published in each class and how to get it (adapt the SDCEP Model Publication Scheme Guide to Information template; include a review date)
   - make all the information described available
   - keep the scheme up to date.
3. Publish the MPS and Guide to Information on kirkcaldydental.co.uk through a "Freedom of Information" link in the footer, and keep a printed copy at reception.
4. The way information is made available must meet the MPS principles (for example say whether there is a charge and how to get information in other formats).

### 5.3 Recognising a valid FOISA request

A FOISA request must:

- be in writing (letter, email, social media message we can keep) or another recordable format (for example audio)
- give the requester's name and an address for reply (postal or email) [check current Scottish Information Commissioner guidance]
- describe the information wanted.

The requester does not have to mention FOISA or say why they want it. Anyone, anywhere in the world, can ask.

**Environmental information** (EIRs) can be requested **verbally** as well as in writing. If a verbal request is about environmental information (for example waste, emissions, energy use), write it down, read it back to the requester and log it. Refer to the Scottish Information Commissioner's guidance on what counts as environmental information.

### 5.4 Responding – 20 working days

1. **Log** the request on the day it arrives (FOI log: date received, requester, information requested, due date, decision, date sent).
2. **Due date**: within **20 working days** of receipt. This is required by law.
3. **Check validity.** If the request is unclear, ask for clarification promptly. The 20 working days then run from receipt of the clarification.
4. **Check the publication scheme.** If the information is already published, direct the requester to where it is.
5. **Find the information** and check whether any exemption applies (5.5).
6. **Send** the information in a form that helps the requester understand it, and be ready to explain it. Never include personal information about patients or staff.
7. If necessary, consult the indemnity/dental defence organisation [name] before releasing information.
8. Tell the requester in the response that they can ask for a review (5.6).

Examples of information the public may ask NHS dentists or Health Boards about include Dental Reference Officer information, complaint types, grant applications, NES CPD information, planning permission, decisions affecting local services and how waiting list priority is decided.

### 5.5 Refusing a request (exemptions)

1. Some information is completely excluded – for example where release would breach the Data Protection Act 2018 (such as patient records), or documents lodged with a court.
2. Other information may be exempt only if release would cause **substantial prejudice** (real and significant harm), for example serious harm to commercial interests.
3. Most exemptions also need a **public interest test**: withhold only if the public interest in not disclosing outweighs the public interest in release.
4. A request can be refused if the cost of complying exceeds the FOISA upper cost limit of **£600**. (There is no upper fee limit for environmental information, but an EIR request can be refused if "manifestly unreasonable".)
5. If we refuse, we **must** write to the requester giving:
   - the reasons, including which exemption applies and why
   - why the public interest favours non-disclosure (if the exemption is subject to it)
   - their right to ask for a review **within 40 working days**.
   It is a legal requirement to give reasons.
6. For advice on exemptions contact the Scottish Information Commissioner enquiry service.

### 5.6 Reviews and appeals

1. A requester who is unhappy with our response (or who received none) can ask for a review, in writing, within **40 working days** of receiving our response.
2. Where possible, the review is done by someone **not involved** in the original decision.
3. The reviewer reconsiders the request and responds within **20 working days** (required by law), either releasing some or all of the information or upholding the original decision with reasons.
4. The response tells the requester of their further right of appeal to the **Scottish Information Commissioner** [current address and website – check].
5. If the Commissioner investigates and finds we did not comply, they can order release of the information. Co-operate fully with any investigation.

### 5.7 Template wording

**Acknowledgement**: "Thank you for your request for information, received on [date]. We will reply by [date – 20 working days]."

**Refusal / partial refusal**: "We are unable to provide [information] because exemption [section] of the Freedom of Information (Scotland) Act 2002 applies, because [reason]. [Public interest reasoning.] If you are unhappy with this response, you may ask us to review it. Please write to [FOI officer, address/email] within 40 working days of receiving this letter. If you remain unhappy after the review, you can appeal to the Scottish Information Commissioner."
