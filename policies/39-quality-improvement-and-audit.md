## 1. Purpose

To give patients a high-quality service, the practice must keep assessing its performance and improving clinical outcomes and patient care. There is always room for improvement, even in procedures where we are highly skilled. We often learn more from our mistakes than from what we do well.

This policy sets out how Kirkcaldy Dental & Implant Clinic:

- meets the NHS Scotland Terms of Service requirement for every dentist to complete Quality Improvement (QI) activity;
- plans and runs QI activity and audit as a team;
- uses audit and QI as the quality assurance part of its risk management system, to check that staff do what the policies say;
- records, shares and acts on the results.

## 2. Scope

This policy applies to:

- every dentist on the NHS Fife dental list working at the practice (Terms of Service requirement);
- all other team members, who take part in team QI projects and practice audits;
- both NHS and private care, including implant treatment.

## 3. Legislation and guidance

As cited in the PSM:

- The National Health Service (General Dental Services) (Scotland) Regulations 2010 – Terms of Service for Dentists.
- The National Health Service (General Dental Services) (Scotland) Amendment Regulations 2017 – replaced the clinical audit requirement with a broader QI requirement, and defines QI as *"the systematic, critical and reflective analysis of the quality of dental care provided by the dentist, and of any changes made by the dentist to bring about improvement in quality of care, patient experience, patient safety and clinical effectiveness, (including the processes used by that dentist for diagnosis, intervention and treatment and use of resources)"*.
- General Dental Council – CPD criteria (QI activity can count as verifiable CPD if it meets these).
- NHS Education for Scotland (NES) – *Quality Improvement – Dental* and *eSEA, Quality Improvement Activity* resources (TURAS), including how to plan, carry out, report and claim QI allowance.

## 4. Responsibilities

**Practice Owner / Principal Dentist [Nas]** – leads QI in the practice; agrees the annual QI and audit plan; makes sure each dentist is on track for their 15 hours per cycle; makes sure changes from QI are put into practice.

**Each dentist** – completes at least 15 hours of QI activity in each 3-year cycle, keeps evidence, and submits activities to NES for approval where QI allowance is to be claimed.

**Practice Manager [Name]** – keeps the QI and audit plan and log, schedules audits, keeps reports, and puts results on the team meeting agenda.

**Policy leads** – run the audits required in their area (see 5.4).

**All team members** – take part in team QI projects, collect data accurately and help put changes in place.

## 5. Policy and procedure

### 5.1 NHS requirement for dentists

1. Each dentist completes a **minimum of 15 hours of QI activity in each 3-year cycle** (current cycle: August 2025 – July 2028).
2. The 15 hours may include **up to 5 hours of QI research activity**.
3. QI activity can also count towards verifiable GDC CPD if it meets the GDC's CPD criteria.
4. QI activity allowance is available through NES for general dental practitioners whose QI activity is approved by NES or an NHS Board.
5. **Process for an allowance claim** (follow current NES guidance):
   1. Plan the activity and apply to NES for approval before starting.
   2. Once approved, complete the activity and produce a report **within 6 months** (except enhanced SEA, where the event has already taken place).
   3. Submit the report to NES for review and certification.
   4. Once certified by NES, claim the QI allowance from Practitioner Services Division (PSD).
6. Dentists who join part way through a cycle agree a QI plan with the Practice Owner within [3 months] of starting.

### 5.2 Choosing a topic

1. Choose an issue that matters to the practice, its patients or the individual's needs – for example an area where an incident, complaint, audit result or patient feedback showed a problem.
2. Team projects are preferred where possible: a team assessing an activity relevant to all of them (for example decontamination) is more likely to lead to lasting improvement than an individual project (for example one person's radiograph quality).
3. Suggested early topics for a new practice: [practice decision – e.g. medical history completion rates; radiograph image quality; decontamination process timings; implant post-operative complications and follow-up; patient wait times; "was not brought" follow-up for children; antimicrobial prescribing].

### 5.3 QI methods

The practice may use any of the methods listed in the PSM:

| Method | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **Clinical audit** | Measure practice against an agreed standard, change, then re-measure (audit cycle). |
| **Enhanced Significant Event Analysis (eSEA)** | Structured team analysis of a significant event, positive or negative (see 37-risk-management-and-significant-events.md). |
| **Model for Improvement / PDSA cycles** | Test small changes quickly: Plan, Do, Study, Act, and repeat. |
| **Care bundles** | A small set of evidence-based steps that are all done every time (e.g. an implant surgery checklist), with compliance measured. |
| **Peer review** | Discussion of cases or practice with colleagues, e.g. other local practices. |
| **Practice-based research** | Up to 5 of the 15 hours. |

### 5.4 Audit cycle (standard method)

1. **Choose the topic** and say why it matters.
2. **Set the standard** – from guidance (SDCEP, PSM, FGDP(UK), NICE) or agreed by the team, with a target (e.g. "100% of adult records have a medical history signed within the last 12 months").
3. **Collect data** – define the sample (e.g. 20 consecutive records per clinician), period and data collection form. Remove patient-identifiable details from the report.
4. **Compare results with the standard.**
5. **Agree and make changes** – with an owner and date for each.
6. **Re-audit** after [3–6 months] to see if the change worked.
7. **Write up and share** at a team meeting; keep the report.

### 5.6 QI as quality assurance

The risk management system must include quality assurance to check that everyone follows the policies. The audit programme above is the main way the practice does this. Where an audit shows a policy is not followed, the Practice Owner decides whether to change the process, retrain staff, or change the policy, and records the decision.

## 6. Records and monitoring

1. The Practice Manager keeps a **QI and audit log**: title, method, lead, team members, start date, completion date, hours (per dentist), NES approval reference (if any), outcome, re-audit date.
2. Each dentist keeps their own QI evidence (NES certificates, reports, hours) and CPD record.
3. Audit reports and action plans are kept in [location] for [practice decision – at least the current and previous QI cycle].
4. Results are presented at a team meeting and recorded in the minutes.
5. The Practice Owner reviews the QI and audit plan **every 12 months** (see the annual review calendar in the policies README).

## 7. Training

1. All dentists and the Practice Manager complete the NES QI learning resources on TURAS [within 6 months of the practice opening].
2. Team members are shown how to collect audit data accurately and confidentially.
3. QI results and learning are shared at team meetings so the whole team learns from them.
